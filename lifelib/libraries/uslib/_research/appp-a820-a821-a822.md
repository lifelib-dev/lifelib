## Scope and applicability

### What business A-820 governs

Per **A-820 ¶5** (life) and **¶6** (annuities), the appendix sets the **minimum standard of valuation** for:

- all **life insurance and endowment** policies and contracts (¶5);
- **individual annuity and pure endowment contracts**, and **annuities and pure endowments purchased under group
  annuity and pure endowment contracts** (¶6);
- **disability and accidental death benefits** in or supplementary to policies (¶5.d, ¶5.e, ¶13.c);
- **group life, substandard life and other special benefits**, for which ¶5.f prescribes only
  "tables which provide for an adequate reserve";
- **accident and health insurance contracts**, by cross-reference only (¶22);
- and, from ¶23 onward, everything issued on or after the Valuation Manual operative date, by delegation to the
  Valuation Manual.

**A definitional point that changes scope.** ¶1.e: *"The term 'life insurance' means contracts that incorporate
mortality risk, including annuity and pure endowment contracts, and as may be specified in the Valuation Manual."*
So in A-820's PBR half, **annuities are inside "life insurance"**. But ¶1's own preamble limits when these definitions
bite — see the next subsection — and the pre-operative-date half (¶¶5–22) plainly treats life (¶5, ¶¶11–13) and
annuities (¶6, ¶¶14–15) as separate tracks. Do not carry ¶1.e's definition backwards into ¶¶5–22.

### Mortality-table effective dates (A-820 ¶5), verbatim triggers

- **¶5.a** — *"For ordinary policies of life insurance issued on the standard basis on or after January 1,
  2004, excluding preneed policies (which follow Appendix A-817) any disability and accidental death benefits in the
  policies"* → the **Commissioners 2001 Standard Ordinary Mortality Table**; at company election for one or more
  **specified plans**, the **2001 CSO with 25-Year Select Mortality Factors**; or *"Any ordinary mortality table
  adopted subsequently by the NAIC for use in determining the minimum standard for valuation for such policies"*.
- **¶5.b** — *"For ordinary policies of life insurance issued on the standard basis, prior to January 1,
  2004, excluding any disability and accidental death benefits in the policies and including preneed policies issued
  on or after January 1, 2012 (see A-817)"* → the **Commissioners 1980 Standard Ordinary Mortality Table**; at company
  election for specified plans, the **1980 CSO with Ten-Year Select Mortality Factors**.
- **¶5.c** — industrial life, standard basis, excluding disability and AD benefits → **Commissioners 1961 Standard
  Industrial Mortality Table** or any industrial table **adopted after 1980** by the NAIC.
- **¶5.d** — total and permanent disability benefits in or supplementary to ordinary policies → the tables of
  **Period 2 disablement rates and the 1930 to 1950 termination rates of the 1952 Disability Study of the Society of
  Actuaries**, "with due regard to the type of benefit", or any tables adopted after 1980 by the NAIC; **for active
  lives combined with a mortality table permitted for life insurance reserves**.
- **¶5.e** — accidental death benefits → the **1959 Accidental Death Benefits Table** or any adopted after 1980;
  **combined with a mortality table** for life insurance reserves.
- **¶5.f** — group life, substandard, other special benefits → *"tables which provide for an adequate reserve."*

**Note what ¶5 does *not* name.** The **2017 CSO** is nowhere in A-820's printed text. It can only enter through
¶5.a.iii's forward reference to tables "adopted subsequently by the NAIC" — and in practice reaches post-2017 issues
through the Valuation Manual under ¶23 instead.

### Annuity mortality effective dates (A-821), verbatim

- **¶10** — *"Except as provided in paragraph 12 of this Appendix, the Annuity 2000 Mortality Table shall be
  used for determining the minimum standard of valuation for any individual annuity or pure endowment
  contract issued on or after January 1, 2001, through December 31, 2014."*
- **¶11** — *"Except as provided in paragraph 12, the 2012 IAR Mortality Table shall be used for determining
  the minimum standard of valuation for any individual annuity or pure endowment contract issued on or
  after January 1, 2015."*
- **¶12** — the **1983 Table "a" without projection** is used *"solely when the contract is based on life
  contingencies and is issued to fund periodic benefits arising from"* (a) settlements of claims from **court or

  (c) *"Settlements of long term disability claims where a temporary or life annuity has been used in lieu of
  continuing disability payments."* — i.e. the **structured settlement** carve-out.
- **¶15** — *"The 1994 GAR Table shall be used for determining the minimum standard of valuation for any
  annuity or pure endowment purchased under a group annuity or pure endowment contract."* **No effective date is
  printed for ¶15.**

### Transition and grandfathering language — the whole of what is printed

A-820 contains **exactly two** transition provisions, both quoted above: **¶3** (PBR provisions apply to issues on or
after 1/1/2017) and **¶4** (pre-1/1/2017 issues stay on ¶¶5–22, and *"the provisions set forth in paragraphs 23-27 of
this Appendix shall not apply to any such policies and contracts"*). There is **no elective-transition window, no
phase-in, no "mandatory from" date and no company election** anywhere in A-820's text. The only place A-820 anticipates
transition machinery is by delegation — **¶24.e** requires the Valuation Manual to specify, among other things,
*"transition rules and internal controls"*.

## Mechanics

### 1. Definitions that carry weight (A-820 ¶1)

Eleven terms, applying **on or after the Valuation Manual operative date**. The ones an implementer must hold:

| Term | Printed definition (paraphrased except where quoted) |
|---|---|
| **accident and health insurance** (¶1.a) | contracts incorporating **morbidity risk** protecting against economic loss from accident, sickness or medical conditions, "as may be specified in the Valuation Manual" |
| **appointed actuary** (¶1.b) | a qualified actuary appointed in accordance with the Valuation Manual to prepare the actuarial opinion |
| **company** (¶1.c) | an entity that has written, issued or reinsured life, A&H or deposit-type contracts in a U.S. State, district or territory **and has at least one such policy in force or on claim**; or that has done so and is required to hold a certificate of authority |
| **deposit-type contract** (¶1.d) | contracts that **do not incorporate mortality or morbidity risks**, as may be specified in the Valuation Manual |
| **life insurance** (¶1.e) | contracts that **incorporate mortality risk, including annuity and pure endowment contracts** |
| **policyholder behavior** (¶1.g) | "any action a policyholder, contract holder or any other person with the right to elect options … may take under a policy or contract … including, but not limited to, lapse, withdrawal, transfer, deposit, premium payment, loan, annuitization, or benefit elections prescribed by the policy or contract **but excluding events of mortality or morbidity** that result in benefits prescribed in their essential aspects by the terms of the policy or contract" |
| **principle-based valuation** (¶1.h) | "a reserve valuation that uses one or more methods or one or more assumptions determined by the insurer and is required to comply with paragraphs 25-27 of this Appendix as specified in the Valuation Manual" |
| **qualified actuary** (¶1.i) | qualified to sign the applicable actuarial opinion under **American Academy of Actuaries** qualification standards and meeting the Valuation Manual's requirements |
| **tail risk** (¶1.j) | "a risk that occurs either where the frequency of low probability events is higher than expected under a normal probability distribution or where there are observed events of very significant size or magnitude" |
| **Valuation Manual** (¶1.k) | the manual of valuation instructions adopted by the NAIC as specified in this Appendix or as subsequently amended |

**The ¶1.g exclusion is the operative one for a model:** mortality and morbidity are **not** policyholder behaviour, so
the VM-31/VM-G assumption-governance obligations that attach to policyholder behaviour do not attach to the mortality
basis by this route. **¶1.d/¶1.e together are the SSAP No. 51 vs. No. 52 boundary in valuation clothing** — presence of
mortality or morbidity risk, decided on the contract's terms.

### 2. The four general valuation requirements (A-820 ¶2)

Reserves reported in the financial statements shall:

- **a.** "Be computed in accordance with presently accepted actuarial standards";
- **b.** be based on assumptions producing reserves **at least as great as those called for in any contract provision
  as to reserve basis and method**, and in accordance with all other contract provisions; and, considered in light of
  the assets held (including "the investment earnings on the assets and the considerations anticipated to be received
  and retained under the policies and contracts"), **"make adequate provision for the company's obligations under the
  policies and contracts, including but not limited to the benefits under and expenses associated with the policies
  and contracts"**;
- **c.** be computed on assumptions **consistent with the preceding year-end**, "with any exceptions disclosed in the
  notes to the financial statements";
- **d.** "Include provision for all actuarial reserves and related statement items which ought to be established."

¶2.b is the textual root of asset adequacy analysis inside A-820 itself; A-822 then makes the additional reserve
mandatory. ¶2.c is the disclosure hook behind change-in-valuation-basis reporting.

### 3. CRVM — the commissioners reserve valuation method (A-820 ¶¶11–13)

**¶11, the core construction, applies to "policies providing for a uniform amount of insurance and requiring the
payment of uniform premiums".** As printed:

> reserves according to the commissioners reserve valuation method, for the life insurance and endowment
> benefits of policies providing for a uniform amount of insurance and requiring the payment of uniform
> premiums shall be **the excess, if any**, of the present value, at the date of valuation, of the future
> guaranteed benefits provided for by those policies, over the then present value of any future modified net
> premiums therefore.

and the defining condition on the modified net premiums:

> The modified net premiums for a policy shall be **the uniform percentage of the respective contract premiums**
> for the benefits such that the present value, at the date of issue of the policy, of all modified net premiums
> shall be equal to the sum of the then present value of the benefits provided for by the policy and **the excess
> of a. over b.**

with

> **a.** A net level annual premium equal to the present value, at the date of issue, of the benefits provided for
> **after the first policy year**, divided by the present value, at the date of issue, of an annuity of one per annum
> payable on the first and each subsequent anniversary of the policy on which a premium falls due. However, the net
> level annual premium **shall not exceed the net level annual premium on the nineteen-year premium whole life plan
> for insurance of the same amount at an age one year higher than the age at issue of the policy.**
>
> **b.** A net one-year term premium for the benefits provided for in the first policy year.

**Symbols, as an implementer needs them.** Contract issued at age *x*, guaranteed benefit stream *B*, **contract**
gross premiums *G<sub>k</sub>* at durations *k* = 0, 1, 2, …, valuation basis (mortality per ¶5, interest per ¶¶7–10):

```
ä_0  = APV at issue of an annuity of 1 per annum payable on the first and each subsequent
       anniversary on which a premium falls due                                       [¶11.a denominator]
α    = min(  APV_0( benefits provided for AFTER the first policy year ) / ä_0 ,
             P19(x+1)  )                                                              [¶11.a, with the cap]
P19(x+1) = net level annual premium on the NINETEEN-year premium whole life plan, same amount,
       at age x+1  (note: age x+1, not x)                                             [¶11.a proviso]
β    = net one-year term premium for the benefits provided for in the first policy year   [¶11.b]
E    = α − β                                                          (the "excess of a. over b.")
k*   : APV_0( k*·G ) = APV_0(B) + E      ⇒    k* = ( APV_0(B) + E ) / APV_0(G)
V_CRVM(s) = max( 0 , APV_s(B) − k*·APV_s(G) )                    ("the excess, if any")
```

Two structural checks fall straight out of the printed text: the cap is at **age x+1**, and `APV_0(B) − k*·APV_0(G) = −E`,
so the pre-floor reserve at issue equals the negative of the expense allowance.

**¶12 — the excess-first-year-premium rule.** Trigger, as printed: a life insurance policy "for which the **contract
premium** in the first policy year exceeds that of the second year and for which no comparable additional benefit is
provided in the first year for the excess and which provides an endowment benefit or a cash surrender value or a
combination **in an amount greater than the excess premium**". The **assumed ending date** is defined in-line as
"the first policy anniversary on which the sum of any endowment benefit and any cash surrender value then available is
greater than the excess premium". At any anniversary **on or before** the assumed ending date, and "except as otherwise
provided in paragraphs 19 and 20", the reserve is the **greater** of the ¶11 reserve and a second ¶11-style reserve
computed with:

1. "the value defined in that paragraph being **reduced by fifteen percent (15%) of the amount of such excess first
   year premium**";
2. all present values of benefits and premiums determined **without reference to premiums or benefits after the assumed
   ending date**;
3. the policy **assumed to mature on that date as an endowment**;
4. the **cash surrender value provided on that date considered as an endowment benefit**.

"In making the above comparison the mortality stated in paragraph 5 and interest bases stated in paragraphs 7-10 shall
be used."

**Ambiguity to record, not to resolve.** ¶12 says the greater of the reserve "calculated as described in **the preceding
paragraph**" and the reserve "calculated as described in **those paragraphs**, but with (i) the value defined in **that
paragraph** being reduced by fifteen percent (15%)". The singular/plural mismatch is a renumbering artefact: the model
law reads "Subsection A" and "Subsection A(1)". Under the model law the 15% reduction attaches specifically to **item
a.** — the net level annual premium *α*. **A-820 as printed does not disambiguate it to ¶11.a.** Anyone implementing
from A-820 alone should note the reading is supplied by the model law, not by this text.

**¶13 — extension by consistency.** "Reserves according to the commissioners reserve valuation method shall be
calculated by a method **consistent with the principles of paragraphs 11 and 12**" for:

- **a.** life insurance policies providing for a **varying amount of insurance** or requiring **varying premiums**;
- **b.** **group annuity and pure endowment contracts purchased under a retirement plan or plan of deferred
  compensation** established or maintained by an employer (including a partnership or sole proprietorship) or by an
  employee organization or both — **other than** a plan providing IRAs or individual retirement annuities under
  **IRC §408**;
- **c.** disability and accidental death benefits in **all** policies and contracts;
- **d.** all other benefits, except life insurance and endowment benefits in life insurance policies and benefits
  provided by all other annuity and pure endowment contracts.

**¶13.b is a scope fact worth surfacing:** qualified-plan group annuity and pure endowment business is valued on a
**CRVM-consistent** method, **not** CARVM — ¶14 excludes exactly that population from ¶15. ¶13.a is the hook that makes
CRVM reach **universal life and varying-benefit designs** at all.

### 4. CARVM — the commissioners annuity reserve method (A-820 ¶¶14–15)

**¶14, the scope gate, in full:**

> Paragraph 15 shall apply to all annuity and pure endowment contracts **other than** group annuity
> and pure endowment contracts purchased under a retirement plan or plan of deferred compensation,
> established or maintained by an employer (including a partnership or sole proprietorship) or by an
> employee organization, or by both, other than a plan providing individual retirement accounts or
> individual retirement annuities under Section 408 of the Internal Revenue Code, as now or hereafter
> amended.

**¶15, the construction, in full:**

> Reserves according to the commissioners annuity reserve method for benefits under annuity or
> pure endowment contracts, excluding any disability and accidental death benefits in the contracts, shall
> be **the greatest of the respective excesses of the present values, at the date of valuation, of the future
> guaranteed benefits, including guaranteed nonforfeiture benefits, provided for by the contracts at the
> end of each respective contract year, over the present value, at the date of valuation, of any future
> valuation considerations derived from future gross considerations, required by the terms of the
> contract, that become payable prior to the end of the respective contract year.** The future guaranteed
> benefits shall be determined by using the mortality table, if any, and the interest rate, or rates, specified
> in the contracts for determining guaranteed benefits. The valuation considerations are the portions of
> the respective gross considerations applied under the terms of the contracts to determine nonforfeiture
> values.

**Symbols.** For each contract-year end *k* = 1 … *H*:

```
X(k) = APV_τ( guaranteed benefits, INCLUDING guaranteed nonforfeiture benefits,
              provided at the end of contract year k )
     − APV_τ( future valuation considerations derived from future gross considerations, required by the
              terms of the contract, that become payable BEFORE the end of contract year k )
V_CARVM(τ) = max over k of X(k)
```

Two prescriptions inside ¶15 that a model must honour and that are easy to lose:

- the **future guaranteed benefits** are projected on **the mortality table (if any) and interest rate(s) specified in
  the contract for determining guaranteed benefits** — i.e. the *contractual* guarantee basis, not the valuation basis.
  The valuation basis (¶¶7–10 interest, A-821 mortality per ¶6) enters through the **discounting**;
- **"valuation considerations"** are not gross considerations — they are "the portions of the respective gross
  considerations applied under the terms of the contracts to determine nonforfeiture values".

The implementation obligation is **enumeration of every guaranteed-benefit path** to the end of every contract year;
omitting a path can only understate the reserve. A-820 gives no path list — that is what AG 33 and AG 35 supply, and
neither is in the extracted material.

### 5. Minimum standard for annuities — the mortality leg (A-820 ¶6)

> The minimum standard of valuation for individual annuity and pure endowment contracts and
> for annuities and pure endowments purchased under group annuity and pure endowment contracts,
> shall be the commissioners annuity reserve valuation methods defined in paragraphs 14 and 15,
> valuation interest rates provided in paragraphs 7-10, and **the tables defined in Appendix A-821**.

So the CARVM triple is: **method = ¶¶14–15, interest = ¶¶7–10, mortality = A-821**. A-821 is not optional colour; it is
the mortality leg by direct cross-reference.

### 6. Valuation interest rate (A-820 ¶¶7–10)

**¶7.a.i — the dynamic formula, "and the results rounded to the nearer one-quarter of one percent (1/4 of 1%)".**
As printed (the fraction bar under the second *W* is lost in the text layer — the "2" sits on the following line):

```
(a) For life insurance:

        I = .03 + W(R1 - .03) + (W/2)(R2 - .09)

(b) For single premium immediate annuities and for annuity benefits involving life contingencies
    arising from other annuities with cash settlement options and from guaranteed interest
    contracts with cash settlement options:

        I = .03 + W(R - .03)

    Where  R1 is the lesser of R and .09,
           R2 is the greater of R and .09,
           R  is the reference interest rate defined in paragraph 9,
       and W  is the weighting factor defined in paragraph 8;
```

**Routing rules ¶7.a.i(c)–(e), which formula applies to what:**

| Contract class | Basis | Formula used |
|---|---|---|
| Life insurance | — | (a) |
| Single premium immediate annuities; annuity benefits involving life contingencies arising from other annuities with cash settlement options and from GICs with cash settlement options | — | (b), with **W = .80** |
| Other annuities with cash settlement options and GICs with cash settlement options, **guarantee duration in excess of ten (10) years** | **issue year** | (a) — the life insurance formula |
| Other annuities with cash settlement options and GICs with cash settlement options, **guarantee duration of ten (10) years or less** | **issue year** | (b) |
| Other annuities with **no** cash settlement options and GICs with **no** cash settlement options | (issue year — forced, per ¶8.c.vi) | (b) |
| Other annuities with cash settlement options and GICs with cash settlement options | **change in fund** | (b) |

**¶7.a.ii — the stability rule, verbatim:**

> However, if the calendar year statutory valuation interest rate for a life
> insurance policy issued in any calendar year determined without reference to
> this sentence differs from the corresponding actual rate for similar policies
> issued in the immediately preceding calendar year by **less than one-half of one
> percent (1/2 of 1%)**, the calendar year statutory valuation interest rate for the
> life insurance policies shall be equal to the corresponding actual rate for the
> immediately preceding calendar year.

Note this is a **life-only** rule and it references the *actual* prior-year rate, so the recursion is on the published
series, not on a freshly recomputed one.

**¶8.a — weighting factors for life insurance, as printed:**

| Guarantee Duration (Years) | Weighting Factor |
|---|---|
| 10 or less | **.50** |
| More than 10, but not more than 20 | **.45** |
| More than 20 | **.35** |

with the definition, verbatim: *"For life insurance, the guarantee duration is the maximum number of years the life
insurance can remain in force on a basis guaranteed in the policy or under options to convert to plans of life
insurance with premium rates or nonforfeiture values or both which are guaranteed in the original policy"*.

**¶8.b — weighting factor for single premium immediate annuities** and for annuity benefits involving life
contingencies arising from other annuities with cash settlement options and GICs with cash settlement options:
**.80**.

**¶8.c.i — weighting factors for other annuities and GICs, issue year basis, by Plan Type:**

| Guarantee Duration (Years) | Plan Type A | Plan Type B | Plan Type C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 or less | .80 | .60 | .50 |
| More than 5, but not more than 10 | .75 | .60 | .50 |
| More than 10, but not more than 20 | .65 | .50 | .45 |
| More than 20 | .45 | .35 | .35 |

**¶8.c.ii — change in fund basis:** the ¶8.c.i factors **increased by** **A +.15, B +.25, C +.05**.

**¶8.c.iii — the no-forward-guarantee increment:** for annuities and GICs valued on an **issue year basis** (other than
those with no cash settlement options) **that do not guarantee interest on considerations received more than one year
after issue or purchase**, and for annuities and GICs valued on a **change in fund basis** that do not guarantee
interest rates on considerations received **more than twelve (12) months beyond the valuation date**, the factors from
i. or derived in ii. are increased by **+.05 for all three plan types (A .05, B .05, C .05)**.

**¶8.c.iv — guarantee duration for annuities/GICs**, verbatim in substance: for those **with** cash settlement options,
"the number of years for which the contract guarantees interest rates **in excess of the calendar year statutory
valuation interest rate for life insurance policies with guarantee duration in excess of twenty (20) years**"; for those
with **no** cash settlement options, "the number of years from the date of issue or date of purchase to the date annuity
benefits are scheduled to commence."

**¶8.c.v — Plan Type definitions, as printed:**

- **Plan Type A:** "At any time policyholder may withdraw funds only (l) with an adjustment to reflect changes in
  interest rates or asset values since receipt of the funds by the insurance company, or (2) without an adjustment but
  installments over five years or more, or (3) as an immediate life annuity, or (4) no withdrawal permitted."
- **Plan Type B:** "Before expiration of the interest rate guarantee, policyholder may withdraw funds only (1) with an
  adjustment …, or (2) without an adjustment but in installments over five years or more, or (3) no withdrawal
  permitted. **At the end of interest rate guarantee, funds may be withdrawn without an adjustment in a single sum or
  installments over less than five years.**"
- **Plan Type C:** "Policyholder may withdraw funds before expiration of interest rate guarantee in a single sum or
  installments over less than five years either (1) without adjustment …, or (2) **subject only to a fixed surrender
  charge stipulated in the contract as a percentage of the fund.**"

**¶8.c.vi — the issue year / change in fund election:** a company **may elect** either basis for GICs with cash
settlement options and annuities with cash settlement options; GICs and other annuities **with no cash settlement
options must be valued on an issue year basis**. Definitions, verbatim in substance: an **issue year basis** uses "the
calendar year valuation interest rate for the year of issue or year of purchase" for the **entire duration**; a
**change in fund basis** applies "the calendar year valuation interest rate for the year of the change in the fund"
to **each change in the fund**.

**¶9 — the reference interest rate *R*.** The underlying series in every case is *"the monthly average of the composite
yield on seasoned corporate bonds, as published by **Moody's Investors Service, Inc.**"*:

| Business (¶9 sub-paragraph) | Averaging | Period ends June 30 of |
|---|---|---|
| **a.** Life insurance | **the lesser of** the 36-month average and the 12-month average | the calendar year **preceding** the year of issue |
| **b.** SPIAs; annuity benefits involving life contingencies arising from other annuities/GICs with cash settlement options | 12-month average | the calendar year **of** issue or purchase |
| **c.** Other annuities/GICs with cash settlement options, **year of issue** basis, **guarantee duration in excess of ten (10) years** | **the lesser of** the 36-month and 12-month averages | the calendar year **of** issue or purchase |
| **d.** Same, **guarantee duration of ten (10) years or less** | 12-month average | the calendar year **of** issue or purchase |
| **e.** Other annuities/GICs with **no** cash settlement options | 12-month average | the calendar year **of** issue or purchase |
| **f.** Other annuities/GICs with cash settlement options, **change in fund** basis | 12-month average | the calendar year **of the change in the fund** |

**¶10 — alternative method, verbatim:**

> In the event that the monthly average of the composite yield on seasoned corporate bonds is no longer
> published by Moody's Investors Service, Inc. or in the event that the NAIC determines that [it] … is no
> longer appropriate for the determination of the reference interest rate, then an alternative method for
> determination of the reference interest rate adopted by the NAIC may be substituted.

**Implementation consequence.** ¶10 means the reference series must be a **configurable table keyed by calendar year**,
never a hard-coded Moody's feed. ¶8.c.vi means the interest basis is a **per-contract election recorded at issue**, and
the change-in-fund basis makes the valuation rate a function of **the year each increment of fund arose** — a per-layer
attribute, not a per-contract scalar.

### 7. Minimum reserves, optional higher standards (A-820 ¶¶16–18)

**¶16 — the aggregate nonforfeiture-basis floor, in full:**

> In no event shall a company's aggregate reserves for all life insurance policies, excluding
> disability and accidental death benefits be less than the aggregate reserves calculated in accordance
> with the methods set forth in paragraphs 11-15, paragraphs 19-21, and **the mortality table or tables and
> rate or rates of interest used in calculating nonforfeiture benefits for the policies.**

This is an **aggregate** test across all life insurance policies, not seriatim, and it re-runs the *same methods* on the
*nonforfeiture* mortality and interest basis.

**¶17 — optional higher standard, with its constraint:**

> Reserves for any category of policies, contracts or benefits, may be calculated, at the option of
> the company, according to any standards that produce greater aggregate reserves for the category than
> those calculated according to the minimum standard provided herein, **but the rate or rates of interest
> used for policies and contracts, other than annuity and pure endowment contracts, shall not be greater
> than the corresponding rate or rates of interest used in calculating any nonforfeiture benefits provided
> in the policies or contracts.**

**¶18 — reverting to a lower standard, with the appointed-actuary carve-out:**

> A company which adopts at any time a standard of valuation producing greater aggregate
> reserves than those calculated according to the minimum standard provided herein may, adopt a lower
> standard of valuation, but not lower than the minimum provided herein; **provided that the holding of
> additional reserves previously determined by the appointed actuary shall not be deemed to be the
> adoption of a higher standard of valuation.**

The ¶18 proviso, paired with **A-822 ¶4**, is the rule that keeps asset adequacy additional reserves **outside** the
change-in-valuation-basis machinery in both directions.

### 8. Deficiency reserves — the formulaic construction (A-820 ¶¶19–20)

This is the piece the library previously had only by reference. **¶19, in full:**

> If in any contract year the gross premium charged by a company on a policy or contract is less
> than the valuation net premium for the policy or contract calculated by the method used in calculating
> the reserve but using the minimum valuation standards of mortality and rate of interest, the minimum
> reserve required for the policy or contract shall be **the greater of either the reserve calculated according
> to the mortality table, rate of interest, and method actually used for the policy or contract, or the
> reserve calculated by the method actually used for the policy or contract but using the minimum
> valuation standards of mortality and rate of interest and replacing the valuation net premium by the
> actual gross premium in each contract year for which the valuation net premium exceeds the actual
> gross premium.** The minimum valuation standards of mortality and rate of interest to be used are those
> standards stated in paragraph 5 and paragraphs 7-10 of this Appendix.

As a procedure:

```
TRIGGER   in any contract year:  G(k) < π_min(k)
          where π_min(k) = valuation net premium computed by the METHOD ACTUALLY USED for the
          policy, but on the MINIMUM valuation standards of mortality (¶5) and interest (¶¶7-10)

V_actual  = reserve on the mortality table, rate of interest, and method ACTUALLY USED
V_def     = reserve by the METHOD ACTUALLY USED, on the MINIMUM standards of mortality and
            interest, with the valuation net premium REPLACED BY the actual gross premium G(k)
            in each contract year k for which π_min(k) > G(k)

MinimumReserve = max( V_actual , V_def )
```

Note precisely what is substituted: the **actual gross premium**, only **in the deficient years**, inside a reserve that
is otherwise on the **minimum** mortality and interest standards with the **method actually used**. There is no separate
"deficiency reserve" quantity in ¶19 — the deficiency is expressed as a **floor on the policy reserve**.

**¶20 — interaction with the excess-first-year-premium designs, in full:**

> For a life insurance policy for which the gross premium in the first policy year exceeds that of
> the second year and for which no comparable additional benefit is provided in the first year for the
> excess and which provides an endowment benefit or a cash surrender value or a combination in an
> amount greater than the excess premium, the provisions of paragraphs 19 and 20 shall be applied as if
> the method actually used in calculating the reserve for the policy were the method described in
> paragraph 11. The minimum reserve at each policy anniversary of such a policy shall be **the greater of
> the minimum reserve calculated in accordance with paragraphs 11 and 12 and the minimum reserve
> calculated in accordance with paragraphs 19 and 20.**

So for this design: substitute **CRVM (¶11)** as "the method actually used" inside the ¶19 test, then take
`max( min-reserve per ¶¶11–12 , min-reserve per ¶¶19–20 )`. **¶20 refers to itself** ("the provisions of paragraphs 19
and 20") — a renumbering artefact from the model law's "Subsection A and this Subsection B"; read it as "¶19 and this
¶20".

### 9. Indeterminate premium plans (A-820 ¶21)

For a plan of life insurance providing for **future premium determination** by the company based on then-estimates of
future experience, **or** a plan of life insurance or annuity "that is of such a nature that the minimum reserves cannot
be determined by the methods described above", the reserves held shall:

- **a.** "Be appropriate in relation to the benefits and the pattern of premiums for that plan"; and
- **b.** "Be computed by a method that is consistent with the principles of this Appendix."

This is the catch-all that keeps a formulaic reserve computable for designs ¶¶11–15 do not describe.

### 10. What the Valuation Manual must specify (A-820 ¶24) — the PBR/formulaic boundary

This paragraph is the textual authority behind the library's statement that whole life and ordinary UL run the old CRVM
calculation inside a PBR-era manual.

**¶24.a — minimum valuation standards the Valuation Manual must specify, verbatim:**

> i. **The commissioners reserve valuation method for life insurance contracts, or
> other than annuity contracts**, subject to paragraph 3;
> ii. **The commissioners annuity reserve valuation method for annuity contracts**
> subject to paragraph 3; and
> iii. Minimum reserves for all other policies or contracts subject to paragraph 3.

**¶24.b** — which policies are subject to a principle-based valuation under ¶25, and minimum valuation standards
consistent with those requirements.

**¶24.c** — for PBR business: report format requirements under ¶26.c; *"Assumptions shall be presecribed for risks over
which the company does not have significant control or influence"* (the misspelling is as printed/extracted); and
procedures for **corporate governance and oversight of the actuarial function**, with a process for waiver or
modification.

**¶24.d — the decisive one for non-PBR business, verbatim:**

> For policies not subject to a principle-based valuation under paragraph 25-27, the
> minimum valuation standard shall either:
> i. **Be consistent with the minimum standard of valuation prior to the operative
> date of the Valuation Manual**; or
> ii. Develop reserves that quantify the benefits and guarantees, and the funding,
> associated with the contracts and their risks at a level of conservatism that
> reflects conditions that include unfavorable events that have a reasonable
> probability of occurring.

**¶24.e** — other requirements: reserve methods, models for measuring risk, generation of economic scenarios,
assumptions, margins, use of company experience, risk measurement, disclosure, certifications, reports, actuarial
opinions and memorandums, **transition rules** and internal controls.

**¶24.f** — the data and form of data required under ¶28, to whom submitted, and possibly further analyses.

**And ¶27, one sentence, in full:**

> A principle-based valuation may include a **prescribed formulaic reserve component.**

**¶24.a.i–ii + ¶24.d.i + ¶27 together are the chain:** the Valuation Manual must still specify CRVM for life and CARVM
for annuities; for non-PBR business the standard may simply be "consistent with the minimum standard of valuation prior
to the operative date"; and a principle-based valuation is expressly permitted to contain a prescribed formulaic
component. That is A-820's own account of why a VM-20 net premium reserve routes back to formulaic requirements, and
why a CRVM engine is still required equipment in 2026.

### 11. Requirements of a principle-based valuation (A-820 ¶¶25–26, 28)

**¶25 — the four conditions a company must meet:**

- **a.** "Quantify the benefits and guarantees, and the funding, associated with the contracts and their risks at a
  level of conservatism that reflects conditions that include unfavorable events that have a reasonable probability of
  occurring **during the lifetime of the contracts**. For polices or contracts with significant **tail risk**, reflects
  conditions appropriately adverse to quantify the tail risk." (typo "polices" as printed/extracted).
- **b.** Incorporate assumptions, risk analysis methods and financial models and management techniques "**consistent
  with, but not necessarily identical to**, those utilized within the company's overall risk assessment process", while
  recognizing potential differences in financial reporting structures and any prescribed assumptions or methods.
- **c.** Assumptions derived in one of two ways: **prescribed in the Valuation Manual**; or, where not prescribed,
  "established utilizing the company's available experience, **to the extent it is relevant and statistically
  credible**", or, where company data is not available/relevant/credible, "other relevant, statistically credible
  experience".
- **d.** "Provide margins for uncertainty including adverse deviation and estimation error, **such that the greater the
  uncertainty, the larger the margin and resulting reserve**."

**¶26 — obligations of a company using PBR:** (a) establish **corporate governance and oversight** procedures
consistent with the Valuation Manual; (b) provide the commissioner **and the board of directors** an **annual
certification of the effectiveness of internal controls** with respect to the PBR valuation, designed to assure all
material risks inherent in the liabilities **and associated assets** are included and that valuations follow the
Valuation Manual, "based on the controls in place as of the end of the preceding calendar year"; (c) develop, and
**file with the commissioner upon request**, a PBR valuation report complying with the Valuation Manual.

**¶28 — experience reporting:** "A company shall submit mortality, morbidity, policyholder behavior, or expense
experience and other data as prescribed in the Valuation Manual."

### 12. A-821 — annuity mortality: purpose, formulas, worked example, tables

**Purpose (¶1).** To recognize four tables for the minimum standard of valuation for annuity and pure endowment
contracts: **1983 Table "a"**, the **Annuity 2000 Mortality Table**, the **2012 Individual Annuity Reserving (2012 IAR)
Table**, and the **1994 Group Annuity Reserving (1994 GAR) Table**.

**Definitions (¶¶2–9), the load-bearing ones:**

- **¶5 "Period table"** — "a table of mortality rates applicable to a given calendar year (the Period)."
- **¶6 "Generational mortality table"** — "a mortality table containing a set of mortality rates that decrease for a
  given age from one year to the next **based on a combination of a Period table and a projection scale containing
  rates of mortality improvement**."
- **¶7 "2012 IAR Table"** — the **Generational** table developed by the SOA Committee on Life Insurance Research
  "containing rates, q<sub>x</sub><sup>2012+n</sup>, derived from a combination of the 2012 IAM Period Table and
  Projection Scale G2, using the methodology stated in paragraphs 13 and 14."
- **¶8 "2012 IAM Period Table"** — the Period table "containing **loaded** mortality rates for calendar year 2012",
  rates q<sub>x</sub><sup>2012</sup>, shown in Appendices I and II.
- **¶9 "Projection Scale G2"** — "a table of annual rates, G2<sub>x</sub>, of mortality improvement by age for
  projecting future mortality rates beyond calendar year 2012", shown in Appendices III and IV.

**¶13 — application of the 2012 IAR Table, as printed:**

```
q_x^(2012+n) = q_x^2012 · (1 – G2_x)^n
```

**¶14 — the rounding rule and worked example, reproduced faithfully.** "The resulting q<sub>x</sub><sup>2012+n</sup>
shall be rounded to **three decimal places per 1,000**, e.g., 0.741 deaths per 1,000. Also, the rounding shall occur
according to the formula above, **starting at the 2012 period table rate**. For example, for a male age 30,
q<sub>x</sub><sup>2012</sup> = 0.741."

```
q_x^2013 = 0.741 * (1 – 0.010) ^ 1 = 0.73359,   which is rounded to 0.734
q_x^2014 = 0.741 * (1 – 0.010) ^ 2 = 0.7262541, which is rounded to 0.726
```

and the explicitly-wrong method, quoted: *"A method leading to incorrect rounding would be to calculate
q<sub>x</sub><sup>2014</sup> as q<sub>x</sub><sup>2013</sup> * (1 – 0.010), or 0.734 * 0.99 = 0.727. **It is incorrect
to use the already rounded q<sub>x</sub><sup>2013</sup> to calculate q<sub>x</sub><sup>2014</sup>.**"

(Cross-check against Appendix II and Appendix IV: male age 30 has q<sub>x</sub><sup>2012</sup> = 0.741 and
G2<sub>x</sub> = 0.010 — the example is internally consistent with the printed tables.)

**¶16 — application of the 1994 GAR Table, as printed:**

```
q_x^(1994+n) = q_x^1994 · (1 – AA_x)^n      where the q_x^1994 and AA_x are as specified in the 1994 GAR Table
```

**Modelling consequence.** The 2012 IAR and 1994 GAR are **generational**: the valuation mortality rate is a function of
**(age, calendar year)**, and the rounding rule forbids the recursive `q(y+1) = q(y)·(1−G2)` shortcut. Implement as
`q(x, 2012+n) = round3( q2012[x] * (1 − G2[x])**n )` evaluated from the **2012 base every time**, with the year offset
*n* recomputed at each projection step. A single-vector `q[x]` array cannot represent this basis.

### 13. A-822 — asset adequacy analysis requirements (the whole appendix)

Four paragraphs, and the appendix contains nothing else.

- **¶1, definition, in full:** *"'Asset adequacy analysis' means an analysis of the adequacy of reserves and related
  actuarial items, in light of the assets supporting such reserves and related items, to meet the obligations of an
  insurer."*
- **¶2, the adequacy requirement:** the reserves and related items, considered in light of the assets held with respect
  to them "including, but not limited to, the investment earnings on the assets, and the considerations anticipated to
  be received and retained under the policies and contracts, shall make adequate provision, **according to presently
  accepted actuarial standards of practice**, for the anticipated cash flows required by the contractual obligations
  and related expenses of the company."
- **¶3, the additional reserve, in full:** *"If the company determines as the result of asset adequacy analysis that a
  reserve should be held in addition to the aggregate reserve held and calculated in accordance with methods set forth
  in **Appendix A-820**, the company shall establish the additional reserve."*
- **¶4, release:** *"Additional reserves established above and deemed not necessary in subsequent years may be released.
  **The release of such reserves would not be deemed an adoption of a lower standard of valuation.**"*

**A-822 ¶3 is the AP&P locus of the "asset adequacy analysis is part of minimum reserves" rule**, and it is expressly
additive to the A-820 aggregate. **A-822 ¶4 read with A-820 ¶18** is the pair that keeps establishment and release of
the AAT additional reserve outside the change-in-valuation-basis accounting.
