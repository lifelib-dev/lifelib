## Scope and applicability

### What business each item governs

| Item | Business governed, as printed |
|---|---|
| **A-585** | "universal life insurance policies" — defined at ¶7 as "a life insurance policy where separately identified interest credits (other than in connection with dividend accumulations, premium deposit funds, or other supplementary accounts) and mortality and expense charges are made to the policy" (p. 1102). Both **flexible premium** (¶3) and **fixed premium** (¶2) forms are in scope, and **interest-indexed** UL is defined at ¶4 but see the gaps section. The reserve is "The terminal reserve for **the basic policy and any benefits and/or riders for which premiums are not paid separately** as of any policy anniversary" (¶8, p. 1102) — separately-premium-rated riders are outside it. |
| **A-250** | "Variable annuity" — "a policy or contract, individual or group, that provides for annuity benefits that vary according to the investment experience of a separate account or accounts maintained by the insurer as to the policy or contract" (¶1, p. 1095). |
| **A-255** | "Modified Guaranteed Annuity" — "a deferred annuity contract, individual or group, the underlying assets of which are held in a separate account, and the values of which are guaranteed if held for specified periods. The contract contains nonforfeiture values that are based upon a market-value adjustment formula if held for shorter periods. This formula may or may not reflect the value of assets held in the separate account. The assets underlying the contract must be in a separate account during the period or periods when the contract holder can surrender the contract." (¶1, p. 1096). |
| **A-270** | "Variable life insurance policy" — "an individual or group policy that provides for life insurance the amount or duration of which varies according to the investment experience of any separate account or accounts established and maintained by the insurer as to the policy" (¶1, p. 1097). |

### Effective dates — there are none

**This is the single most important applicability finding, and it is a negative one.** Searched
across all nine extracted pages: **none of A-585, A-250, A-255 or A-270 prints an effective date, an
operative date, an applicability threshold, a size test, a transition provision or any grandfathering
language.** The words "effective", "operative", "transition", "applies to policies issued on or
after", "grandfather" and any date other than the copyright years do not appear.

There is exactly **one** issue-year keying rule in the four items, and it is an *assumption-selection*
rule rather than an applicability rule — A-585 ¶8.j (p. 1104), quoted in full:

> "All present values shall be determined using (i) an interest rate (or rates) specified by Appendix
> A-820 for policies issued in the same year; (ii) the mortality rates specified by Appendix A-820
> for policies issued in the same year; and (iii) any other tables needed to value supplementary
> benefits provided by a rider which is being valued together with the policy."

reinforced by ¶10 (p. 1104):

> "The mortality and interest bases for calculating present values are the minimum standards in
> Appendix A-820."

**Consequence for the library.** Any statement of the form "A-585 applies to issues before/after
*date*" cannot be sourced to A-585. Its temporal reach is set entirely outside it — by the Standard
Valuation Law's year-of-issue minimum-standard machinery, by SVL §11 and the Valuation Manual
operative date, and by VM-20 §3.B.6's routing of the *All Other* NPR to VM-A/VM-C. A-585 itself
simply states a method and delegates its parameters to A-820 by year of issue. **Do not infer dates
from this text.**

## Mechanics

### A-585 — definitions that carry weight (¶¶1–7, p. 1102)

Quoted or closely paraphrased as printed; the five that change a calculation are marked ●.

1. ● **"Cash surrender value" means the net cash surrender value plus any amounts outstanding as
   policy loans.** (So "cash surrender value" in this item is *gross of* loans; "net cash surrender
   value" is the payable amount.)
2. **"Fixed premium universal life insurance policy" means a universal life insurance policy other
   than a flexible premium universal life insurance policy.**
3. ● **"Flexible premium universal life insurance policy" means a universal life insurance policy
   which permits the policyowner to vary, independently of each other, the amount or timing of one or
   more premium payments or the amount of insurance.** (This definition is what switches the r-ratio
   on — see ¶8.d.)
4. **"Interest-indexed universal life insurance policy" means any universal life insurance policy
   where the interest credits are linked to an external referent.**
5. **"Net cash surrender value" means the maximum amount payable to the policyowner upon surrender.**
6. ● **"Policy value" means the amount to which separately identified interest credits and mortality,
   expense, or other charges are made under a universal life insurance policy.** (The quantity
   compared to the Guaranteed Maturity Fund in ¶8.d and projected in ¶8.i.)
7. ● **"Universal life insurance policy"** — full text quoted in the scope table above. Closing
   sentence: "A universal life insurance policy may provide for other credits and charges, such as
   charges for the cost of benefits provided by rider."

### A-585 — the reserve construction (¶8, pp. 1102–1104)

The standard is stated first (¶8, p. 1102):

> "The minimum valuation standard for universal life insurance policies shall be the Commissioners
> Reserve Valuation Method, as described below for such policies, and the tables and interest rates
> specified below. The terminal reserve for the basic policy and any benefits and/or riders for which
> premiums are not paid separately as of any policy anniversary shall be equal to the net level
> premium reserves less (C) and less (D), where:"

So, with `t` the duration and `x` the issue age:

```
V(t)  =  NetLevelPremiumReserve(t)  −  (C)  −  (D)                                        [¶8]

NetLevelPremiumReserve(t) = ( (A) − (B) ) · r                                             [¶8.a]

(A) = present value of all future guaranteed benefits at the date of valuation            [¶8.a.i]

(B) = ( PVFB / ä_x ) · ä_{x+t}                                                            [¶8.a.ii]

(C) = ( (a) − (b) ) · ( ä_{x+t} / ä_x ) · r                                               [¶8.f]

(D) = Σ additional quantities analogous to (C) arising from structural changes            [¶8.g]
```

**Symbols, each defined by the text itself:**

- **PVFB** — "the present value of all benefits guaranteed at issue assuming future guaranteed
  maturity premiums are paid by the policyowner and taking into account all guarantees contained in
  the policy or declared by the insurer" (¶8.a.ii, p. 1103). Note it is fixed *at issue*, in
  contrast to (A), which is "at the date of valuation". `PVFB / ä_x` is therefore the net level
  valuation premium, and (A) − (B) is the ordinary prospective net level premium reserve.
- **ä_x, ä_{x+t}** — "present values of an annuity of one per year payable on policy anniversaries
  beginning at ages x and x+t, respectively, and **continuing until the highest attained age at which
  a premium may be paid under the policy**. The letter 'x' is defined as the issue age and the letter
  't' is defined as the duration of the policy." (¶8.b, p. 1103). **The annuity term is the
  premium-paying period, not the coverage period** — an easy implementation error.
- **r** — "equal to one, unless the policy is a **flexible premium** policy and the policy value is
  less than the guaranteed maturity fund, in which case 'r' is the ratio of the policy value to the
  guaranteed maturity fund" (¶8.d, p. 1103). i.e.

  ```
  r = 1                                       if the policy is a FIXED premium UL policy
  r = min( 1 , PolicyValue(t) / GMF(t) )      if the policy is a FLEXIBLE premium UL policy
  ```

  **The fixed-premium branch is unconditional: r is always 1 there.** A blanket
  `r = min(1, PV/GMF)` is wrong for fixed premium UL.
- **(a) − (b)** — "as described in **paragraph 9 of Appendix A-820** for the plan of insurance
  defined at issue by the guaranteed maturity premiums and all guarantees contained in the policy or
  declared by the insurer" (¶8.f, pp. 1103–1104). **A-820 was not extracted for this work; the
  content of (a) and (b) is therefore not stated here.** Structurally it is the CRVM expense
  allowance, applied to a synthetic plan of insurance whose premiums are the GMPs.

**Guaranteed Maturity Premium (¶8.c, p. 1103), quoted in full for the flexible-premium form:**

> "The guaranteed maturity premium for flexible premium universal life insurance policies shall be
> that level gross premium, paid at issue and periodically thereafter over the period during which
> premiums are allowed to be paid, which will mature the policy on the latest maturity date, if any,
> permitted under the policy (otherwise at the highest age in the valuation mortality table), for an
> amount which is in accordance with the policy structure.¹ The guaranteed maturity premium is
> calculated at issue based on all policy guarantees at issue (excluding guarantees linked to an
> external referent). The guaranteed maturity premium for fixed premium universal life insurance
> policies shall be the premium defined in the policy which at issue provides the minimum policy
> guarantees.²"

The two footnotes are operative, not commentary:

> **¹** "The maturity amount shall be the initial death benefit where the death benefit is level over
> the lifetime of the policy except for the existence of a minimum-death-benefit corridor, or shall be
> the specified amount where the death benefit equals a specified amount plus the policy value or cash
> surrender value except for the existence of a minimum-death-benefit corridor." (p. 1103)

> **²** "The Guaranteed Maturity Premium for both flexible and fixed premium policies shall be
> adjusted for death benefit corridors provided by the policy. The Guaranteed Maturity Premium may be
> less than the premium necessary to pay all charges. This can especially happen in the first year for
> policies with large first year expense charges." (p. 1103)

So the GMP is a **solve**: the level gross premium, payable over the allowed premium-paying period,
that drives the policy value to the maturity amount at the latest permitted maturity date (or the
end of the valuation mortality table if none), computed on guarantees at issue, **with guarantees
linked to an external referent excluded**, and **adjusted for death benefit corridors**. The maturity
amount is the initial death benefit for an Option A / level design and the specified amount for an
Option B / specified-amount-plus-value design, in both cases ignoring the corridor.

**Guaranteed Maturity Fund (¶8.e, p. 1103), quoted in full:**

> "The guaranteed maturity fund at any duration is that amount which, together with future guaranteed
> maturity premiums, will mature the policy based on all policy guarantees at issue."

The GMF is thus the prospective guarantee-basis fund path implied by the GMP — a second solve, per
duration.

**Projection rule for future guaranteed benefits (¶8.i, p. 1104), quoted in full:**

> "Future guaranteed benefits are determined by (1) projecting the greater of the guaranteed maturity
> fund and the policy value, taking into account future guaranteed maturity premiums, if any, and
> using all guarantees of interest, mortality, expense deductions, etc., contained in the policy or
> declared by the insurer; and (2) taking into account any benefits guaranteed in the policy or by
> declaration which do not depend on the policy value."

**This is the mechanism the r-ratio pairs with, and the pairing is the design logic of the whole
item.** When a flexible-premium policy is underfunded (policy value < GMF), the benefit projection
in (A) uses the **greater** of the two — i.e. the GMF, giving a *fully funded* reserve — and the
resulting net level premium reserve is then **scaled down by r = policy value / GMF**. Over-funded
policies project on the policy value itself and take r = 1.

**Declared guarantees (¶9, p. 1104):**

> "To the extent that the insurer declares guarantees more favorable than those in the policy
> (contractual guarantees), such declared guarantees shall be applicable to the determination of
> future guaranteed benefits."

**Structural changes (¶¶8.g, 8.h, 11 and footnote 3).** ¶8.h (p. 1104): "The guaranteed maturity
premium, the guaranteed maturity fund and (B) above shall be recalculated to reflect any structural
changes in the policy. This recalculation shall be done in a manner consistent with the descriptions
above." ¶8.g: (D) is "the sum of any additional quantities analogous to (C) which arise because of
structural changes³ in the policy, with each such quantity being determined on a basis consistent
with that of (C) using the maturity date in effect at the time of the change."

Footnote 3 (p. 1104) is the definition and is long; quoted in full because it is the only place the
scope of "structural change" is fixed:

> "Structural changes are those changes which are separate from the automatic workings of the policy.
> Such changes usually would be initiated by the policyholder and include changes in the guaranteed
> benefits, changes in latest maturity date, or changes in allowable premium payment period. For fixed
> premium universal life policies with redetermination of all credits and charges no more frequently
> than annually, on policy anniversaries, structural changes also include changes in guaranteed
> benefits, or in fixed premiums, unanticipated by the guaranteed maturity premium for such policies
> at the date of issue, even if such changes arise from automatic workings of the policy. The
> recomputation of (B) in paragraph 8.a.ii. above, for fixed premium universal life structural
> changes, shall exclude from PVFB, the present value of future guaranteed benefits, those guaranteed
> benefits which are funded by the excess of the insurer's declared guarantees of interest, mortality
> and expenses, over the guarantees contained in the policy at the date of issue."

¶11 (p. 1104) gives the sanctioned simplification, and is the closest thing in the item to a worked
method:

> "In effecting structural changes, consistent methods are prescribed when calculating reserves.
> Several such methods are possible, but perhaps the simplest such method would be that of maintaining
> proportionality between the Guaranteed Maturity Fund and Guaranteed Maturity Premium values and the
> current face amount. In applying this method, Guaranteed Maturity Fund and Guaranteed Maturity
> Premium values could be calculated per dollar of face amount and simply multiplied by the new face
> amount. This would eliminate much of the complexity involved in other methods."

**Implementation reading:** hold GMP and GMF **per $1 of face**, and rescale on a face change. That
turns (D) from an open-ended accumulation of change layers into a rescaling, at the cost of being
only one permitted method ("several such methods are possible") — it is permissive language, not a
prescription.

### A-585 — Alternative Minimum Reserves (¶¶12–13, pp. 1104–1105)

The trigger and the test, quoted in full (¶12, p. 1104):

> "If, in any policy year, the guaranteed maturity premium on any universal life insurance policy is
> less than the valuation net premium for such policy, calculated by the valuation method actually
> used in calculating the reserve thereon but using the minimum valuation standards of mortality and
> rate of interest, the minimum reserve required for such contract shall be the greater of a. or b."

> "a. The reserve calculated according to the method, the mortality table, and the rate of interest
> actually used."

> "b. The reserve calculated according to the method actually used but using the minimum valuation
> standards of mortality and rate of interest and replacing the valuation net premium by the
> Guaranteed Maturity Premium in each policy year for which the valuation net premium exceeds the
> Guaranteed Maturity Premium."

And the valuation net premium the test runs on (¶13, p. 1105):

> "For universal life insurance reserves on a net level premium basis, the valuation net premium is:"

```
        PVFB
        ────
         ä_x
```

> "and for reserves on a Commissioners Reserve Valuation Method, the valuation net premium is:"

```
        PVFB     (a) − (b)
        ──── +   ─────────
         ä_x        ä_x
```

**As a procedure:**

```
for each policy year k:
    VNP(k) = valuation net premium on the method ACTUALLY used, on MINIMUM mortality and interest
             ( = PVFB/ä_x                        on a net level premium basis          [¶13]
               = PVFB/ä_x + ((a)−(b))/ä_x        on a CRVM basis                       [¶13] )
    if GMP < VNP(k) for any k:
        AMR_b = reserve on the method actually used, on MINIMUM mortality and interest,
                with VNP(k) REPLACED BY GMP in every year k where VNP(k) > GMP
        MinimumReserve = max( reserve on the method/table/rate actually used , AMR_b )
```

Three details that decide an implementation:

- The trigger is **per policy year** ("If, in any policy year …"), but the replacement in (b) is also
  applied **year by year** ("in each policy year for which the valuation net premium exceeds the
  Guaranteed Maturity Premium") — so a policy can be in the test with the substitution active in only
  some years.
- Both legs are computed on the **method actually used**; only the mortality/interest basis is forced
  to the minimum standards in leg (b) and in the trigger test.
- The GMP replaces the *valuation net premium*, not the gross premium. This is structurally the same
  shape as a deficiency test but its comparator is the **Guaranteed Maturity Premium**, not the
  contract gross premium.

**No worked example, no tables and no factors appear anywhere in A-585.** All mortality and interest
parameters are delegated to Appendix A-820 (¶¶8.j, 10). The item prints no numbers at all beyond
paragraph numbers.

### A-250 — Variable Annuities (PDF p. 1095)

The whole item is seven printed lines of substance. Definitions:

- ¶1 "Variable annuity" (quoted in the scope table above).

Then two operative paragraphs, quoted in full — this is the entire valuation content:

> "2. The company shall maintain in each such separate account assets with a value at least equal to
> the reserves and other contract liabilities with respect to the account."

> "3. The reserve liability for variable annuities shall be established pursuant to the requirements
> of Appendix A-820 in accordance with actuarial procedures that recognize the variable nature of the
> benefits provided and any mortality guarantees."

**Finding, stated plainly: A-250 is a pointer, not a reserve method.** It contains no formula, no
symbol, no table, no factor, no CARVM adaptation, no greatest-present-value construction, no
election-path rule, no interim-value rule and no mention of CARVM at all. It does two things: it
imposes a **separate account asset-coverage floor** (¶2), and it **delegates the reserve entirely to
Appendix A-820** with a two-clause instruction to recognise (i) the variable nature of the benefits
and (ii) any mortality guarantees (¶3). It is not even given its own item heading for "Valuation
Requirements" — the three paragraphs sit under "Definitions".

### A-255 — Modified Guaranteed Annuities (PDF p. 1096)

Definitions: ¶1 "Modified Guaranteed Annuity" (quoted in full in the scope table above); ¶2
**"'Interest credits' means all interest that is credited to the contract."**; ¶3 "'Separate account'
means a separate account established pursuant to the insurance laws pertaining to the insurer."

**Valuation Requirements**, quoted in full — again this is the entire content:

> "4. Reserve liabilities for modified guaranteed annuities shall be established as provided in
> Appendix A-820 in accordance with actuarial procedures that recognize:
> a. That assets of the separate account are based on market values;
> b. The variable nature of benefits provided; and
> c. Any mortality guarantees."

> "5. As a minimum, the separate account liability will equal the surrender value based upon the
> market-value adjustment formula contained in the contract. If that liability is greater than the
> market value of the assets, a transfer of assets will be made into the separate account so that the
> market value of the assets at least equals that of the liabilities. Any additional reserve that is
> needed to cover future guaranteed benefits shall be established."

> "6. The market-value adjustment formula, the interest guarantees, and the degree to which projected
> cash flow of assets and liabilities are matched must also be considered. The company shall determine
> whether the assets in the separate account are adequate to provide all future benefits that are
> guaranteed."

**Separate Accounts:**

> "7. The insurer shall maintain in each separate account assets with a value at least equal to the
> valuation reserves and other contract liabilities respecting such account."

**Finding: A-255 is a pointer plus one floor.** It is thicker than A-250 by exactly three operative
rules, and they are worth having:

1. A **floor**: separate account liability ≥ the surrender value produced by **the contract's own
   MVA formula** (¶5). The MVA formula is the contract's, not a prescribed one — A-255 prints no MVA
   formula and no parameters for one.
2. An **asset top-up**: if that liability exceeds the market value of the separate account assets, a
   transfer into the separate account must make market value at least equal the liability (¶5). This
   is a general-account-funds-the-shortfall rule stated on the asset side.
3. A **residual reserve**: "Any additional reserve that is needed to cover future guaranteed benefits
   shall be established" (¶5), with ¶6 requiring consideration of the MVA formula, the interest
   guarantees and asset/liability cash flow matching, and an affirmative company determination of
   asset adequacy for all guaranteed benefits.

Everything else — the reserve method itself — is delegated to **Appendix A-820**. **A-255 does not
mention CARVM, does not prescribe an interim value, and does not contain a single formula, symbol,
table or factor.**

**A-255 ¶1 is also a scope definition with load-bearing use elsewhere.** The library records that
VM-21 §2.A.2 excludes contracts falling under VM-A item A-255 [REG-R35, recorded at
`_research/registered-index-linked-annuity.md` and `_research/variable-annuity.md`]. The test
for that exclusion is the ¶1 definition quoted above; its four elements are (i) deferred annuity,
individual or group, (ii) underlying assets held in a separate account, (iii) values guaranteed if
held for specified periods, (iv) nonforfeiture values based on an MVA formula if held for shorter
periods — plus the requirement that "The assets underlying the contract must be in a separate account
during the period or periods when the contract holder can surrender the contract." The exclusion
itself is VM-21's text, not A-255's; A-255 supplies only the definition.

### A-270 — Variable Life Insurance (PDF pp. 1097–1099) — *no reference id assigned*

Fourteen definitions (¶¶1–14, pp. 1097–1098). The ones that feed a calculation:

- ¶3 **"Assumed investment rate" means "the rate of investment return that would be required to be
  credited to a variable life insurance policy, after deduction of charges for taxes, investment
  expenses and mortality and expense guarantees to maintain the variable death benefit equal at all
  times to the amount of death benefit, other than incidental insurance benefits, which would be
  payable under the plan of insurance if the death benefit did not vary according to the investment
  experience of the separate account."**
- ¶4 "'Benefit base' means the amount to which the net investment return is applied."
- ¶9 "'Minimum death benefit' means the amount of the guaranteed death benefit, other than incidental
  insurance benefits, payable under a variable life insurance policy regardless of the investment
  performance of the separate account."
- ¶10 "'Net investment return' means the rate of investment return in a separate account to be applied
  to the benefit base."
- ¶12 "'Policy processing day' means the day on which charges authorized in the policy are deducted
  from the policy's cash value."
- ¶13 "'Scheduled premium policy' means a variable life insurance policy under which both the amount
  and timing of premium payments are fixed by the insurer."; ¶6 "'Flexible premium policy' means any
  variable life insurance policy other than a 'scheduled premium policy' as defined in paragraph 13."
- ¶14 "'Variable death benefit' means the amount of the death benefit, other than incidental insurance
  benefits, payable under a variable life insurance policy dependent on the investment performance of
  the separate account, which the insurer would have to pay in the absence of any minimum death
  benefit."

¶¶2, 5, 8, 11 define "Affiliate", "Control" (with a **ten percent (10%)** voting-securities
presumption), "Incidental insurance benefit" and "Person" — corporate-governance vocabulary, not
valuation inputs.

**Valuation Requirements.** ¶15 (p. 1098) is the same pointer as A-250 ¶3 and A-255 ¶4: "Reserve
liabilities for variable life insurance policies shall be established as provided in Appendix A-820
in accordance with actuarial procedures that recognize the variable nature of the benefits provided
and any mortality guarantees."

**¶16 is different, and it is the one place in the three separate-account items where a genuine
reserve mechanic is printed.** It is the **guaranteed minimum death benefit** reserve, and unlike
everything else in A-250/A-255/A-270 it is a construction, not a delegation. Opening sentence
(p. 1098):

> "Reserve liabilities for the guaranteed minimum death benefit shall be the reserve needed to provide
> for the contingency of death occurring when the guaranteed minimum death benefit exceeds the death
> benefit that would be paid in the absence of the guarantee, and shall be maintained in the general
> account of the insurer and shall not be less than the greater of the following minimum reserves:"

**Leg a — the one-third depreciation term-cost floor** (¶16.a, p. 1098), quoted in full:

> "The aggregate total of the term costs, if any, covering a period of one full year from the
> valuation date or, if less, covering the period provided for in the guarantee not otherwise provided
> for by the reserves held in the separate account, on each variable life insurance contract, assuming
> an immediate one-third depreciation in the current value of the assets in the separate account
> followed by a net investment return equal to the assumed investment rate; or"

**Leg b — the "attained age level" reserve** (¶16.b, pp. 1098–1099). The recursion, as printed:

```
AttainedAgeLevelReserve(y) = max( 0 , Residue(y) + Payment(y) )                       [¶16.b]
        "shall not be less than zero and shall equal the 'residue' … of the prior year's
        'attained age level' reserve on the contract, with any such 'residue,' increased or
        decreased by a payment computed on an attained age basis"

Residue(y) = max( 0 , [ AttainedAgeLevelReserve(y−1) · (1 + i_v)  −  TabularClaims(y) ]
                       / TabularProbabilityOfSurvival(y) )                            [¶16.b.i]
        "determined by adding interest at the valuation interest rate to the prior year's
        reserve, deducting the tabular claims based on the 'excess,' if any, of the guaranteed
        minimum death benefit over the death benefit that would be payable in the absence of a
        guarantee, and dividing the net result by the tabular probability of survival"

Payment(y)  =  ( (A) − (B) − (C) )  /  ä_temporary(attained age, charge-collection period)   [¶16.b.ii]
        (A) = present value of the future guaranteed minimum death benefits
        (B) = present value of the future death benefits that would be payable in the absence
              of such guarantee
        (C) = any "residue," as described in paragraph 16.b.i., of the prior year's "attained
              age level" reserve on such variable life insurance contract
        ä_temporary = "the present value, at the valuation date, of a temporary life annuity of
              one per annum at the current attained age payable over the period in which future
              charges for this risk will be collected under the contract"
        if no future charges for this risk will be collected:  Payment(y) = (A) − (B) − (C)
```

Two constraints printed with the recursion:

- The "excess" driving the tabular claims in the residue "shall be based on the actual level of death
  benefits that would have been in effect during the preceding year in the absence of the guarantee,
  taking appropriate account of the reserve assumptions regarding the distribution of death claim
  payments over the year" (¶16.b.i, pp. 1098–1099).
- **A cap on the projection rate for (B)**: "The amounts of the future death benefits referred to in
  (B) shall be computed assuming a net investment return of the separate account which may differ from
  the assumed investment rate or the valuation interest but **in no event may exceed the maximum
  interest rate permitted for the valuation of life contracts**." (¶16.b.ii, p. 1099).

**Basis and approximation** (¶16.c, p. 1099): "The valuation interest rate and mortality table used in
computing the two minimum reserves described in 16 a. and 16 b. above shall conform to acceptable
standards for the valuation of life insurance contracts. In determining the minimum reserves, the
company may employ suitable approximations and estimates, including but not limited to groupings and
averages." Note the softer wording — "acceptable standards", not the hard A-820 delegation used in
¶15.

**¶17 — incidental insurance benefits** (p. 1099), as printed: "Reserve liabilities for all fixed
incidental insurance benefits and any guarantees associated with variable accidental insurance
benefits shall be maintained in the general account and reserve liabilities for all variable aspects
of the variable incidental insurance benefits shall be maintained in a separate account, in amounts
determined in accordance with the actuarial procedures appropriate to the benefit." (On "variable
**accidental**" see Gaps and caveats.)

**¶¶18–19 — separate accounts** (p. 1099): "The assets of separate accounts shall be valued at least
as often as variable benefits are determined but, in any event, **at least monthly**." and the same
asset-coverage floor carried by A-250 ¶2 and A-255 ¶7.

## What this settles for the library

### Confirmed

1. **`us/regulatory/statutory-accounting-and-capital.md`, "The reserve hierarchy", row *Universal
   life*: "Model #585 §5 CRVM adaptation (GMP/GMF)" and "NPR routes through VM-A item **A-585**".**
   Substantively confirmed. A-585 is the Commissioners Reserve Valuation Method adapted for UL and it
   is built on the Guaranteed Maturity Premium, the Guaranteed Maturity Fund and the r-ratio, exactly
   as the row asserts. **[R155]** now sources it first-hand.
2. **`products/universal_life/technical-notes.md` (≈ line 381): "Guaranteed Maturity Premium /
   Guaranteed Maturity Fund with the `r = min(1, policy value / GMF)` ratio and the alternative
   minimum reserve test".** All three components confirmed to exist and to be the operative mechanics
   — but the r formula needs the fixed/flexible qualification below.
3. **SSAP No. 51 as the accounting home.** A-585's own header names **SSAP No. 51—Life Contracts**
   and **SSAP No. 56—Separate Accounts** as the relevant SSAPs, consistent with the library's use of
   SSAP No. 51 for life-contract reserves and SSAP No. 56 for separate accounts [REG-R79][REG-R83].
4. **A-255's relevance to MVA business.** The library's separate-accounts section says a RILA
   separate account may be at book value and may carry its own AVR and IMR [REG-R83]. A-255 ¶5's
   requirement that the separate account liability equal at least the contract-MVA surrender value,
   with an asset transfer to cover any shortfall, is the reserve-side counterpart and is now sourced
   **[R157]**.

### Contradicted or in need of correction

5. **The "not retrieved" caveats are now stale for these four items.** Both
   `us/regulatory/statutory-accounting-and-capital.md` ("**Neither the AG I text nor A-585, A-820 and
   A-830 as printed in the manual was retrieved**", ≈ line 242) and
   `products/universal_life/technical-notes.md` ("the AP&P Appendix A print of A-585 was **not
   retrieved**", ≈ line 393) and `products/indexed_ul/technical-notes.md` ("**A-585, A-820 and
   A-830 were not retrieved**", ≈ line 377) must be narrowed to **A-820 and A-830 only**. A-585,
   A-250, A-255 and A-270 have now been read in full.
6. **`us/regulatory/statutory-accounting-and-capital.md` ≈ line 226 and
   `products/registered_index_linked_annuity/technical-notes.md` ≈ line 679: "the VM-A index's two
   closest formulaic items, A-250 (variable annuities) and A-255 (modified guaranteed annuities), were
   likewise not read".** Now read — **and the finding is that neither is a reserve method.** A-250 is
   three sentences: a separate-account asset floor and a delegation to A-820. A-255 is a delegation to
   A-820 plus an MVA-surrender-value floor, an asset top-up rule and a residual "additional reserve"
   requirement. **Neither contains a formula, a symbol, a factor, a table, or the word CARVM.**
   Calling them "the closest formulaic items" is defensible only in the sense of *nearest by subject
   matter*; they add **nothing** to a RILA formulaic CARVM run beyond the MVA floor. The library
   should say so: **a RILA CARVM run still rests on the SVL text alone, and reading A-250/A-255 does
   not change that.** That was the open question and it is now closed.
7. **The r-ratio formula needs a qualifier.** `r = min(1, policy value / GMF)` is correct **only for
   a flexible premium universal life insurance policy**. A-585 ¶8.d: "'r' is equal to one, unless the
   policy is a flexible premium policy and the policy value is less than the guaranteed maturity
   fund". **For a fixed premium UL policy r ≡ 1 regardless of funding level.**
8. **A-585 does not cite Model #585.** The item's "Relevant NAIC Model Laws/Regulations" line names
   only the **Standard Valuation Law (#820)**. Statements of the form "A-585 *is* Model #585 §5"
   should be softened to "A-585 carries the UL CRVM adaptation, and the AP&P print attributes it to
   the Standard Valuation Law (#820)". The library's own [REG-R5] annotation for Model #585 §5 stays
   valid as a separate source; the two texts were not compared line by line here because Model #585
   was not re-read for this extraction.
9. **`us/regulatory/technical-notes.md`, "Formulaic reserves" — the material gap, and the most
   consequential correction.** That section gives exactly two formulaic constructions: CRVM as the
   SVL §5.A modified-net-premium `k*` engine, and CARVM as the greatest-present-value engine. It then
   says, in the VM-20 section, "**All Other, and IUL where no DR or SR is computed, take the VM-A/VM-C
   formulaic basic reserve** — the CRVM engine above". **For universal life that instruction sends the
   implementer to the wrong engine.** A-585 prescribes a *different* CRVM construction for UL:

   ```
   SVL §5.A engine (in the library today):  V(s) = max(0, APV_s(B) − k*·APV_s(G))
                                            with a uniform percentage k* of CONTRACT gross premiums

   A-585 engine (missing from the library): V(t) = ((A) − (B))·r − (C) − (D)
                                            with (B) driven by the GUARANTEED MATURITY PREMIUM,
                                            not by contract gross premiums
   ```

   The A-585 engine has no `k*`, no 19-pay cap in its own text (it delegates the expense allowance to
   A-820 ¶9 via "(a) − (b)"), and introduces three objects the library's formulaic section does not
   define at all: the **GMP solve**, the **GMF path**, and the **r-ratio**. "Formulaic reserves" needs
   a third subsection.
10. **The Alternative Minimum Reserve test is absent from the library and is not the same thing as a
    deficiency reserve.** `us/regulatory/technical-notes.md`, "Deficiency and other additional
    reserves", defines a deficiency as "where the valuation net premium exceeds the actual gross
    premium collected" and routes it to AG I / Model #830. **A-585 ¶12's comparator is the Guaranteed
    Maturity Premium, not the gross premium**, and its remedy is a *substitution of the GMP for the
    valuation net premium* inside a re-run of the reserve on minimum mortality and interest, then a
    max against the reserve as actually computed. It is a distinct minimum, and it belongs in the
    formulaic section for UL.
11. **Indexed UL gets one directly relevant sentence.** A-585 ¶8.c: the GMP "is calculated at issue
    based on all policy guarantees at issue (**excluding guarantees linked to an external referent**)".
    Read with the ¶4 definition of an interest-indexed UL policy as one "where the interest credits are
    linked to an external referent", this means **the index-linked crediting is stripped out of the GMP
    solve entirely** — the GMP is computed on the non-indexed guarantees. `products/indexed_ul/`
    should carry this; it is the only index-specific reserve rule in the item.

### Left open

12. **(a) − (b), and hence the whole CRVM expense allowance for UL, is delegated to Appendix A-820
    ¶9 and cannot be stated from this extraction.** The library's existing SVL §5.A α/β material
    [REG-R1] is the closest thing it has, but A-585 points at **A-820 ¶9**, not at the SVL, and A-820
    was not extracted. Until A-820 is read, the (C) term and the CRVM leg of ¶13's valuation net
    premium remain unimplementable from library sources.
13. **The prescribed mortality table and valuation interest rate for a UL reserve are likewise in
    A-820** (¶¶8.j, 10). A-585 prints no rate, no table and no factor.
14. **Whether A-585 as printed in the March 2026 manual differs from Model #585 §5 as printed in the
    model law** is not settled here — Model #585 was not re-read for this extraction.
15. **The VM-20 §3.B.6 → VM-A → A-585 routing is asserted by VM-20 and VM-A** [REG-R3][REG-R110],
    **not by A-585.** A-585 contains no reference to VM-20, VM-A, the Valuation Manual, or
    principle-based reserving of any kind.

## Gaps and caveats

### What the documents do not address

- **No effective dates anywhere.** Restated because it is the negative finding most likely to be
  mis-remembered later: none of the four items carries an effective date, operative date,
  applicability threshold, transition rule or grandfathering clause. Do not attribute a date to
  A-585, A-250, A-255 or A-270.
- **A-585 carries the *valuation* half of the UL model regulation only.** The extracted pages contain
  definitions and valuation requirements and nothing else — **no nonforfeiture provisions, no
  mandatory policy provisions, no annual-report-to-policyowner requirements, and no separate
  interest-indexed UL section**. Model #585's own interest-indexed UL requirements (filing,
  demonstrations, the external referent) are **not** in this appendix print; the only indexed-UL rule
  here is the ¶8.c exclusion of externally-referenced guarantees from the GMP. Anything the library
  says about UL nonforfeiture must continue to cite Model #585 [REG-R5], not A-585.
- **No numbers.** A-585 prints no mortality table, no interest rate, no factor and no threshold. A-250,
  A-255 and A-270 print none either, apart from A-270 ¶5's 10% control presumption (governance, not
  valuation) and A-270 ¶16.a's "one-third depreciation".
- **No worked examples in any of the four items.** ¶11's proportionality method is the only
  quasi-procedural passage, and it is offered permissively ("Several such methods are possible").
- **Nothing on VM-20, VM-A, VM-C or PBR.** The routing that makes A-585 matter to a post-operative-date
  net premium reserve is asserted elsewhere [REG-R3 §3.B.6][REG-R110]; A-585 is silent on it.
- **Nothing on reinsurance, deficiency reserves, secondary guarantees, shadow accounts, AG 38 or
  Model #830.** A-585 is a standalone UL CRVM adaptation; ULSG machinery is not in it.
- **A-250 and A-255 contain no CARVM adaptation, no elective-benefit path enumeration, no interim
  value rule and no index-crediting rule.** For an index-linked separate account contract they are
  scope definitions plus, in A-255, an MVA floor.

### Extraction artefacts and ambiguities

The PDF text layer for these pages has **irregular line breaks mid-word and mid-phrase**, and it
**loses fraction bars, superscripts and diacritics**. Specifics, so a later reader can judge what is
solid and what is reconstructed:

1. **Fraction bars are lost.** ¶8.a.ii extracts as "(B) is the quantity PVFB äx+t where PVFB is …"
   with a bare "äx" on the following line; ¶8.f as "(C) is the quantity ((a)-(b))äx+t r where (a)-(b)
   is as described in paragraph 9 of" with "äx" on the following line; ¶13 as "PVFB / äx" and "PVFB +
   (a)-(b) / äx  äx" laid out over two lines. The **reading** — (B) = (PVFB/ä_x)·ä_{x+t}, (C) =
   ((a)−(b))·(ä_{x+t}/ä_x)·r, CRVM VNP = PVFB/ä_x + ((a)−(b))/ä_x — is the only arrangement consistent
   with the surviving token order and with (A)−(B) being a prospective net level reserve, but the
   **denominators are inferred from layout, not read from a bar character**. Anyone relying on the
   exact placement of `r` in (C) should re-open p. 1103.
2. **Word-internal breaks are frequent**: "surre nder", "polic y", "po licy", "morta lity",
   "supplem entary", "meth od", "th e", "declar ed", "prem iums", "guar anteed", "ca lculated",
   "separa te", "underlyi ng", "i ndividual", "de duction", "mortali ty", "th e", "res erves",
   "insu rer", "co llected", "valu ation", "Valu ation", "even i f", "ov er". These are spacing
   artefacts only; no text is missing. All quotations above have had them silently rejoined, and only
   those.
3. **Numbered-list numerals sit on their own lines** in A-585 ¶¶1–13 and in A-250/A-255 (e.g. a line
   containing just "8."), while A-270 pp. 1098–1099 and A-585 pp. 1104–1105 run them inline. This is
   a two-column-vs-single-flow extraction difference, not a difference in the source.
4. **Footnote markers survive but their superscripting does not.** "policy structure.1", "minimum
   policy guarantees.2", "structural changes3" appear with the digit inline. The three footnotes are
   present in full at the bottom of pp. 1103 and 1104 and are quoted above.
5. **A-270 ¶17 reads "any guarantees associated with variable *accidental* insurance benefits"**
   (p. 1099) in a paragraph otherwise entirely about *incidental* insurance benefits, which is the
   term defined at ¶8 and used in the same sentence's second clause. This may be as printed or may be
   an extraction/typographical artefact; **it is recorded as extracted and not corrected**. Re-check
   p. 1099 before relying on the distinction.
6. **A-270 ¶16.b.ii is internally redundant as printed**: the first sentence defines the payment as
   the level annual amount whose present value equals (A) − (B) − (C), and the third sentence then
   says "This result shall be divided by the present value … of a temporary life annuity of one per
   annum". Both sentences describe the same division. The recursion above renders it once, as
   Payment = ((A) − (B) − (C)) / ä_temporary, which is the only non-circular reading; the redundancy
   is in the source, not in the extraction.
7. **A-250's three paragraphs all sit under the heading "Definitions"** — there is no "Valuation
   Requirements" heading in that item, unlike A-255 (¶4 onward) and A-270 (¶15 onward). Recorded as
   printed; it does not change the effect of ¶3.
8. **Page 1100–1101 were not extracted** (they fall between A-270 and A-585 and presumably carry
   A-588, modified guaranteed life insurance, per the VM-A index at [REG-R110]). **That is an
   assumption about what is on those pages, not an observation** — they were not read.
