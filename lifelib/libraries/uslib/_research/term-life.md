## Regulatory and actuarial references

### R1 — NAIC Model 830: "Valuation of Life Insurance Policies Model Regulation" (Regulation XXX)
- Publisher: National Association of Insurance Commissioners
- Doc type: model regulation (PDF, 35 pp. as extracted)
- URL: https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/model-law-830.pdf
- Retrieved: YES (PDF downloaded and text-extracted)
- Key content: provides (1) tables of select mortality factors and rules for their use;
  (2) rules for a minimum valuation standard for plans with non-level premiums or benefits
  (Section 6); (3) rules for plans with secondary guarantees (Section 7). Basic reserves for
  policies with guaranteed nonlevel gross premiums = greater of segmented reserves and
  unitary reserves (Section 6.A). "Contract segmentation method" (Section 4.B) divides the
  period from issue into segments using ratios of guaranteed gross premium rates per $1,000.
  Deficiency reserves (Sections 4.C, 5.B) = excess of quantity A (basic reserve recomputed
  substituting guaranteed gross premiums where less than net premiums) over the basic
  reserve; for durations in the first segment, X percent of the select mortality factors may
  be used ("X factors"), with X subject to conditions, actuarial opinion/certification per
  ASB standards, and X factors reflecting company expectations. Valuation basis 1980 CSO
  with optional select mortality factors (Section 5.A); select factors for durations 1-15
  reflect SOA data. Unitary reserve definition caps the valuation net premium comparison at
  the net level annual premium on the nineteen-year premium whole life plan. Optional
  exemptions: YRT reinsurance (6.E), attained-age-based YRT (6.F), exemption from unitary
  reserves for certain n-year renewable term policies (6.G), certain juvenile policies
  (6.H). Policy fee treatment: guaranteed gross premiums without policy fees may be used
  in reserves where the fee is a level dollar amount after year 1 (Section 5.D).

### R2 — NAIC: "Valuation Manual" (Jan. 1 current edition; incl. VM-20 Requirements for Principle-Based Reserves for Life Products)
- Publisher: National Association of Insurance Commissioners (copyright 2025)
- Doc type: valuation manual / regulation (PDF, 457 pp. as extracted)
- URL: https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/pbr_data_valuation_manual_current_edition.pdf
- Retrieved: YES (PDF downloaded and text-extracted)
- Key content for term (VM-20): minimum reserve components — net premium reserve (NPR,
  VM-20 Section 3, seriatim), deterministic reserve (DR) and stochastic reserve (SR) with
  exclusion tests; term NPR mortality is the 2017 CSO Mortality Table as defined in VM-M
  Section 1.H (Section 3.C.1); NPR lapse rates for term (Section 3.C.3.b): 10%/yr during a
  level premium period of less than 5 years; 6%/yr during a level premium period of 5+
  years; for policies with an endowment/return-of-premium benefit, 6% for the first half of
  the initial level period and 0% for the remainder except the final year; 10%/yr in premium
  paying periods after an initial level period of <5 years; 0% once the final premium is
  payable; prescribed shock lapse in the final year of a level premium period, by length of
  level periods before/after and the percent premium increase per $1,000 (incl. policy fee):
  e.g. prior period >10 yrs to ART with increase <400% -> 70%; >=400% -> 80%; >10 yrs to a
  5<PP period -> 50%; 5<PP<=10 to ART -> 70%/80%; short-to-short combinations 25%-50%.
  Valuation net premiums after a shock lapse are limited (two uniform percentages, Section
  3.B.4). NPR floor: not less than cost of insurance to the next paid-to-date (Section 3.D).
  The Deterministic Exclusion Test "no longer applies to term insurance" (guidance note,
  Section 6). Post-level term profits: for the DR of term policies issued on/after
  1/1/2017, if PV of post-level-term cash inflows exceeds PV of outflows, a 100% lapse must
  be assumed at the end of the level term so no PLT profits are reflected; PLT losses must
  be reflected (Section 9.D.6.a); for the SR and for pre-2017 issues, assumptions must be
  adjusted so PLT profits approach zero where data lack credibility (Section 9.D.6.b).
  NPR interest: calendar-year NPR valuation rate per Section 3.C.2 (with a +1.5%, capped at
  125%, adjustment rule for certain amounts). 2017 CSO is also referenced for nonforfeiture
  (may be used in place of 2001 CSO from 1/1/2017; mandatory transition per VM-20/VM-M).

### R4 — SOA: "U.S. Post-Level Term Lapse & Mortality Experience" report (May 2021)
- Publisher: Society of Actuaries
- Doc type: experience study report (PDF, 155 pp.)
- URL: https://www.soa.org/globalassets/assets/files/resources/research-report/2021/us-post-level-term-lapse-mort-report.pdf
- Retrieved: YES (PDF downloaded and text-extracted)
- Key content: 25 contributing companies; lapse study covers issue years 1990+ and study
  period 2000-2017 (policy-year basis); mortality study same period. Jump to ART is the
  most common U.S. PLT premium structure (large premium jump at end of level term, then
  annually increasing ART scale); Graded PLT structures (lower initial jump, premiums grade
  up over several years) have become popular, including via in-force changes. Shock lapses
  ranged 27%-96% for Jump to ART and 31%-80% for Graded, increasing with the size of the
  premium jump; first-year PLT mortality deterioration ranged 154%-1,066% of level-period
  mortality for Jump to ART and 127%-247% for Graded. Initial premium jump defined as ratio
  of first PLT premium to level-term premium including policy fee (2014 study used
  per-$1,000 rates excluding the fee). Key shock-lapse drivers: initial premium jump,
  attained age, billing type, premium mode; monthly-mode policies show materially lower
  shock lapse and lower PLT mortality deterioration than annual-mode. Lapses remain
  elevated in PLT durations but decrease by duration; Jump to ART mortality deterioration
  decreases over PLT durations (below 200% after ~10 years), Graded stays relatively level.
  Cumulative premium jump explains Graded persistency: e.g. initial jump 3.01-4.00x reaches
  cumulative 10.01-14.00x by PLT duration 3.

### R5 — IRC Section 7702, "Life insurance contract defined" (26 U.S.C. 7702)

## Extracted specifications

### 1. Product architecture and premium structure
- Core design: guaranteed level gross premiums for an initial level term period, with the
  policy renewable annually thereafter without evidence of insurability at annually
  increasing premiums, to expiry at attained age 95 [S2][S3][S5][S6].
- Premiums and death benefit are fully guaranteed; there is no cash value, no dividends
  (non-participating), no policy loans [S3][S6]. Lincoln explicitly lists "Loans: N/A" [S3];
  the Pacific Life specimen contract is labeled Non-Participating and terminates "without
  value" on lapse [S6].
- The guaranteed maximum annual premiums for every policy year (level period + full ART
  tail to age 95) are printed in the policy specifications at issue [S6].
- Death benefit proceeds = face amount + rider benefits + pro-rata refund of premium paid
  beyond the month of death − premiums due and unpaid [S6].
- Premium payment modes and modal factors (applied to annual premium):
  - Banner: semi-annual 0.51, quarterly 0.26, monthly EFT 0.085 [S2]
  - Lincoln: semi-annual 0.515, quarterly 0.262, monthly 0.0875 [S3]
  - Corebridge (AGL): semiannual 0.5200, quarterly 0.2650, monthly 0.0845 [S5]
  - Pacific Life specimen: semi-annual 0.52, quarterly 0.27, monthly PAC 0.08333 [S6]
- Grace period 31 days; policy remains in force during grace; premium to end of policy
  month deducted from proceeds if death occurs in grace [S3][S6][S7].
- Reinstatement: within 3 years of lapse, evidence of insurability + overdue premiums with
  6.00% compound interest (Pacific Life) [S6]; within 5 years (Lincoln; within 15 days after
  the 46-day post-due-date window, no evidence needed) [S3].

### 2. Level term periods offered
- Protective Classic Choice: 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40 years [S1]
- Banner OPTerm: 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40 years [S2]
- Lincoln TermAccel: 10, 15, 20, 30 years [S3]
- Pacific Life PL Promise Term: 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 years (schedule forms S16LYT10-30) [S4]
- Corebridge Select-a-Term: 18 periods — 10, every year from 15 through 30, and 35 [S5]

### 3. Issue ages (age nearest birthday [S2][S5][S6]; Lincoln also ANB [S3])

  30-yr 18-58 (18-43); 35-yr 18-50 (18-40); 40-yr 18-45 (18-40). Minimum age 18.

  10-yr 20-75 (all classes); 15-yr 20-75 (all classes); 20-yr 20-70 (20-65);

  William Penn (NY) variants slightly tighter (e.g. 20-yr NT 20-65). Minimum age 20.
- Lincoln [S3]: 10/15/20-yr: 18-60 all classes; 30-yr: NT 18-55, Tobacco 18-50.

### 4. Face amounts and premium banding
- Minimum face amount $100,000 at all carriers examined [S1][S2][S3][S5]; Pacific Life
  specimen sample also shows $100,000 minimum [S6]. Protective has no set maximum [S1];
  Lincoln TermAccel caps at $1,000,000 (reflecting its accelerated/online underwriting
  program) [S3]; Corebridge caps instant/AU+ approval at $1 million [S5].
- Premium rate bands (rate per $1,000 decreases by band):
  - Banner: 10 bands — $100k-249,999 / 250k-499,999 / 500k-749,999 / 750k-999,999 /
    1.0M-1,499,999 / 1.5M-1,999,999 / 2M-2,999,999 / 3M-4,999,999 / 5M-10M / >10M [S2]
  - William Penn: 7 bands [S2]
  - Lincoln: 3 bands — $100k-249,999.99 / 250k-499,999.99 / 500k-1,000,000 [S3]
  - Corebridge: 4 bands — $100k-249,999 / 250k-499,999 / 500k-999,999 / >=1,000,000 [S5]

### 5. Rate (underwriting) classes
- Protective: Select Preferred, Preferred, Non-Tobacco, Tobacco (4 classes) [S1]
- Banner: Preferred Plus NT, Preferred NT, Standard Plus NT, Standard NT, Preferred
  Tobacco, Standard Tobacco (6 classes); table ratings applied to Standard Plus rates [S2]
- Lincoln: Preferred Plus NT, Preferred NT, Standard NT, Preferred Tobacco, Standard
  Tobacco (5 classes); substandard to Table D; flat extras to $5/1,000 [S3]
- Corebridge: Preferred Plus, Preferred NT, Standard Plus, Standard NT, Special NT,
  Preferred Tobacco, Standard Tobacco, Special Tobacco (8 classes); substandard through
  Table H (issue ages <=70, on Special rates) or Table D (ages >70) [S5]
- Risk class improvement: Pacific Life contract allows re-qualification to a better risk
  class on any anniversary after the 2nd, through age 70, with evidence of insurability
  and a fee up to $100 [S6].

### 6. Policy fees
- Banner: $90 annual (William Penn $80), in addition to banded per-$1,000 rates [S2]
- Lincoln: $80 (band 1) / $90 (bands 2-3), not commissionable [S3]
- Corebridge: $74 band 1 (commissionable) / $64 bands 2-4 (non-commissionable) [S5]

### 8. Post-level term (PLT) design
- Standard design ("Jump to ART"): face amount unchanged; premium jumps at the end of the
  level period and increases annually thereafter to expiry at 95 [S2][S6]. The SOA found
  Jump to ART is the most common structure in the U.S. market [R4].
- Corebridge variant: the death benefit DECREASES immediately after the level period, and
  the premium generally stays near its level-period amount immediately after the level
  period, then increases in later years [S5].
- Lincoln variant: automatic face amount decrease at the end of the level period with the
  gross premium held at the same level amount for three years, then annual premium
  increases to age 95 — explicitly described as a design to avoid premium "sticker-shock"
  and antiselective termination [S3].
- Graded PLT premium structures (smaller initial jump, premiums grading up annually with
  larger subsequent increases) have become popular, in some cases implemented by changing
  PLT premiums on in-force blocks [R4].
- Experience: shock lapse at end of level term 27%-96% (Jump to ART) / 31%-80% (Graded),
  increasing with the initial premium jump ratio and with attained age; first-year PLT
  mortality deterioration 154%-1,066% (Jump to ART) / 127%-247% (Graded) of level-period
  mortality; monthly premium mode materially lowers shock lapse and PLT mortality
  deterioration vs annual mode [R4]. Lapse rates start rising one to two policy years
  before the end of the level period [R6].

### 9. Conversion provisions
- Banner: convertible for the duration of the guaranteed level premium period or to
  attained age 70, whichever comes first; policies issued at age 66+ convertible during the
  first five policy years [S2].
- Lincoln: conversion period = earlier of the level term period (10/15/20/30 years) and
  attained age 70; without evidence of insurability if no premium in default, no face
  increase, same (or equivalent) rate class including substandard/flat extras; policies
  issued on/after 9/12/16 include a Conversion Products Amendment guaranteeing access to
  the full permanent portfolio for the first seven policy years of the conversion period
  and to a limited portfolio (at least one permanent product) thereafter [S3].
- Corebridge: conversion to any UL issued by AGL/US Life through the 96th month (10-year
  plans) or 120th month (longer plans), and full convertibility to the end of the level
  period or attained age 70 if earlier, without evidence of insurability [unverified —
  from search snippets of Corebridge/marketing materials; the fetched S5 document does not
  spell out the conversion window].
- Pacific Life (PL Promise Term marketing): conversion at any time during the level premium
  period, not to exceed attained age 70, to a designated conversion UL product with no
  additional underwriting [S4 (product positioning); window quantified in unfetched
  materials — treat exact PL Promise window as [unverified]].
- Pacific Life (specimen contract mechanics) [S6]: conversion any time before the
  Conversion Period End Date stated in the specifications (sample shows a 5-year period on
  a 10-year plan); no evidence of insurability; new policy on any permanent plan the company
  makes available for conversion; same or most-comparable risk class; new policy face
  cannot exceed the term face; premiums at attained-age current rates; suicide/contestable
  periods carry over from the original issue date; conversion not allowed while waiver-of-
  premium disability benefits apply; partial conversion allowed once (converted portion
  >= $250,000, remainder >= minimum face); conversion credit (sample $140, equal to one
  annual premium) applied against the new policy's initial premium if conversion occurs
  after policy year 1.
- Protective: Conversion Choice rider extends conversion periods and adds an ExtendCare
  chronic-illness benefit on conversion; conversion without new underwriting [S1][S7];
  numeric windows (8/13/18 years by plan, to age 70) [unverified].
- Experience: SOA 2016 study analyzes conversion incidence and post-conversion mortality
  by age, gender, risk class, size; defines conversion as exercise of the option to convert
  to permanent without underwriting [R7].

### 12. Valuation and reserving (U.S. statutory)
- Pre-PBR statutory basis (Regulation XXX): basic reserve = greater of segmented and
  unitary reserves per contract segmentation method; deficiency reserves where guaranteed
  gross premiums < valuation net premiums, mitigated by X-factor select mortality; 1980 CSO
  basis with select factors; exemptions for certain n-year renewable term from unitary
  reserves; policy fee excludable when level after year 1 [R1].
- Current basis (VM-20 PBR): minimum reserve built from seriatim NPR plus excess of
  modeled DR/SR; term NPR uses 2017 CSO (VM-M 1.H), prescribed NPR interest, and
  prescribed lapses: 6%/yr during level periods of 5+ years (10% if <5 years), ROP-style
  policies 6% then 0%, 0% after final premium; prescribed shock lapse at the end of the
  level period of 25%-80% depending on period lengths and premium increase (e.g. 70%
  jumping to ART with <400% increase, 80% with >=400%) [R2].
- The deterministic exclusion test no longer applies to term insurance [R2].
- PLT profits: DR must assume 100% lapse at end of level term when PV(PLT inflows) >
  PV(PLT outflows) for post-2017 issues (no PLT profits capitalized); PLT losses must be
  reflected; SR/pre-2017 blocks must grade PLT profits toward zero where experience lacks
  credibility [R2].
- NPR floored at cost of insurance to next paid-to-date [R2].
- Valuation mortality table family: 2017 CSO composite / smoker-distinct / preferred
  structure, ANB/ALB, loaded/unloaded [R3].
- Tax qualification: 7702 CVAT or GPT+corridor; corridor percentages 250% (ages 0-40)
  grading to 100% (ages 90-95) [R5].

### 13. Experience assumptions for modeling
- Level-period lapses: industry study data available by product (10/15/20/30-year term),
  sex, issue age, policy year, face band, mode; e.g. 30-year term lapse rates at attained
  ages 60+ run 1.0%-1.5%; anticipatory lapses rise 1-2 years before the level period ends
  [R6].
- Shock lapse and PLT: see section 8; SOA tables give shock lapse by initial premium jump
  band (e.g. 3.01-4.00x, 8.01-10.00x), attained age, mode, billing type; PLT lapses remain
  elevated but decline by PLT duration; mortality deterioration declines by duration for
  Jump to ART (below ~200% after 10 PLT years) and persists for Graded [R4].
- Base mortality: ILEC inter-company experience (2012-2019 observations) is the standard
  industry reference for term mortality A/E analysis (expected basis 2015 VBT [unverified])
  [R8].
- Conversion behavior: SOA 2016 conversion study covers conversion incidence and
  post-conversion mortality; an updated 2009-2023 SOA/LIMRA study is underway [R7].

## Variations across insurers

1. Term period menus differ: Protective and Banner sell 10-40 year periods including 35-
   and 40-year terms [S1][S2]; Lincoln offers only 10/15/20/30 [S3]; Corebridge offers 18
   periods including every annual duration 15-30 [S5]; Pacific Life offers 10-30 in 5-year
   steps [S4]. The 10/15/20/30 menu is the classic core; 20-year has the largest in-force
   exposure industry-wide [R6].
2. Post-level term design is the biggest structural variation: (a) classic Jump-to-ART
   with face unchanged (Banner, Pacific Life) [S2][S6]; (b) automatic face decrease with
   premium held level 3 years then ART (Lincoln) [S3]; (c) immediate face decrease with
   premium initially near-level then increasing (Corebridge) [S5]; (d) graded PLT premium
   structures adopted by several carriers, sometimes retroactively on in-force [R4].
3. Rate class counts range from 4 (Protective) to 8 (Corebridge) [S1][S5]; most carriers
   use a preferred-plus/preferred/standard(+/-) x tobacco split with substandard table
   ratings layered on a standard-type class [S2][S3][S5].
4. Policy fees range $64-$90 and may or may not be commissionable [S2][S3][S5][S6]; band
   structures range from 3 bands (Lincoln) to 10 (Banner) [S2][S3].
5. Issue-age ranges: minimum 18 (Protective, Lincoln) or 20 (Banner, Corebridge); maximum
   80 on 10-year plans (Protective, Corebridge) but only 60 for Lincoln's simplified
   digital program [S1][S3][S5].
6. Conversion: all carriers allow conversion without evidence of insurability, typically
   bounded by the level period and attained age 70, but details vary — Banner grants 5
   years at issue ages 66+ [S2]; Lincoln guarantees full-portfolio access for 7 years via
   amendment [S3]; Pacific Life prints an explicit Conversion Period End Date and pays a
   conversion credit of about one annual premium [S6]; Protective sells an optional rider
   (Conversion Choice) that lengthens the window and adds a chronic-illness benefit
   [S1][S7].
7. Pricing at the competitive cell (F40/$1M/best NT/20-yr) is tightly clustered: the top
   7 carriers are within $4 per year of each other (~$477-$480), with laggards up to 54%
   higher [S4].
8. Representative design for a reference model: a Banner/Pacific-Life-style contract —
   level guaranteed premiums for 10/15/20/30 years, banded per-$1,000 rates plus a fixed
   annual policy fee, 4-6 rate classes, renewal on a guaranteed ART scale to attained age
   95 with unchanged face (Jump to ART), conversion to permanent before min(end of level
   period, age 70), accelerated-death-benefit rider included, non-participating, no cash
   value [S2][S6][R4 - Jump to ART "the most common structure for the U.S. term market"].
