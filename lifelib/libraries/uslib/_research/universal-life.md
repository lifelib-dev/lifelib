- Facts extracted (specimen values are for the sample insured: Male 35,
  Standard Nonsmoker, $100,000 basic coverage):
  - Policy specifications: planned annual premium $4,124.59; 7-pay premium
    $6,702.10; guideline single premium $34,138.15; guideline level premium
    $2,825.52; death benefit qualification test = Guideline Premium Test
    ("THIS ELECTION IS IRREVOCABLE FOR THE LIFE OF THE CONTRACT"); death
    benefit option A; guaranteed interest rate policy years 2+: 3% (year-1 rate

    per year; end year 10. Mechanics: during first policy month the surrender
    charge equals the initial amount less 1/12 of the reduction factor; it then
    declines by 1/12 of the reduction factor each monthly payment date, reaching
    zero after the end year. Each face-increase coverage layer gets its own
    surrender charge set; face decreases do NOT reduce the surrender charge.
  - Guaranteed maximum monthly COI rates per $1,000 NAR by policy year (issue
    age 35): year 1: 0.10090; year 5: 0.12840; year 10: 0.19940; year 20:
    0.45950; year 30: 1.27900; year 40: 3.23010; year 50: 9.24140; year 60:
    23.81220; year 70: 46.82420; year 77: 77.62690; years 78-86 (attained ages
    112-120): 83.33330 (= 1000/12, i.e., rate capped so monthly charge = full
    NAR/12); year 87+ (age 121+): 0.
  - Coverage charge (per-unit expense charge) for basic coverage $100,000:
    $26.00/month policy years 1-10; $15.60/month years 11-86; 0 thereafter
    (equivalently $0.26 then $0.156 per $1,000 face per month).
  - Monthly deduction = coverage charge + administrative charge + rider/benefit
    charges + cost of insurance charge; deducted from accumulated value on each
    monthly payment date before the monthly deduction end date; the deduction
    provides coverage for the FOLLOWING policy month. Insurer may charge less
    than maximums; lesser charges apply uniformly by class.
  - COI charge = (max monthly COI rate / 1000) x NAR allocated to the coverage
    layer; NAR allocated proportionately to layers by face amount.
  - Net amount at risk = (death benefit as of most recent monthly payment date /
    NAR factor) - accumulated value, with AV measured at the beginning of the
    policy month before the monthly deduction is assessed.
  - Accumulated value roll-forward: on policy date AV = net premium - first
    monthly deduction; on each other day AV = prior-day AV + interest + net
    premiums received - withdrawals and withdrawal fees - (on a monthly payment
    date) the monthly deduction. Interest credited daily using a 365-day year at
    no less than the guaranteed annual rate; additional interest discretionary,
    uniform by class.
  - Premium processing: premium load deducted first, then net premium credited
    to AV. Net premium = premium received - premium load; premium load =
    premium x premium load rate (current rate may be below the maximum, uniform
    by class). Minimum premium payment $50. Payments while a loan is outstanding
    are treated as loan repayments unless designated as premium.
  - Death benefit options: A = total face amount; B = total face amount + AV at
    death; C = total face amount + premiums paid - withdrawals/distributions
    (may fall below face if withdrawals exceed premiums). DB at death = greater
    of the option amount and the minimum DB under the qualification test.
    Option changes: to A or B only, at most once per policy year; total face
    adjusted so DB is unchanged at the change date; change rejected if it would
    create a MEC (unless requested).
  - Death benefit qualification tests: CVAT minimum DB = minimum required to
    qualify as life insurance under the Code, but not less than 101% of AV.
    GPT minimum DB = AV x corridor percentage by attained age: 0-40: 250%; 41:
    243; 42: 236; 43: 229; 44: 222; 45: 215; 46: 209; 47: 203; 48: 197; 49:
    191; 50: 185; 51: 178; 52: 171; 53: 164; 54: 157; 55: 150; 56: 146; 57:
    142; 58: 138; 59: 134; 60: 130; 61: 128; 62: 126; 63: 124; 64: 122; 65:
    120; 66: 119; 67: 118; 68: 117; 69: 116; 70: 115; 71: 113; 72: 111; 73:
    109; 74: 107; 75-90: 105; 91: 104; 92: 103; 93: 102; over 93: 101.
  - Guideline premium limit: sum of premiums paid less a portion of withdrawals
    may not exceed the greater of the guideline single premium and the sum of
    annual guideline level premiums to the earlier of payment date or age 100;
    excess refunded; insurer may force distributions to maintain 7702 status.
  - MEC limit: premiums less a portion of withdrawals may not exceed the 7-pay
    limit (cumulative 7-pay premiums during the 7-pay testing period) per IRC
    7702A; excess refunded unless owner elects MEC status in writing.
  - Face amount changes: increases require application + evidence of
    insurability, insured no older than age 90, minimum increase $25,000, each
    increase = new coverage layer with own COI rates, coverage charge and
    surrender charge; decreases: one per year, none in year 1, remaining face
    >= $1,000, layers reduced LIFO (most recent first).
  - Withdrawals: allowed on/after first policy anniversary; minimum $200; fee up
    to $25; no surrender charge on withdrawal; remaining net cash surrender
    value >= $500; under Option A a withdrawal that would increase NAR reduces
    total face, EXCEPT (free partial withdrawal) during the first 15 policy
    years, for the first withdrawal in a policy year, up to the lesser of
    $10,000 or 10% of net cash surrender value; additional GPT-driven face
    reduction rules keyed to 15% of total face withdrawn in 12 months. Under
    Options B/C, withdrawals reduce AV (B) or increase the withdrawal offset
    (C) rather than face.
  - Surrender: cash surrender value = AV - surrender charge; net CSV = CSV -
    policy debt.
  - Loans: available after free-look on sole security of AV; minimum loan $200;
    maximum loan = AV - 3x most recent monthly deduction - surrender charge -
    existing policy debt; loan interest accrues daily at a rate no greater than
    3.25%, due at end of policy year, capitalized if unpaid.
  - Grace period and lapse: triggered if AV less policy debt on a monthly
    payment date cannot cover the current monthly deduction; grace = 61 days;
    required payment >= 3x the monthly deduction due plus premium load; if
    insured dies in grace, DB proceeds reduced by overdue charges; upon lapse
    policy terminates with no value.
  - Reinstatement: within 5 years of end of grace; requires evidence of
    insurability, premium (net of load) covering grace-period deductions and
    loan interest plus 3 months of coverage; COI schedule resumes as if lapse
    never occurred; suicide/contestability restart at reinstatement.
  - Maturity: "This policy does not mature" — coverage continues for life.
    After the monthly deduction end date (age 121): monthly deductions cease,
    premiums not accepted, loans and loan repayments allowed, loan interest
    continues to accrue, withdrawals not allowed.
  - Basis of values (this 2008-era form): minimum nonforfeiture values use the
    guaranteed interest rate and 2001 CSO mortality, age nearest birthday;
    rates same for smokers and nonsmokers, sex-distinct except unisex issues
    use 80% male / 20% female blend.
  - Misc: non-participating; suicide exclusion and contestability 2 years
    (restart on increases/reinstatement); misstatement of age/sex adjusts DB by
    ratio of incorrect to correct COI rate; insurer reserves right to distribute
    AV if NAR exceeds 3x original total face; income benefit (settlement)
    options guaranteed at 2% interest, life income purchase rates based on
    Annuity 2000 female table with 5-year setback; deferral right up to 6
    months on surrenders/withdrawals/loans.

## Regulatory and actuarial references

### R1. NAIC — Universal Life Insurance Model Regulation (Model 585), January 2001 reprint
- Publisher: National Association of Insurance Commissioners
- URL fetched: https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/model-law-585.pdf
- Retrieved: YES (full PDF read, 14 pages)
- Facts extracted:
  - Definition (Sec. 3H): a UL policy is "a life insurance policy where
    separately identified interest credits ... and mortality and expense
    charges are made to the policy." Flexible premium UL permits the owner to
    vary amount/timing of premiums or amount of insurance; fixed premium UL is
    any other UL. Interest-indexed UL = interest credits linked to an external
    referent.
  - Scope (Sec. 4): all individual UL policies except variable UL.
  - Valuation (Sec. 5): minimum standard is CRVM as adapted; terminal reserve =
    net level premium reserve ((A)-(B))r less (C) and (D), built on the
    Guaranteed Maturity Premium (GMP: level gross premium at issue that matures
    the policy on the latest maturity date, else at highest age in valuation
    table, on policy guarantees) and Guaranteed Maturity Fund (GMF: amount
    that with future GMPs matures the policy on guarantees); r = min(1, policy
    value / GMF) for flexible premium policies; future guaranteed benefits
    projected on the greater of GMF and policy value using all guarantees
    (declared guarantees included); alternative minimum reserve test applies
    when GMP < valuation net premium; recalculation required on structural
    changes.
  - Nonforfeiture (Sec. 6A, flexible premium): minimum cash surrender value =
    retrospective accumulation of premiums paid minus accumulations of (i)
    benefit charges, (ii) averaged administrative expense charges for year 1
    and insurance-increase years, (iii) actual administrative expense charges
    other years, (iv) initial/additional acquisition expense charges not
    exceeding the initial/additional expense allowances, (v) service charges,
    (vi) withdrawal deductions — all at the actual credited interest rate(s) —
    minus any unamortized unused expense allowances. Averaged charges use the
    arithmetic average of charge rates in policy years 2-20. Initial expense
    allowance is that of the Standard Nonforfeiture Law for a fixed-premium
    endowment at the initial face, premiums to the highest premium-paying age,
    maturing at the latest maturity date; unused allowance amortized by the
    annuity ratio a(x+t)/a(x) on guaranteed mortality/interest. A surrender
    charge is permissible provided the result is not below the minimum CSV.
  - Mandatory policy provisions (Sec. 7): annual report to policyowner (report
    period ending within 3 months of mailing); notice that illustrations of
    current/future benefits may be requested; policy must state guarantees
    (minimum interest credits, maximum mortality and expense charges) and only
    guaranteed figures may appear in the policy; description of CSV calculation
    incl. guaranteed maximum expense loads, guaranteed minimum interest,
    guaranteed maximum mortality charges, surrender/withdrawal charges;
    interest credits may not remain conditional longer than 24 months; grace
    period of at least 30 days after lapse with written notice at least 30 days
    before termination, where lapse (unless otherwise defined) occurs when net
    cash surrender value first equals zero; misstatement of age/sex: DB = that
    purchased by the most recent mortality charge at correct age/sex; maturity
    date statement must warn coverage may not continue to maturity even if
    scheduled premiums paid.
  - Periodic disclosure (Sec. 9): annual report must show policy value at start
    and end of period, amounts credited/debited by type (interest, mortality,
    expense, riders), current death benefit, net cash surrender value,
    outstanding loans, and a warning notice if, on guarantees, the net CSV will
    not maintain the policy in force to the end of the next reporting period
    without further premiums.
  - Interest-indexed UL (Sec. 10): filing requirements (index description,
    crediting mechanics, investment policy) and annual actuarial opinion on
    asset/liability cash flows.

### R2. IRC §7702 — Life insurance contract defined
- Publisher: Legal Information Institute, Cornell Law School (U.S. Code)
- URL fetched: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/7702
- Retrieved: YES (fetched and summarized)
- Facts extracted:
  - A contract qualifies as life insurance if it meets either the cash value
    accumulation test (CVAT) or the guideline premium/corridor requirements
    (GPT).
  - CVAT: cash surrender value may not at any time exceed the net single
    premium needed to fund future benefits.
  - GPT: premiums paid may not exceed the guideline premium limitation =
    greater of guideline single premium (GSP) and sum of guideline level
    premiums (GLP); GLP is a level annual amount payable over a period not
    ending before age 95.
  - Cash value corridor: death benefit must be at least the applicable
    percentage of cash surrender value, grading from 250% through age 40 down
    to 105% at ages 75-90 and to 100%/101% area beyond age 95 (full statutory
    table; the specimen policy table in [S3] implements it).
  - 2021 change (Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021): for contracts issued
    after December 31, 2020 the statutory minimum rates were replaced by the
    dynamic "applicable ... insurance interest rate"; during the transition
    period beginning January 1, 2021 the insurance interest rate is 2% (CVAT
    minimum rate), with the guideline premium minimum rate defined relative to
    it (previously fixed 4% CVAT/GLP and 6% GSP) [statutory framework as
    summarized from the fetched text; pre-2021 fixed 4%/6% rates are
    [unverified] recollection].
  - Computational rules: deemed maturity date between age 95 and age 100;
    death benefit deemed not to increase.
- Note: the fetch was summarized by an automated reader; wording above reflects
  that summary. Exact subsection text should be re-verified before quoting in
  a formal document.

### R3. IRC §7702A — Modified endowment contract defined
- Publisher: Legal Information Institute, Cornell Law School (U.S. Code)
- URL fetched: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/7702A
- Retrieved: YES (fetched and summarized)
- Facts extracted:
  - A MEC is a contract meeting §7702 that was entered into on or after June
    21, 1988 and fails the 7-pay test, or one received in exchange for a MEC.
  - 7-pay test: "a contract fails ... if the accumulated amount paid under the
    contract at any time during the 1st 7 contract years exceeds the sum of the
    net level premiums which would have been paid ... if the contract provided
    for paid-up future benefits after the payment of 7 level annual premiums."
  - 7-pay premiums computed under §7702(b)(2)/(e) rules; first-year death
    benefit deemed provided to maturity (ignoring scheduled reductions after
    year 7).
  - Material change rule: on a material change (incl. DB increases) the
    contract is treated as newly entered; benefit reductions within the first 7
    years cause retroactive reapplication of the test; 90-day reinstatement
    exception for nonpayment-driven reductions.
  - Consequences: distributions (including loans) from a MEC taxed under §72(e)
    income-first rules with 10% additional tax (via cross-references to §72) —
    consequence detail [unverified] beyond the cross-reference itself.

    (beginning of year), highest in policy year 1 (dump-ins); Lifetime
    Guarantee products have the highest current-to-planned premium ratio in
    early years, Current Assumption products the highest thereafter; Lifetime
    Guarantee products show the most consistent premium persistency (required
    premiums to hold the secondary guarantee); VUL notably lower persistency
    than fixed and indexed.
  - Lapse: for Lifetime Guarantee focus, after policy year 3 lapse rates are
    materially lower than other product focuses; persistency ratios lower for
    policies that terminated during the study than for survivors.
  - Methodology: age-nearest-birthday; Balducci exposure; lapse assigned to
    policy year by actual lapse date. Detailed tables are behind the paid
    Experience Studies Pro package (not retrieved).
  - Participating companies include Nationwide, Pacific Life, Securian, John
    Hancock, Lincoln, Principal, Sammons, Brighthouse, Allianz, Global
    Atlantic, Thrivent, Western & Southern, Columbus Life, New England Life.

### R8. Actuarial Standards Board — ASOP No. 2 (Revised Edition, Doc. No. 204): "Nonguaranteed Elements for Life Insurance and Annuity Products" (adopted September 2021)
- Publisher: Actuarial Standards Board
- URL fetched: https://www.actuarialstandardsboard.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/asop002_204-2.pdf
- Retrieved: YES (full PDF read)
- Facts extracted:
  - Effective for actuarial services performed on or after June 1, 2022; scope
    covers determination and illustration of NGEs on individual policy forms
    where NGEs may vary at insurer discretion; UL explicitly in scope;
    dividends excluded (ASOP 15); illustrations under the NAIC model regulation
    excluded (ASOP 24).
  - Definitions: NGE = "any premium, charge, or benefit within an insurance
    policy that 1) affects policy costs or values, 2) is not guaranteed in the
    policy, and 3) can be changed at the discretion of the insurer" (examples:
    credited interest, COI charges, expense charges); guaranteed element = a
    premium/value/charge/benefit that limits an NGE (e.g., minimum credited
    interest rates, maximum COI charges); anticipated experience factor;
    determination policy; NGE framework; NGE scale; policy class.
  - Core principles (sec. 3.2): NGE scales are determined with the expectation
    that they will be revised only if anticipated experience factors have
    changed since issue or the previous revision; and NGE scales are based on
    reasonable expectations of future experience and "are not determined with
    the objective of recouping past losses or distributing past gains."
  - Revision process for in-force policies (3.4.2): review prior
    determinations, analyze emerging experience vs anticipated factors, decide
    whether to revise, and determine revised scales such that prospective
    profitability from the revision date, reflecting the pattern of profits by
    duration, is not materially greater than under the original scales/factors.
  - Background: NY Regulation 210 (March 2018) cited as an example of
    increased NGE regulation.

## Extracted specifications

All facts tagged. Specimen-specific values (Male 35 Standard Nonsmoker,
$100,000) are labeled as such.

### 1. Product definition and account mechanics
- Regulatory definition: UL = life policy with separately identified interest
  credits and mortality/expense charges made to an indeterminate policy value;
  flexible premium UL lets the owner vary premium amount/timing and insurance
  amount independently [R1].
- Account value (accumulated value) roll-forward [S3]:
  - At issue: AV = net premium − first monthly deduction.
  - Daily: AV(t) = AV(t−1) + daily interest + net premiums received −
    withdrawals/fees − monthly deduction (on monthly payment dates).
  - Interest credited daily, 365-day year, at no less than the guaranteed rate;
    excess interest discretionary and uniform within class [S3].
- Processing order per premium: (1) deduct premium load; (2) credit net premium
  to AV [S3]. Monthly deduction is taken on each monthly payment date (same day
  each month as policy date) and pays for the FOLLOWING month's coverage [S3].
- Monthly deduction components [S3]:
  monthly deduction = per-unit coverage charge + per-policy administrative
  charge + rider charges + COI charge.
- COI charge = (monthly COI rate / 1000) × NAR [S3]; NAR = DB / NAR factor −
  AV, where the NAR factor discounts one month at the guaranteed rate
  (specimen: 1.0024663 = 1.03^(1/12)) and AV is measured before the deduction
  [S3]. COI rates are per $1,000 of NAR in all three current-assumption source
  products [S1][S2][S3].
- Charges cease at attained age 121 (monthly deduction end date) [S2][S3];
  Symetra deducts COI to age 120 with premiums payable to 120 [S1].

### 2. Issue ages, classes, face amount bands
- Issue ages: 15 days–85 (Symetra, incl. juvenile class) [S1]; 18–85 with
  select-preferred classes capped at 75 (Protective) [S2]; 18–85 for NLG-to-120
  GUL (Nationwide) [S4].
- Risk classes: 6-7 classes typical — e.g., Super/Select Preferred NT,
  Preferred NT, Standard Plus NT, Standard NT, Preferred Tobacco, Standard
  Tobacco (+ juvenile) [S1][S2][S4]; specimen policy class "Standard
  Nonsmoker" [S3]. Juvenile insureds get a smoker/nonsmoker redetermination at
  age 20 [S3].
- Minimum face: $100,000 all classes (Symetra) [S1]; $50,000
  Non-Tobacco/Tobacco, $100,000 preferred classes (Protective) [S2].
- Face increases create separate coverage layers with own charges (min increase
  $25,000, max age 90 in specimen) [S3]; decreases restricted (none year 1, one
  per year, floor) [S2][S3].

### 3. Premium structure and tax-qualification limits
- Premiums flexible; planned premium is billing target only, no guarantee of
  coverage [S3]. Minimum premium remittance $50 (specimen) [S3].
- Premium expense load: Symetra current 6% all years, guaranteed max 9% [S1];
  Protective 10% all years [S2]; Pacific Life guaranteed max 6.95% (current may
  be lower, uniform by class) [S3].
- 7702 qualification: GPT or CVAT elected at issue; election irrevocable [S3].
  GPT limit = max(GSP, cumulative GLP to earlier of payment/age 100) [S3][R2];
  corridor factors 250% (ages 0-40) grading to 105% (75-90) and ~101% above 93
  [S3][R2]; CVAT floor 101% of AV in the specimen implementation [S3].
  Specimen guideline premiums for M35 NS $100k + riders: GSP $34,138.15, GLP
  $2,825.52 [S3].
- 7702A MEC 7-pay test; specimen 7-pay premium $6,702.10 [S3]; statutory test
  and material-change rules [R3]. Insurer refuses/refunds MEC-causing premiums
  absent owner election [S3].
- Post-2020 issues use the dynamic §7702 insurance interest rate (2% during the
  2021 transition; replaced the fixed statutory 4%/6% basis) [R2].

### 4. Charge structure (summary table)

### 5. Interest crediting
- Guaranteed minimum: 2% (Symetra, 2014-era form still sold) [S1]; 2.5%
  (Protective, 2015-era) [S2]; 3% years 2+ (Pacific Life 2008-era form) [S3].
- Crediting style varies: new-money (each net premium earns its declared rate
  locked for 12 months from receipt — Symetra) [S1] vs portfolio (current rate
  declared periodically — Protective, Pacific Life) [S2][S3].
- Daily crediting on a 365-day year (specimen) [S3]. Model 585 requires the
  policy to state guaranteed minimum interest and forbids conditional interest
  credits beyond 24 months [R1].
- Loaned AV may earn a different (guaranteed) rate: Protective credits 2.5% on
  the loaned portion [S2].

### 6. Death benefit options and corridor
- Option A (level): DB = face amount; Option B (increasing): DB = face + AV;
  Option C (return of premium): DB = face + premiums − withdrawals (Symetra
  caps C at 2x initial face) [S1][S3].
- DB = max(option amount, corridor minimum under elected 7702 test) [S3].
- Option changes limited (once/year, to A or B only; face adjusted to keep DB
  level at change) [S3].
- Death benefit proceeds = DB − policy debt − due/unpaid monthly deductions in
  grace; interest paid on proceeds from date of death [S3].

### 7. Surrender, withdrawals, loans
- CSV = AV − surrender charge; NCSV = CSV − policy debt [S3][R1 definitions].
- Surrender charge patterns: 9-year decreasing schedule keyed to per-$1,000
  rates by sex/class/issue age [S1][S2]; or dollar-amount schedule amortizing
  monthly to zero after year 10, per coverage layer [S3]. Nonforfeiture floor:
  retrospective minimum CSV per Model 585 Sec. 6A (expense-allowance
  amortization) [R1].
- Withdrawals: from first anniversary; min $200; fee $25; no surrender charge
  on withdrawal; Option A face-reduction rule with annual free-withdrawal
  carve-out (lesser of $10,000 or 10% of NCSV, first withdrawal each year,
  first 15 years) [S3]; $25 fee (Protective) and face cannot drop below
  minimum [S2].
- Loans: fixed-rate design — accrual up to 3.25%, loaned AV credited at
  guaranteed rate; max loan = AV − 3 monthly deductions − surrender charge −
  debt [S3]; 5% in arrears with 2.5% credited on loaned value (200bp
  guaranteed spread) [S2]; variable-rate design — Moody's Corporate Bond Yield
  Average-based, floor 3% [S1]. Loan repayment priority: payments applied to
  loans first unless designated premium [S3].

### 8. Lapse, grace, no-lapse guarantees, reinstatement
- Lapse trigger: AV (or cash value) less policy debt insufficient to cover the
  current monthly deduction [S2][S3]; Model 585 default: lapse when NCSV first
  equals zero, grace >= 30 days, 30-day advance written notice [R1]. Both
  fetched products use a 61-day grace period [S2][S3]; required grace premium
  >= 3 monthly deductions + load [S3].
- No-lapse guarantees on current-assumption UL are typically short (Symetra: 5
  years on minimum premium) [S1] or optional/flexible shadow-account
  endorsements (Protective "Lapse Protection Endorsement" with catch-up
  provision) [S2]; dedicated GUL products guarantee to attained age 120 (or a
  chosen age, e.g., 70 with later extension right) — Nationwide NLG UL II
  [S4].
- Reinstatement: within 5 years of lapse, evidence of insurability + premium to
  cover grace shortfall and 3 months forward; COI schedule resumes as if no
  lapse [S3].

### 9. Maturity / age-121 mechanics
- Modern forms have no maturity date: coverage continues for life; at attained
  age 121 monthly deductions and premiums stop, interest continues, loans
  allowed, withdrawals not allowed [S3]; premiums/charges discontinue at 121
  [S2]; Symetra: lifetime = age 120 in most states [S1]. Guaranteed max COI
  rates grade to 1000/12 per month just before the end date (net single-month
  cost of full NAR) and to zero after [S3]. IRS guidance on post-age-100
  coverage was noted as unsettled in the specimen [S3].

### 10. Riders (typical set)
- Chronic illness / LTC acceleration: 50-100% of DB, lien or acceleration
  design, IRC 101(g) qualification [S1]; ExtendCare chronic illness rider
  [S2]; LTC Rider II cash indemnity 2%/3%/4% monthly up to 2x HIPAA per diem
  [S4].
- Terminal illness acceleration: 75% of DB up to $500k [S1]; 60% up to $1M
  [S2].
- Accidental death benefit [S1][S2]; children's term [S2]; disability benefit
  (monthly credit to policy) [S2]; additional/annual renewable term riders
  incl. additional-insured term [S1 form list][S3]; surrender value
  enhancement rider for trust/executive benefit market (higher early CSV,
  charge offset) [S3]; charitable giving benefit +1% of face [S1]; return of
  premium windows at years 16/21 on GUL [S4]; income provider option
  (installment DB payout) [S2].

## Variations across insurers

1. Crediting style: new-money 12-month rate locks per premium (Symetra CAUL
   [S1]) vs periodically declared portfolio rate (Protective [S2], Pacific
   Life [S3]). Portfolio-style declared rates are the more common design and
   the simpler modeling default [unverified as to market share].
2. Guaranteed minimum interest: 2%-3% among the fetched forms, correlated with
   issue era (3% on the 2008 form [S3], 2.5% on the 2015 form [S2], 2% on the
   2014/2023 form [S1]). New issues cluster at 2% or below following the 2021
   §7702 rate change [R2][unverified for the market generally].
3. Premium loads 6%-10%; some insurers keep a gap between current and
   guaranteed load (6%/9% [S1]) — i.e., the load itself is an NGE — others
   state a single rate [S2][S3].
4. Per-policy charges $5-$10/month current ($30 guaranteed max seen [S1]);
   per-unit expense charges universal but bases differ: initial face [S2] vs
   coverage-layer face with a step-down after year 10 [S3]; duration-varying
   [S1].
5. Surrender charges: 9-10 year runoff is standard; expression differs (rate
   per $1,000 by sex/class/age [S1][S2] vs fixed dollar amount with linear
   monthly amortization [S3]).
6. COI structure: all use monthly rate per $1,000 NAR with guaranteed maximums
   (CSO-capped) and lower current scales; rates vary by issue age/sex/class/
   duration (select & ultimate by policy year) [S1][S2][S3].
7. Death benefit options: A and B universal; a return-of-premium Option C
   offered by some (Symetra, Pacific Life) [S1][S3].
8. Loans: fixed-rate with guaranteed spread (0.75% spread in [S3]; 2.5% spread
   in [S2]) vs variable Moody's-indexed rate with floor [S1].
9. Lapse protection: short built-in NLG (5 years [S1]) vs optional
   flexible-duration shadow-account endorsement [S2] vs full GUL products
   with lifetime secondary guarantees and ROP windows [S4]. The market
   separates "current assumption" (interest-sensitive, cash-value oriented)
   from "lifetime guarantee" product focuses; each is ~27% of UL exposure,
   with cash-accumulation at 33% [R7].
10. Representative design for a reference model: flexible premium UL with
    portfolio crediting (guaranteed min 2-2.5%), premium load 6-10%, monthly
    per-policy charge $5-10, per-unit charge per $1,000 face for a limited or
    full duration, monthly COI on NAR with select-by-duration current rates
    capped at CSO-based guarantees, 9-10 year declining surrender charge,
    DB options A/B with GPT election and corridor factors, 61-day grace,
    charges ceasing at age 121 with coverage continuing, fixed-rate loans with
    a modest guaranteed spread, 10%-of-value free partial withdrawals with a
    $25 fee. Every element of this composite is present in at least one
    fetched document ([S1][S2][S3]); the composite itself is a synthesis.
