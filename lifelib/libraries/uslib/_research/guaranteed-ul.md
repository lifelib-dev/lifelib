## Regulatory and actuarial references

### R1 — NAIC Actuarial Guideline XXXVIII (AG 38), "The Application of the Valuation of Life Insurance Policies Model Regulation" (full text, as revised 2012)
- Publisher: National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC)
- URL fetched: https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/inline-files/cipr_ag38_121212.pdf
- Retrieved: YES (13-page PDF, full text extracted)
- Facts extracted:
  - Purpose: directs how the Valuation of Life Insurance Policies Model Regulation (Model #830, "Regulation XXX," adopted March 1999) applies to product designs with embedded guarantees; "policy designs which are created to simply disguise those guarantees or exploit a perceived loophole must be reserved in a manner similar to more typical designs with similar guarantees." [R1]
  - Catch-up provisions (Guideline example 7): a UL secondary guarantee with an unlimited right to make up past premium deficiencies constitutes an "unexpired secondary guarantee"; basic and deficiency reserves are computed as if the stipulated premium requirement had been met, then reduced (pro-rata between basic and deficiency components) by the catch-up amount required at the valuation date, floored at zero. [R1]
  - Section 8A (policies issued before 7/1/2005): 9-step method — derive minimum gross premiums satisfying the secondary guarantee at issue; treat them as "specified premiums" under Model 830 7B/7C; measure prefunding (shadow account amount, or excess cumulative premiums including interest credited at the secondary-guarantee rate); compute the ratio of prefunding to the single payment needed to fully fund the remaining guarantee; blend the net single premium for the guarantee with basic+deficiency reserves via that ratio; reduce by surrender charges (account value less cash surrender value). [R1]
  - Section 8B (issued 7/1/2005–12/31/2006): same structure but on a segmented basis and with the fully-funded denominator divided by one minus a 7% premium load allowance (0.93); funding ratio capped at 1; the 7% load "approximates an average premium load level as evidenced by policies currently sold in the market." [R1]
  - Section 8C (issued 1/1/2007–12/31/2012): adds prescribed maximum lapse rates for the reserve calculation — no more than 2%/yr for the first 5 years, then no more than 1%/yr to a policy anniversary that depends on issue age (issue 0–50: 30th anniversary; 51–60: anniversary at age 80; 61–70: 20th anniversary; 71–89: anniversary at age 90; 90+: no lapse), 0% thereafter; net single premium may use the same lapse criteria; surrender charge offset multiplied by ratio of net level premium for the SG period to net level premium for whole life; business reserved under 8C must be supported by dedicated asset adequacy analysis. [R1]
  - Section 8D (in force 12/31/2012, issued 7/1/2005–12/31/2012): addresses designs with multiple sets of interest credits or charges entering the secondary guarantee calculation; applies if the company's in-force ULSG face subject to 8D exceeds 2% of individual permanent face or $1 billion; primary methodology holds the 12/31/2011-basis reserve plus any excess of a deterministic-reserve-style amount computed per the (August 2012) draft Valuation Manual with prescribed caps on net reinvestment earnings (lesser of 12-month average Moody's composite corporate bond yield and 7%); alternative methodology based on the November 1, 2011 LATF statement; stand-alone actuarial memorandum, FAWG review, and confidential reporting requirements. [R1]
  - Section 8E (issued on/after 1/1/2013): Method I defines minimum gross premiums for standard designs — Policy Design #1 (shadow account with a single set of charges/credits): the minimum gross premium each year is the premium that takes a zero shadow account at the start of the year to zero at year-end using guaranteed shadow-account charges and credits (interest, mortality charges, premium loads, expense charges); Policy Design #2 (cumulative premium test): premium keeping paid accumulated premiums equal to required accumulated premiums; Policy Design #3 (multiple sets of charges/credits): use the set producing the lowest premiums, ignoring conditions limiting their application. Guaranteed shadow-account credits are capped at an Index (Moody's composite yield on seasoned corporate bonds, spot or 12-month average, elected at filing) plus 3% per annum. Method II (other designs): minimum gross premiums are the lowest premium schedule keeping the policy in force over the SG period that produces the greatest deficiency reserve at issue, with X factors set to 1, tested over level, increasing and combination premium patterns, plus a good-faith analytical review of likely premium patterns. Reserve steps then follow the 8B/8C funding-ratio structure with the ratio floored at −1 and capped at 1, a reserve floor, and required Actuarial Opinion plus senior-officer Company Representation for Method I products. [R1]
- History note: AG 38 is commonly called "AXXX" as the UL-secondary-guarantee analogue of Regulation XXX for term insurance; the 2005–2012 amendments responded to increasingly complex shadow account designs, and the 2012 changes (8D/8E) came out of the NAIC/industry dispute over aggressive minimum-premium interpretations. ### R2 — NAIC Valuation Manual, Jan. 1, 2026 edition (VM-01 definitions; VM-20 Requirements for Principle-Based Reserves for Life Products)
- Publisher: NAIC
- URL fetched: https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/pbr_data_valuation_manual_current_edition.pdf (457 pages)
- Retrieved: YES (PDF fetched; ULSG-relevant sections extracted)
- Facts extracted:
  - VM-01 definition: a "secondary guarantee" is "a conditional guarantee that a policy will remain in force, even if its fund value is exhausted," for more than five years, or for five years or less if the specified premium for the SG period is less than the net level reserve premium for that period on the applicable CSO basis, or if the initial surrender charge is less than 100% of the first-year annualized specified premium. [R2]
  - VM-01: a "shadow account" is "a notional account, typically consisting of premium and interest credits and cost of insurance and expense charges, that is associated with certain types of UL policies and is used in conjunction with a secondary guarantee." [R2]
  - VM-01 "non-material secondary guarantee": only one SG, in required-premium (specified annual or cumulative premium) form; SG duration no longer than 20 years from issue through issue age 60, grading down by 2/3 year per age to age 82, five years thereafter; PV of required premium at least as great as PV of net premiums from the appropriate Valuation Basic Table over the maximum SG duration. [R2]
  - Life PBR exemption: companies may not file the exemption for ULSG policies (or policies with SG riders) whose secondary guarantee does not meet the non-material SG definition. [R2]
  - VM-20 reserve = net premium reserve (NPR) floor plus excesses of deterministic reserve (DR) and stochastic reserve (SR) computed by reserving category; ULSG is its own reserving category; a group of ULSG meeting the non-material SG definition and passing both exclusion tests may hold only NPR. [R2]
  - ULSG NPR (VM-20 Section 3.B.5): during the SG period the NPR is the greater of (c) a reserve computed assuming the secondary guarantee is in effect and (d) a reserve ignoring the SG, subject to Section 3.D.2 floors (after SG expiry, only (d)):
    - At issue: determine the level gross premium that keeps the policy in force to the end of the SG period n under the SG provisions; determine a valuation net premium ratio equating PV net premiums to PV benefits over n; expense allowance components x1 = first-year expense equal to the level gross premium, y2–5 = 10% of the level gross premium in years 2–5, z1 = $2.50 per $1,000 of insurance issued, amortized over the premium-paying span of the SG period. [R2]
    - At each valuation date: compute the actual secondary guarantee ASG (actual shadow account value, or actual accumulated premiums for cumulative designs) and the fully funded secondary guarantee FFSG (minimum shadow account value, or cumulative premium level, that would fully fund the guarantee); NPR = min(ASG/FFSG, 1) x NSP − E, where NSP is the net single premium for the SG coverage to attained age x+n on prescribed interest, mortality and lapse assumptions. [R2]
    - Prescribed lapse for the SG-based NPR: ratio R = (FFSG − ASG)/(FFSG − LSG) bounded in [0,1], where LSG is the "level secondary guarantee" (shadow account value that would exist had the level gross premium been paid); annual lapse rate L = R x 1% + (1 − R) x 0.5% x r, held level for the projection. A brand-new policy (ASG = 0) uses 1% lapse. [R2]
    - "Multiple secondary guarantees" definition and anti-abuse guidance note: sequential SGs strung together are treated as a single SG; designs disguising guarantees must be treated like typical designs with similar guarantees. [R2]
  - ULSG exclusion-test treatment: ULSG with a material secondary guarantee is not permitted to use the stochastic exclusion in some paths — specifically the manual requires the SR (or the SET) treatment for the ULSG category, with the SET certification/demonstration path renewed at least every third year. [R2]
  - VM-20 also defines the deterministic reserve on a gross-premium-valuation basis and the stochastic reserve at CTE 70 over prescribed scenarios. [unverified — standard VM-20 structure; the ULSG-specific items above are [R2]]

## Extracted specifications

### Product architecture and guarantee mechanics
- ULSG is flexible-premium universal life with the usual UL account-value mechanics (premium loads, monthly COI/expense deductions, interest crediting) plus a secondary guarantee: a conditional guarantee that the policy stays in force even if the account/fund value is exhausted [R2 definition; S2, S4, S9 product statements].
- Two canonical secondary-guarantee designs, both recognized by regulation:
  - Shadow account: a notional account with its own premium credits, interest credits, and COI/expense charges; the policy cannot lapse while the shadow (guarantee) value is positive [R2 VM-01; R1 8E Design #1]. Product examples: Nationwide's "extended no-lapse guaranteed value" that must stay > 0 net of indebtedness [S4]; Pacific Life's "net no-lapse guarantee value" that must be "sufficient to cover monthly policy charges," with the guarantee terminating if it hits zero [S2, S9].
  - Cumulative premium test: policy stays in force if cumulative premiums paid (less withdrawals/indebtedness) meet a required accumulated-premium schedule [R1 8E Design #2; R2]. Product examples: Nationwide's initial (first-5-years) no-lapse guarantee [S4]; Penn Mutual Protection Guard's requirement that premiums paid less withdrawals exceed required no-lapse premiums [S5].
- Guarantee duration election: the owner selects the guarantee horizon at issue and effectively "buys" duration through funding level:
  - Pacific Life: base guarantee to age 90 included; extendable to any duration up to lifetime/age 121 via higher premiums [S2, S9].
  - Nationwide: elect attained-age-120 (issue 18–85) or attained-age-70 (issue 18–60) horizon at issue; both customizable; the age-70 election can be upgraded to age-120 without underwriting within limits (after 3rd anniversary, before earlier of age 65 / end of year 15) [S4].
  - Protective (Lifetime Assurance UL): no-lapse guarantee to age 90, 95, 100, 105, 110, or lifetime (121); minimum coverage length 20 years [S7, S8].
  - Corebridge: owner chooses "the number of years or age" of the guarantee [S1]; the launch announcement described a default to-age-105 guarantee [unverified — press-release snippet not fetched].
- Late/missed premiums: because the guarantee measure accumulates with interest, timing matters. Pacific Life explicitly allows premiums up to one month early or late without harming the guarantee [S9]. Protective's design degrades gracefully — a missed premium shortens the guarantee age rather than voiding the guarantee (example: guarantee to 105 falls to 96 after skipping premiums in years 11 and 21) [S3]. Nationwide's initial NLG is a strict cumulative test [S4].
- Catch-up provisions: Protective states lapse protection, once lost, "may be restored by paying additional or higher premiums," possibly significantly higher than illustrated [S7]; Pacific Life states additional premiums are required to resume a terminated no-lapse feature [S2]; Penn Mutual markets skip-and-catch-up flexibility within the premium test [S5]. AG 38 example 7 prescribes reserve treatment for unlimited catch-up rights (treated as unexpired secondary guarantees) [R1].
- Effect of loans/withdrawals on the guarantee:
  - Penn Mutual: any policy loan nullifies the no-lapse guarantee; withdrawals reduce the premium-test measure [S5].
  - Nationwide: indebtedness directly reduces both the initial NLG premium test and the extended NLG value; partial surrenders and fees also subtract [S4].
  - Pacific Life / Protective: loans and withdrawals reduce/impair the guarantee value or its duration (and withdrawals within the surrender-charge period also trigger surrender charges at Pacific Life) [S2, S3, S7].
- Thin cash value: guaranteed crediting is low (2.0% at Protective and Penn Mutual [S3, S5, S7]) and charges are high (25% premium load at Protective [S3, S7]); carriers state outright that a policy sustained solely by the no-lapse guarantee will not build cash value [S2] and that "cash value accumulation is unlikely" [S7]. Model 585 gives the commissioner authority to require higher cash values where substantially level benefit charges develop low or no cash values [R3].

### Issue ages and underwriting
- Pacific Life PL Promise GUL: 0–80 age nearest birthday [S2].
- Corebridge Secure Lifetime GUL 3: 18–80 age nearest birthday; standard through Table C [S6].
- Protective Advantage Choice UL / Lifetime Assurance UL: Select Preferred 18–75; Preferred/Non-Tobacco/Tobacco 18–85 [S3, S7].
- Nationwide NLG UL II: 18–80 for preferred-type classes, 18–85 for standard classes (age nearest birthday); four nontobacco and two tobacco classes [S4].
- Penn Mutual Protection Guard UL: base policy issue ages not stated in the brochure; chronic illness rider 20–85, disability riders 0–55 [S5].

### Face amounts and banding
- Minimum face: $25,000 (Pacific Life) [S2]; $50,000 tobacco/non-tobacco and $100,000 preferred classes (Protective, both products) [S3, S7]; $100,000 (Nationwide; Corebridge) [S4, S6].
- Banding (used for per-unit charges/rates): Pacific Life and Protective use five bands with identical breakpoints — $25K or $50K to $99,999 / $100K–$249,999 / $250K–$499,999 / $500K–$999,999 / $1M+ [S2, S7].
- No maximum face (Protective) [S3].

### Death benefit options
- Level-only (Option 1 / DB = face amount; DB = greater of spec amount and minimum required DB for corridor): Pacific Life, Nationwide — increasing option explicitly unavailable [S2, S4].
- Level and increasing options with post-issue switches (once a year after year 1, no evidence): Penn Mutual [S5].
- Face decreases permitted subject to minimums: once/year after year 1, at least $1,000 (Pacific Life) [S2]; after 3 years with a partial-surrender charge (Protective LAUL) [S7]; DB reduction preserving the original guarantee period with proportionate reduction of values and premiums (Corebridge) [S1]. Face increases generally not allowed (new policy required) [S2].

### Premium structure and premium guarantees
- Flexible premium chassis, but sold and illustrated with a level "no-lapse guarantee premium" solved for the elected guarantee duration; paying more shortens the payment period or extends the guarantee; single-pay/1035 funding is a design "sweet spot" [S1, S2].
- Modal loadings: non-annual modes carry modal factors (cost more); monthly requires EFT (Pacific Life) [S2].
- Guaranteed premium concept: Corebridge markets "guaranteed premium that fits your budget" — level guaranteed premiums for the elected guarantee [S1]; Protective LAUL is described as "level-pay UL ... predictable premium payments" [S7].
- Rolling target compensation: 2-year rolling target (Nationwide) [S4].

### Charge structure (from retrieved documents)
- Premium load: 25% of every premium, all years (Protective ACUL and LAUL — unusually transparent disclosure) [S3, S7]; "premium expense charge deducted from each premium payment" (Pacific Life) [S2, S9]; "premium charge (including sales charges)" (Nationwide) [S4].
- Per-policy charge: $5.50/month (Protective, both products), ceasing at age 121 [S3, S7]; "monthly administrative fee" (Nationwide, amount not public) [S4].
- Per-unit charge: monthly expense charge per $1,000 of initial face amount, varying by age/gender/class (Protective; in CA also by duration) [S3, S7]; "monthly per-thousand charge" (Nationwide) [S4]; "coverage charges" (Pacific Life) [S9].
- COI: monthly, varying by issue age, gender, underwriting class, policy year and ratings, ceasing at 121 (Protective) [S3, S7]; equivalents at Pacific Life and Nationwide [S2, S4].
- Rider charges: monthly, where riders are elected (e.g., PL waiver rider, chronic illness rider) [S2].
- Actual COI rate tables and shadow-account charge/credit schedules are not published in any retrieved public document — see Gaps.

### Interest crediting
- Guaranteed minimum crediting rate on the (real) account value: 2.0% (Protective ACUL/LAUL; Penn Mutual) [S3, S5, S7]; current rates discretionary, set periodically/annually [S3, S5].
- Loaned values: credited 3.0% guaranteed (Nationwide) [S4]; 2.0% guaranteed (Protective LAUL) [S7].
- Shadow-account credited rates are separate, internal parameters (not disclosed in consumer materials) [R2 definition; S4 mechanics]; AG 38 8E caps guaranteed shadow-account credits at Moody's composite corporate yield + 3% for reserve-classification purposes [R1].
- Model 585 requires the policy to state guaranteed minimum interest and maximum charges, and forbids interest credits remaining conditional for longer than 24 months [R3].

### Surrender charges and return-of-premium features
- Surrender charge periods vary widely: none (Protective LAUL) [S7]; 9 years (Protective ACUL) [S3]; 19 years, also applied to face decreases and withdrawals (Pacific Life) [S2]; 20 years declining to 0 (Nationwide) [S4]. Charges vary by age/gender/class where present [S3].
- Return of premium (mid-duration exit) features are now standard in the GUL segment:
  - Corebridge: built-in — year 20: 50% of premiums paid; year 25: 100%, capped at 40% of face [S1].
  - Pacific Life ESVR: ages 30+: year 15: 50/75%, year 20: 75/100%, year 25: 100% (by face band); ages 0–29: years 25/35; cap 40% of death benefit; 60-day window; requires guarantee to at least age 100 and $50K+ face [S2]; consumer version quotes 75% at year 15 and 100% at years 20/25 for ages 30+/$250K+ [S9].
  - Nationwide PAMSVR: year 16: 50%, year 21: 100%; cap = lesser of 40% of spec amount or % of adjusted premiums; not available with age-70 guarantee [S4].
  - Protective ACUL: year 21: 50%, year 26: 100%; cap 50% of lowest death benefit; NT classes only, ages 18–70, table <= 4, flat extra <= $5/M [S3]. Protective LAUL: simpler — 25% of premiums any time on/after 10th anniversary, same class/table limits, cap 50% of lowest DB [S7].
- Withdrawal/partial surrender fee: $25 across Pacific Life, Nationwide, Protective [S2, S4, S7]; Nationwide minimum partial surrender $500, maximum tied to net surrender value less three months' deductions [S4].

### Loans
- Availability: after policy year 1 (Nationwide, Protective, Pacific Life implied) [S3, S4, S7].
- Fixed-rate designs: 5.0% charged in arrears with 3.0% credited on loaned values, both guaranteed (Nationwide — 200 bps guaranteed spread) [S4]; 5% charged / 2.0% credited (Protective LAUL — 300 bps spread) [S7]; 5% in arrears (Protective ACUL) [S3].
- Variable-rate design: adjustable loan rate declared each January 1 (Penn Mutual) [S5].
- Maximum loan: net surrender value less three months of deductions (Nationwide) [S4].
- Guarantee interaction: loans reduce the guarantee value (Nationwide, Pacific Life, Protective) or void the guarantee entirely (Penn Mutual) [S4, S2, S7, S5].

### Riders (common ULSG lineup, all from retrieved documents)
- Terminal illness accelerated death benefit: up to 75% of DB, max $500,000, 12-month prognosis (Pacific Life) [S2, S9]; up to 60% of DB or $1 million (Protective LAUL) [S7]; offered by Nationwide and Penn Mutual [S4, S5].
- Chronic illness accelerated benefit: 2-of-6 ADLs or severe cognitive impairment as trigger; per-diem-capped payments (Pacific Life, $3M advance cap in client guide) [S2, S9]; Corebridge Accelerated Access Solution (monthly payments until DB exhausted) [S1]; Penn Mutual CIAB automatically included, 90-day condition duration [S5]; Protective ExtendCare [S7]; Nationwide offers a true LTC Rider II (long-term care, including informal care) [S4].
- Longevity/income riders: Corebridge Lifestyle Income Solution (DB converted to income from age 85) [S1]; Nationwide Income Provider-style owner-directed settlement options / Protective Income Provider Option endorsement (guaranteed installment payout of DB) [S4, S7].
- Disability riders: waiver of monthly deductions (issue ages 20–59 PacLife, to age 65 benefit; 0–55 Penn Mutual with 4-month wait; Nationwide pays monthly deductions but not the full no-lapse premium — an important guarantee gap) [S2, S5, S4]; waiver of specified premium (Protective LAUL, 6-month wait) [S7]; Penn Mutual Disability Completion Benefit (waives all charges and credits premium to CV to age 70) [S5].
- Children's term riders: $10,000/child typical with conversion privileges (PacLife: 5x up to $50K at child age 25; Penn Mutual: conversion at 23) [S2, S9, S5].
- Accidental death benefit; additional insured term; guaranteed increase option; overloan protection; supplemental (business) exchange (Penn Mutual list) [S5, S4, S6].

### Maturity / age-121 provisions
- Nationwide: premiums to attained age 120, maturity at 120 with maturity extension provision [S4].
- Protective LAUL: no stated maturity age; premiums and all charges cease at attained age 121 (de facto paid-up continuation) [S7].
- Protective ACUL: charges discontinue at age 121 [S3].
- Guarantees quoted "to age 121" are treated as lifetime [S2, S7, S8]. Model 585 requires disclosure that coverage may not continue to the stated maturity date even if scheduled premiums are paid [R3].

### Tax framework (IRC 7702 / 7702A)
- Policies must qualify under IRC 7702 via CVAT or GPT+corridor; GUL death-benefit-only designs are typically GPT/corridor-tested [R4 for tests; design attribution unverified].
- Post-2020 dynamic 7702 interest rates (2% transition rate in 2021) lowered funding-limit floors, relevant to current GUL pricing [R4].
- Heavy prefunding of the guarantee (single-pay/short-pay) can create a MEC under the 7702A 7-pay test, making loans/withdrawals income-first taxable with a 10% additional tax before 59 1/2; benefit reductions within 7 years force retesting at the reduced level [R5, S4].
- Accelerated benefit riders are designed to qualify under IRC 101(g) [S1, S2].

### Statutory valuation of ULSG (modeling-relevant summary)
- Base UL valuation: Model 585 CRVM-for-UL using guaranteed maturity premium/fund with the r-ratio; minimum nonforfeiture values via retrospective accumulation [R3].
- Secondary guarantees, policies issued before 2017 (or in states pre-PBR): AG 38 applies on top of Model 830; the reserve interpolates between the basic+deficiency reserve for minimum-premium funding and the net single premium for the guarantee, using the funding ratio (shadow account or excess cumulative premium over the fully-funding level, with 7% load allowance), less an adjusted surrender-charge offset; prescribed conservative lapse (2%/1%/0% pattern by issue age band) [R1].
- AG 38 8E (2013+ issues) is the direct ancestor of the VM-20 ULSG NPR: Method I minimum-premium definitions per design type, shadow-account credit caps of Moody's + 3%, Method II greatest-deficiency-reserve search over premium patterns [R1].
- VM-20 (post-2017 PBR): reserve = NPR + max(0, max(DR, SR) − NPR-ish floor per category); ULSG NPR during the SG period = greater of SG-based amount [funding ratio x NSP − amortized expense allowance (x1 = level gross premium, y2–5 = 10% of it, z1 = $2.50/M)] and the no-SG amount; dynamic prescribed lapse L = R x 1% + (1 − R) x 0.5% x r driven by the funding ratio R between fully-funded, actual, and level-funded guarantee values [R2].
- ULSG with a material SG cannot use the life PBR exemption and generally cannot avoid deterministic/stochastic modeling; only "non-material" SG business (single required-premium SG, <= 20-year duration grading down, adequately-priced required premium) can be held at NPR-only after passing both exclusion tests [R2, R9].
- Reserve financing of ULSG "redundant" reserves through captives is constrained by Model 787: Primary Security must at least equal a VM-20-based Required Level (greater of DR and NPR, or greatest of DR/SR/NPR if the stochastic exclusion fails) [R6].
- Experience for assumptions: lifetime-guarantee ULSG lapse rates run 45% below non-lifetime SG lapse rates (2015–2021 experience) [R7]; in company tail modeling, a median 40% of ULSG policies are assumed kept in force purely by the guarantee after 31 years, 63% of companies use dynamic lapse, and lapse plus tail investment returns are rated the most critical assumptions [R8].
- Actuarial practice standards: ASOP 52 governs PBR work (assumption setting with margins, model validation, sensitivity testing, documentation) [R10]. ASOP No. 2 (nonguaranteed elements) also applies to UL current-rate/current-charge management. [unverified — ASOP 2 not fetched]

## Variations across insurers

1. Guarantee mechanism.
   - Pure shadow-account designs dominate flagship lifetime-GUL products: Nationwide's extended no-lapse guaranteed value [S4], Pacific Life's net no-lapse guarantee value [S2, S9]; Corebridge's continuation guarantee is of the same family (its detailed internal mechanics were not retrievable from public documents — see Gaps) [S1, S6].
   - Cumulative-premium-test designs persist in two places: as a simple initial guarantee layered under a shadow account (Nationwide's 5-year initial NLG) [S4], and as the whole design for shorter/limited guarantees (Penn Mutual Protection Guard, guarantee up to 30 years) [S5].
   - Regulation recognizes exactly these two families (VM-01 shadow account definition; AG 38 8E Policy Designs #1/#2) and treats multi-charge-set variants punitively (AG 38 Design #3, VM-20 multiple-SG rules) [R1, R2].
2. Guarantee duration menu. Modern products let the client dial duration continuously through funding (PacLife: any duration from age 90 to 121 [S2]; Corebridge: any years/age [S1]), or from a discrete menu (Protective LAUL: 90/95/100/105/110/121 [S8]; Nationwide: age-120 or age-70 chassis, customizable [S4]). Penn Mutual's design caps the guarantee at 30 years — a "guard rail" design rather than lifetime GUL [S5].
3. Loans vs the guarantee. The harshest treatment is Penn Mutual (any loan nullifies the guarantee) [S5]; mainstream treatment deducts indebtedness from the guarantee value (Nationwide, PacLife) [S4, S2]. This distinction materially changes liability modeling of loan utilization.
4. Surrender charges. Full range observed: none (Protective LAUL, offset by only a 25%-of-premium ROP) [S7], 9 years (Protective ACUL) [S3], 19 years incl. withdrawals/face decreases (PacLife) [S2], 20 years (Nationwide) [S4].
5. Return-of-premium exit windows. Nearly universal in the segment but with different anniversaries (15/20/25 PacLife; 16/21 Nationwide; 20/25 Corebridge; 21/26 Protective ACUL; any time after 10 for LAUL's 25%) and caps (40% of face/DB at PacLife/Nationwide/Corebridge vs 50% of lowest DB at Protective) [S1–S4, S7, S9].
6. Death benefit options. Guarantee-focused carriers restrict to level-only (PacLife, Nationwide) [S2, S4]; products marketed with accumulation flexibility keep level+increasing (Penn Mutual) [S5].
7. Charges transparency. Protective publishes its load structure (25% premium load, $5.50/month, per-mille expense charge) [S3, S7]; others disclose only charge categories [S2, S4]. No carrier publishes COI tables or shadow-account parameters.
8. Representative design for a reference implementation: a flexible-premium UL with (a) level death benefit option only, (b) a single shadow account with guaranteed premium loads, expense charges, COI rates and credited rates distinct from the base account, (c) owner-elected guarantee duration to age 90–121 funded by a solved level "no-lapse premium," (d) indebtedness deducted from the guarantee value, (e) 15–20-year surrender charge schedule, (f) ROP exit windows near years 15–25 capped at 40–50% of face, and (g) catch-up permitted (guarantee restorable with interest-accumulated shortfall). This matches AG 38 8E Policy Design #1 and the VM-20 ULSG NPR machinery directly [S2, S4, S7, R1, R2 — synthesis; the synthesis itself is my judgment].
