# Appendix A-830 — Valuation of Life Insurance Policies (Model #830, "Regulation XXX") — primary-source extraction from the AP&P Manual (As of March 2026)

## Source

**Exact printed title.** The appendix opens with a three-line title block, reproduced here line for line:

> Appendix A-830
> Valuation of Life Insurance Policies
> (Including the Introduction and Use of New Select Mortality Factors)

The running header on odd pages is "A-830  Appendix A" and on even pages "Valuation of Life Insurance Policies  A-830". The words
"Model #830" and "Regulation XXX" **do not appear anywhere in the appendix** — the manual prints the instrument under its own
appendix designation only. The one place the underlying instrument is named is in the applicability paragraph, which refers to
"the Valuation of Life Insurance Policies Model Regulation" (PDF p. 1206).

**Header block, as printed** (PDF p. 1206). Immediately under the title the manual prints two cross-reference lists:

- *Relevant NAIC Model Laws/Regulations:* Standard Valuation Law (#820); Actuarial Opinion and Memorandum Regulation (#822).

Note what is **not** in that list: Model #830 itself, the Standard Nonforfeiture Law, and AG 38. The manual positions A-830 as an
adjunct to the Standard Valuation Law and the AOMR.

**Where it sits.** NAIC *Accounting Practices and Procedures Manual*, **As of March 2026** — Appendix A ("Excerpts of NAIC Model
Laws"), item **A-830**. PDF pages **1206–1232** of the 2,117-page volume; the appendix's own internal pagination runs **A830-1**

**Edition line.** The manual prints a copyright/edition footer at the foot of every page of this appendix:
`© 1999-2026 National Association of Insurance Commissioners`. There is no separate revision-history or adoption-history note

**Retrieval note.** Free download from content.naic.org, retrieved and read **2026-08-06**. The full manual (2,117 pages) was

**Reference id.** This document is **R154** in the shared U.S. numbering
(`references/regulatory-and-actuarial-references.md`). Cite as **[REG-R154 ¶N]** using the appendix's own paragraph numbers,

**Structural point that affects every citation to this text.** The appendix is numbered as a **flat sequence of paragraphs 1–32**

## Scope and applicability

### The applicability sentence, verbatim

> "This appendix shall apply to all life insurance policies, with or without nonforfeiture values, issued on or after the
> effective date of this appendix, subject to the following exceptions and conditions." (¶3, PDF p. 1206)

followed immediately by an anti-expansion clause:

> "Nothing in this section shall be construed to expand the applicability of the Valuation of Life Insurance Policies Model
> Regulation to include life insurance policies exempted under this section." (¶3, PDF p. 1206)

Two things follow that matter to the library. First, the printed scope is **all life insurance policies**, not "term and ULSG" —
term and ULSG are simply the two designs the exceptions and conditions do not carve out and the calculation paragraphs are built
around. Second, ¶2 makes the appendix's own construction the statutory method for anything it reaches:

> "The method for calculating basic reserves defined in this appendix will constitute the Commissioners' Reserve Valuation
> Method for policies to which this appendix is applicable." (¶2, PDF p. 1206)

### Effective dates — read this before recording any date

**The appendix nowhere prints a calendar effective date for itself.** Every applicability sentence uses the phrase "the
effective date of this appendix" as an unresolved placeholder. There is **no "Effective Date" paragraph, no authority
paragraph, and no severability paragraph** in the extracted pages: the text runs Purpose (¶¶1–2), Applicability (¶3),
Definitions (¶¶4–15), calculation requirements (¶¶16–32), Attachment — and stops. A search of the whole extraction for date
expressions returns only the phrases quoted below.

The **only calendar dates printed anywhere in A-830 are 1 January 2004**, and they all mark the same event: the cutover from the
1980 CSO basis (with elective select factors) to the 2001 CSO Mortality Table. The date-bearing sentences, verbatim:

### Grandfathering and transition language

- **Reentry grandfathering (¶3.a.i).** The appendix does not apply to a policy issued on or after its effective date if that
  policy is issued "in accordance with and as a result of the exercise of a reentry provision contained in the original life
  insurance policy of the same or greater face amount, issued before the effective date of this appendix, that guarantees the
  premium rates of the new policy." The carve-out **propagates**: it "also shall not apply to subsequent policies issued as a
  result of the exercise of such a provision, or a derivation of the provision, in the new policy" (p. 1206). Modelling
  consequence: reentry chains descending from a pre-effective-date policy stay on the old basis indefinitely, so the
  applicability flag is inherited at issue, not derived from the issue date alone.
- **Election lock-in (¶26.g).** If the attained-age-based YRT exemption is elected, "this approach shall be applied in
  determining reserves for all attained-age-based YRT life insurance policies issued on or after the effective date of this
  appendix" (p. 1217) — an all-or-nothing company-level election, not a per-policy one.
- **Post-issue unilateral changes (¶20).** Guarantees the insurer adds unilaterally after issue that bite for more than one year
  are handled by a triple-maximum rather than a transition rule; see Mechanics.
- **Secondary guarantees changed after issue (¶29.b).** "Secondary guarantees that are unilaterally changed by the insurer after
  issue shall be considered to have been made at issue," and the ¶30/¶31 reserves are **recalculated from issue** to reflect the
  change (p. 1219).

### The exceptions (¶3.a) — six of them

1. **Reentry policies** (above).
2. **Short-secondary-guarantee universal life.** The appendix does not apply to a UL policy meeting **all three** of:
   (a) "Secondary guarantee period, if any, is five (5) years or less";
   (b) "Specified premium for the secondary guarantee period is not less than the net level reserve premium for the secondary
   guarantee period based on the CSO valuation tables as defined in paragraph 9 and the applicable valuation interest rate" —
   for contracts issued from 1/1/2004 that net level reserve premium uses **ultimate** 2001 CSO rates; and
   (c) "The initial surrender charge is not less than 100 percent of the first year annualized specified premium for the
   secondary guarantee period." (p. 1207)
   A drafting note points the reader to ¶29 for what counts as a secondary guarantee, and a second drafting note at ¶29 confirms
   that UL and VUL policies with secondary guarantees meeting ¶3.a.ii "are not subject to this appendix" (p. 1219).
3. **Variable life** — any variable life policy whose amount or duration of insurance varies with separate account investment
   experience.
4. **Variable universal life** — same wording, applied to VUL.
5. **Group life certificates**, unless the certificate "provides for a stated or implied schedule of maximum gross premiums
   required in order to continue coverage in force for a period in excess of one year."
6. **Preneed policies**, "which follow the requirements of **A-817**."

### The conditions (¶3.b) — the two routing rules

- Policies with **guaranteed nonlevel gross premiums or guaranteed nonlevel benefits (other than universal life policies)**, or
  both, are valued under **¶¶21–28**.
- **Flexible premium and fixed premium universal life** policies containing provisions that let a policyholder keep the policy
  in force over a **secondary guarantee period** are valued under **¶¶29–32**.

Nothing in ¶3.b routes a level-premium, level-benefit non-UL policy anywhere. Such a policy is still within the ¶3 scope
sentence and still subject to ¶2's declaration that the appendix's basic reserve method is CRVM for it, but the appendix
supplies no separate calculation paragraph for it. Treat that as an observation about the printed structure, not as a licence to
infer an outcome.

### 2. The contract segmentation method (¶5) — segment determination

**Definition as printed.** The method divides "the period from issue to mandatory expiration of a policy into successive
segments, with the length of each segment being defined as the period from the end of the prior segment (from policy inception,
for the first segment) to the end of the latest policy year as determined below."

**Segment length rule, verbatim (PDF p. 1208):**

> "The length of a particular contract segment shall be set equal to the minimum of the value t for which G t is greater than
> R t (if G t never exceeds R t the segment length is deemed to be the number of years from the beginning of the segment to the
> mandatory expiration date of the policy)"

**The two ratios, reproduced faithfully** (the print sets these as stacked fractions; superscript/subscript levels are lost in
the text layer, so subscripts are shown in parentheses here):

```
                GP(x+k+t)
    G(t)  =  ---------------
               GP(x+k+t-1)

                q(x+k+t)
    R(t)  =  ---------------
               q(x+k+t-1)
```

**Symbols, as defined in the print:**

- `x` = original issue age.
- `k` = the number of years from the date of issue to the beginning of the segment.
- `t` = 1, 2, …; **"t is reset to 1 at the beginning of each segment"**.
- `GP(x+k+t-1)` = "Guaranteed gross premium per thousand of face amount for year t of the segment, **ignoring policy fees only
  if level for the premium paying period of the policy**." (So the premium indexed at attained age `x+k+t-1` is the premium
  falling due at the start of segment-year `t`, i.e. policy year `k+t`.)
- `q(x+k+t-1)` = "the valuation mortality rate for deficiency reserves in policy year k+t **but using the unmodified select
  mortality rates if modified select mortality rates are used in the computation of deficiency reserves**." The print defines
  only the denominator rate; the numerator `q(x+k+t)` is the same quantity one policy year later, which the text leaves implicit.

**The one-percent tolerance, verbatim:**

> "However, Rt may be increased or decreased by one percent in any policy year, at the company's option, but Rt shall not be
> less than one" (p. 1207)

So `R(t)` is company-elective within `max(1, R(t) ± 1%)`, elected **per policy year**. The drafting note gives the rationale
without arithmetic: the tolerance exists "to prevent irrational segment lengths due to such things as premium rounding", the
worked illustration being a plan whose gross premiums are a ratio times the underlying ultimate mortality rates with the ratio
varying by issue age, where rounding to whole cents would otherwise produce segments longer than one year; the tolerance
"allows the creation of one year segments for a plan in which premiums parallel the underlying valuation mortality table."

**Degenerate-premium conventions, verbatim:**

> "However, if GP x+k+t is greater than 0 and GP x+k+t-1 is equal to 0, G t shall be deemed to be 1000. If GPx+k+t and
> GPx+k+t-1 are both equal to 0, Gt shall be deemed to be 0." (p. 1208)

Implementation reading: a premium restarting after a zero-premium year forces `G = 1000`, which will exceed any `R`, so the
segment ends there; a run of zero premiums gives `G = 0`, which can never exceed `R ≥ 1`, so zero-premium years never break a
segment.

**Mortality basis for the segmentation calculation itself.** Per ¶5, for contracts from 1/1/2004 "all calculations are made
using the 2001 CSO Mortality Rate, and, if elected, the optional minimum mortality standard for deficiency reserves stipulated
in paragraph 17" — i.e. the segment boundaries move if the company elects X-factor mortality, **except** that the unmodified
select rates are used in `R(t)` where modified select rates are used for the deficiency reserve itself.

### 3. Segmented reserves (¶11)

**Construction.** Segmented reserves are "the present value of all future guaranteed benefits less the present value of all
future net premiums **to the mandatory expiration of a policy**, where the net premiums within each segment are a uniform
percentage of the respective guaranteed gross premiums within the segment."

**The per-segment uniform percentage.** For each segment, the percentage is set so that at the **beginning of that segment** the
present value of net premiums within the segment equals:

```
   PV(death benefits within the segment)
 + PV(any unusual guaranteed cash value (see ¶24) occurring at the END of the segment)
 - (any unusual guaranteed cash value occurring at the START of the segment)
 + [first segment only]  max(0-style excess) of Item (a) over Item (b), where

     Item (a) = PV at issue of the benefits provided for IN THE FIRST SEGMENT after the first policy year
                --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                PV at issue of an annuity of 1 per year payable on the first and each subsequent
                anniversary WITHIN THE FIRST SEGMENT on which a premium falls due

                capped at: the net level annual premium on the NINETEEN-YEAR premium whole life plan
                of the same renewal year equivalent level amount at an age ONE YEAR HIGHER than the
                age at issue of the policy

     Item (b) = a net one-year term premium for the benefits provided for in the first policy year
```

That "excess of Item (a) over Item (b)" is the CRVM expense allowance, and the appendix's version of it is **segment-scoped**:
both the numerator benefits and the annuity denominator are restricted to the **first segment**, where the unitary version at
¶14 runs them to mandatory expiration. An implementation that reuses one expense-allowance routine for both must therefore
parameterise the horizon. The 19-pay-whole-life cap at age **x+1** is printed identically in ¶11 and ¶14.

**Interest rate cap (¶11.c).** "The interest rates used in the present value calculations for any policy may not exceed the
maximum valuation interest rate, determined with a guarantee duration equal to the **sum of the lengths of all segments** of the
policy." Since ¶5 divides the whole period from issue to mandatory expiration into successive segments, that sum spans the same
period as the unitary rule at ¶14.b ("the length from issue to the mandatory expiration of the policy") — the two are stated
differently but reference the same span.

**Present-value scope (¶11.d).** "For both basic reserves and deficiency reserves computed by the segmented method, present
values shall include future benefits and net premiums **in the current segment and in all subsequent segments**." A valuation at
a date inside segment 3 is not a segment-3-only calculation.

**Drafting note (p. 1210), which pre-empts three design questions.** Segmentation is not limited to plans without cash surrender
values, "otherwise companies could avoid segmentation entirely by designing policies with minimal (positive) cash values";
segmentation for plans with CSVs "should be based **solely upon gross premium levels**"; and "The calculation of segmented
reserves shall not be linked to the occurrence of a positive unitary terminal reserve at the end of a segment. The requirement
of this appendix to hold the greater of the segmented reserve or the unitary reserve eliminates the need for any linkage."

### 4. Unitary reserves (¶14)

"the present value of all future guaranteed benefits less the present value of all future modified net premiums", with
guaranteed benefits and modified net premiums "considered to the mandatory expiration of the policy", and modified net premiums
a **single** uniform percentage of the respective guaranteed gross premiums such that at issue:

```
 PV(net premiums) = PV(all death benefits and pure endowments)
                  + [ Item (a) - Item (b) ]

   Item (a) = PV at issue of the benefits provided for AFTER THE FIRST POLICY YEAR
              -----------------------------------------------------------------
              PV at issue of an annuity of 1 per year payable on the first and each
              subsequent anniversary OF THE POLICY on which a premium falls due

              capped at the same nineteen-year premium whole life plan premium at age x+1

   Item (b) = a net one-year term premium for the benefits provided for in the first policy year
```

Interest cap (¶14.b): the maximum valuation interest rate with guarantee duration equal to "the length from issue to the
mandatory expiration of the policy".

The drafting note states the purpose plainly: to define "as specifically as possible what has become commonly called the unitary
method", because A-820 does not define "unitary" for nonlevel policies and its requirement for reserves computed by a method
"consistent with the principles of Appendix A-820" "has not been uniformly interpreted."

### 5. Basic reserves for nonlevel non-UL policies (¶21)

> "Basic reserves shall be calculated as the **greater of the segmented reserves and the unitary reserves**. Both the segmented
> reserves and the unitary reserves for any policy shall use the same valuation mortality table and selection factors."
> (p. 1213)

At the insurer's option, **one** of two adjustments may be made in calculating segmented reserves and net premiums:

- **(i)** treat the **unitary reserve**, if greater than zero, applicable at the end of each segment as a **pure endowment**, and
  subtract the unitary reserve, if greater than zero, applicable at the beginning of each segment from the present value of
  guaranteed life insurance and endowment benefits for each segment; or
- **(ii)** the same construction using the **guaranteed cash surrender value** in place of the unitary reserve.

### 6. Deficiency reserves (¶17, ¶22)

**The quantity A construction (¶17, PDF p. 1211).** Deficiency reserves "are calculated for each policy as the excess, if greater
than zero, of the quantity A over the basic reserve. The quantity A is obtained by **recalculating the basic reserve for the
policy using guaranteed gross premiums instead of net premiums when the guaranteed gross premiums are less than the
corresponding net premiums**."

This is more specific than "valuation net premiums in excess of gross": A is a **full re-run of the basic reserve** with the net
premium replaced, duration by duration, by the guaranteed gross premium wherever the gross is the smaller of the two. The
substitution is one-sided — where the gross premium exceeds the net premium the net premium stands.

**Which basis (¶22.a).** The deficiency reserve at any duration is calculated:

- on a **unitary** basis if the corresponding basic reserve under ¶21 is unitary;
- on a **segmented** basis if the corresponding basic reserve under ¶21 is segmented;
- on the **segmented** basis if the basic reserve is equal to both.

So the deficiency basis is a slave to the outcome of the ¶21 max, with the tie broken toward segmented.

**Trigger (¶22.b), verbatim:** ¶22 applies "to any policy for which the guaranteed gross premium at any duration is less than the
corresponding modified net premium calculated by the method used in determining the basic reserves, but **using the minimum
valuation standards of mortality (specified in paragraph 17) and rate of interest**." The comparison net premium is therefore on
the *deficiency* standard, not necessarily on the standard used for the basic reserve.

**Scope (¶22.c):** the excess of A over the basic reserve is taken "for the current and all remaining periods".

**Segment lengths (¶22.d):** "For deficiency reserves determined on a segmented basis, the quantity A is determined using
segment lengths **equal to those determined for segmented basic reserves**." Segments are not re-derived on the deficiency
mortality basis.

### 7. Mortality standards and the X-factor mechanics (¶¶16–18)

**Basic reserves (¶16).** Prior to 1/1/2004, at company election for one or more specified plans, the minimum mortality standard
for basic reserves may use the 1980 CSO valuation tables with select mortality factors, the factors being (a) the ten-year select
factors referenced in A-820, (b) the select mortality factors in the Attachment, or (c) any later NAIC-adopted table of select
factors. **Effective 1/1/2004 the 2001 CSO Mortality Table is the minimum standard for basic reserves** "(or any other valuation
mortality table adopted by the NAIC after the effective date of the 2001 CSO table for this purpose)."

A drafting note records the provenance of the Attachment factors: durations 1–15 "reflect the Society of Actuaries' data for the
years 1983 through 1986, split by sex and smoking status, with fifteen years of mortality improvement, based on Society of
Actuaries' Projection Scale A applied. A **50% margin** was added. The factors were then graded to the 1980 CSO Tables over the
next five durations."

**Deficiency reserves (¶17).** Same three-way election prior to 1/1/2004; **effective 1/1/2004 the 2001 CSO Mortality Table is
the minimum standard for deficiency reserves.** Then the X-factor sentence, verbatim:

> "If select mortality rates are used, they may be multiplied by X percent for durations in the first segment, subject to the
> conditions specified in Sectio ns 17c. to iv. (a)." (p. 1211 — the cross-reference is garbled in the print; it reads as ¶17.c
> through ¶17.c.vi.(a))

with an anti-cherry-picking rule: "In demonstrating compliance with those conditions, the demonstrations may not combine the
results of tests that utilize the 1980 CSO Mortality Table with those tests that utilize the 2001 CSO Mortality Table, unless
the combination is explicitly required by regulation or necessary to be in compliance with relevant Actuarial Standards of
Practice."

**The X conditions (¶17.c), all six:**

| | Condition as printed |
|---|---|
| i | "X may vary by policy year, policy form, underwriting classification, issue age, or any other policy factor expected to affect mortality experience" |
| ii | X is such that, **using the valuation interest rate used for basic reserves**, (a) ≥ (b), where (a) is "The actuarial present value of future death benefits, calculated using the mortality rates resulting from the application of X" and (b) is "The actuarial present value of future death benefits calculated using **anticipated mortality experience without recognition of mortality improvement beyond the valuation date**" |
| iii | X is such that the resulting mortality rates "are at least as great as the anticipated mortality experience, without recognition of mortality improvement beyond the valuation date, **in each of the first five (5) years after the valuation date**" |
| iv | "The appointed actuary shall **increase** X at any valuation date where it is necessary to continue to meet all the requirements of paragraph 17.c." |
| v | "The appointed actuary **may decrease** X at any valuation date as long as X continues to meet all the requirements of paragraph 17.c." |
| vi | "The appointed actuary shall specifically take into account the **adverse effect on expected mortality and lapsation of any anticipated or actual increase in gross premiums**." |

So the X test is a **two-limb** test: an aggregate present-value limb (ii) *and* a year-by-year floor over the next five years
(iii). Passing the PV test alone is not sufficient.

**Consequences of X < 100 (¶17.c.vi.(a)).** "If X is less than 100 percent at any duration for any policy, the following
requirements shall be met":

1. the appointed actuary "shall annually prepare an actuarial opinion and memorandum for the company in conformance with the
   **asset adequacy analysis** requirements as outlined in **Appendix A-822**";
2. the appointed actuary "shall disclose, in the **Regulatory Asset Adequacy Issues Summary**, the impact of the insufficiency
   of assets to support the payment of benefits and expenses and the establishment of statutory reserves during one or more
   interim periods"; and
3. the appointed actuary "shall annually opine for all policies subject to this appendix as to whether the mortality rates
   resulting from the application of X meet the requirements of paragraph 17.c. This opinion shall be supported by an actuarial
   report, subject to appropriate Actuarial Standards of Practice promulgated by the Actuarial Standards Board of the American
   Academy of Actuaries. The **X factors shall reflect anticipated future mortality, without recognition of mortality
   improvement beyond the valuation date, taking into account relevant emerging experience.**"

The appendix prescribes **no X table, no floor value and no cap**; X is a company quantity constrained only by ¶17.c, and by
¶17.c.i it may be set above 100 as well as below.

**The first-segment restriction (¶18), verbatim (p. 1212):**

> "This paragraph applies to both basic reserves and deficiency reserves. **Any set of select mortality factors may be used only
> for the first segment.** However, if the first segment is less than ten (10) years, the appropriate ten-year select mortality
> factors referenced in Appendix A-820 may be used thereafter **through the tenth policy year from the date of issue**."

The drafting note gives the reasoning: the appendix "does not allow the use of select mortality factors beyond the first
segment", because a premium increase big enough to force a new segment will bring increased lapsation and mortality
deterioration; and "for policies that have reentry provisions, select mortality factors shall not be used in segments beginning
after reentry **unless a new policy is actually issued**." The ten-year carve-back is allowed "in recognition that the mortality
deterioration is unlikely to occur to a significant degree within the first ten (10) years."

**Policy fees (¶19).** Guaranteed gross premiums **without** policy fees may be used wherever the calculation involves the
guaranteed gross premium, "but only if the policy fee is a level dollar amount after the first policy year." Asymmetry worth
coding explicitly: "In determining deficiency reserves, policy fees **may** be included in guaranteed gross premiums, even if
not included in the actual calculation of basic reserves."

### 8. Minimum values and floors (¶23)

- **Mean reserves:** basic reserves may not be less than **the tabular cost of insurance for the balance of the policy year**.
- **Mid-terminal reserves:** basic reserves may not be less than the tabular cost of insurance "for the balance of the current
  modal period **or to the paid-to-date, if later, but not beyond the next policy anniversary**".
- The tabular cost of insurance "shall use the same valuation mortality table and interest rates as that used for the
  calculation of the segmented reserves. However, if select mortality factors are used, they shall be the **ten-year select
  factors** referenced in Appendix A-820." Effective 1/1/2004 the table is the **ultimate** rates in the 2001 CSO Table.
- **The surrender-value floor, verbatim:** "In no case may total reserves (including basic reserves, deficiency reserves and any
  reserves held for supplemental benefits that would expire upon contract termination) be less than the amount that the
  policyowner would receive (including the cash surrender value of the supplemental benefits, if any, referred to above),
  **exclusive of any deduction for policy loans**, upon termination of the policy." (p. 1214) Note this floor is on **total**
  reserves, not on the basic reserve alone.

### 9. Unusual pattern of guaranteed cash surrender values (¶24)

**The test (¶24.c).** A policy has an unusual pattern of guaranteed CSVs "if any future guaranteed cash surrender value exceeds
the prior year's guaranteed cash surrender value by more than the sum of":

```
   110% x (scheduled gross premium for that year)
 + 110% x (one year's accrued interest on [ prior year's guaranteed CSV + scheduled gross premium ],
           using the NONFORFEITURE interest rate used for calculating policy guaranteed cash surrender values)
 +   5% x (first policy year surrender charge, if any)
```

**Before the first unusual value (¶24.a).** Reserves actually held prior to the first unusual guaranteed CSV "shall not be less
than the reserves calculated by treating the first unusual guaranteed cash surrender value as a **pure endowment** and treating
the policy as an **n year policy providing term insurance plus a pure endowment** equal to the unusual cash surrender value,
where n is the number of years from the date of issue to the date the unusual cash surrender value is scheduled."

**After an unusual value (¶24.b).** Reserves held subsequent to any unusual guaranteed CSV are floored by the same n-year
term-plus-pure-endowment construction aimed at the **next** unusual value, "treating any unusual guaranteed cash surrender value
at the end of the prior segment as a **net single premium**", where:

- `n` = years from the date of the last unusual guaranteed CSV prior to the valuation date to the earlier of (a) the date of the
  next unusual guaranteed CSV scheduled after the valuation date, or (b) the mandatory expiration date;
- the net premium for a given year in the n-year period = **net-to-gross ratio × the respective gross premium**; and
- the net-to-gross ratio is:

```
        PV at start of the n-year period of death benefits payable during the n-year period
      + PV at start of the n-year period of the next unusual guaranteed cash surrender value, if any
      - the amount of the last unusual guaranteed cash surrender value, if any, scheduled at the start of the period
      -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
        PV at start of the n-year period of the scheduled gross premiums payable during the n-year period
```

**Where this sits in the stack.** The drafting note is explicit: "This requirement is independent of both the segmentation
process and the unitary process. **After the greater of the segmented or the unitary reserve has been determined**, then this
paragraph imposes an additional floor on the ultimate reserve," to "assure adequate funding of significant increases in
guaranteed cash surrender values." Unusual CSVs also enter the segmented reserve itself, via ¶11.a.ii and ¶11.a.iii.

### 10. Post-issue unilateral guarantee changes (¶20)

Reserves for policies with changes to guaranteed gross premiums, guaranteed benefits, guaranteed charges or guaranteed credits
"that are unilaterally made by the insurer after issue and that are effective for more than one year after the date of the
change" shall be **the greatest of**: "(1) reserves calculated ignoring the guarantee, (2) reserves assuming the guarantee was
made at issue, and (3) reserves assuming that the policy was issued on the date of the guarantee." Three parallel valuations, not
one.

### 11. The four exemptions (¶¶25–28)

**¶25 — Optional exemption for yearly renewable term reinsurance.** At company option:

- valuation net premium for each future policy year = **the tabular cost of insurance for that future year**;
- basic reserves never less than the tabular cost of insurance for the appropriate ¶23 period;
- deficiency reserves: for each policy year compute the excess, if greater than zero, of the valuation net premium over "the
  respective **maximum guaranteed gross premium**"; the deficiency reserve is never less than the sum of the present values, at
  the valuation date, of those excesses;
- pre-2004 calculations use the maximum valuation interest rate and the 1980 CSO tables with or without ten-year select factors;
  **from 1/1/2004 they use the ultimate rates in the 2001 CSO Table**;
- **"A reinsurance agreement shall be considered YRT reinsurance for purposes of this paragraph if only the mortality risk is
  reinsured."**
- **Ceding-side consequence:** "If the assuming company chooses this optional exemption, the ceding company's reinsurance
  reserve credit shall be **limited to the amount of reserve held by the assuming company** for the affected policies."

The drafting note explains the exemption exists because traditional YRT reserves "are already adequate and sufficient" and
without it YRT reinsurance "would be subject to the more complex segmentation calculations."

**¶26 — Optional exemption for attained-age-based YRT life insurance policies.** Same four calculation rules as ¶25 (a–d), plus
a definition and a lock-in:

- A policy qualifies if **both** (i) the premium rates on **both the initial current premium scale and the guaranteed maximum
  premium scale** "are based upon the attained age of the insured such that the rate for any given policy at a given attained age
  of the insured is **independent of the year the policy was issued**", and (ii) those rates "are the same as the premium rates
  for policies covering all insureds of the same **sex, risk class, plan of insurance and attained age**."
- Policies that *become* attained-age-based after an initial period may use the approach after that period if the initial period
  is constant for all insureds of the same sex, risk class and plan, **or** runs to a common attained age for all such insureds,
  and the ¶26.e conditions are met afterwards.
- **¶26.g lock-in:** if elected, the approach applies to **all** attained-age-based YRT policies issued on or after the effective
  date of the appendix.

**¶27 — Exemption from unitary reserves for certain n-year renewable term policies.** Unitary basic reserves and unitary
deficiency reserves need not be calculated where **all three** hold:

- (a) the policy "consists of a series of n-year periods, including the first period and all renewal periods, where n is the same
  for each period, except that for the final renewal period, n may be truncated or extended to reach the expiry age, **provided
  that this final renewal period is less than 10 years and less than twice the size of the earlier n-year periods**, and for
  each period, the premium rates on **both the initial current premium scale and the guaranteed maximum premium scale are
  level**";
- (b) prior to 1/1/2004, "the guaranteed gross premiums in all n-year periods are not less than the corresponding net premiums
  based upon the 1980 CSO Table with or without the ten-year select mortality factors" — from 1/1/2004 the calculation uses
  ultimate 2001 CSO rates; and
- (c) **"There are no cash surrender values in any policy year."**

Drafting note: without it, "companies issuing certain n-year renewable term policies could be forced to hold reserves higher than
n-year term reserves, even though in many cases gross premiums are well above valuation mortality rates."

**¶28 — Exemption from unitary reserves for certain juvenile policies.** Judged "based upon the initial current premium scale at
issue", all three required:

- (a) "At issue, the insured is age **twenty-four (24) or younger**";
- (b) "Until the insured reaches the end of the juvenile period, which shall occur **at or before age twenty-five (25)**, the
  gross premiums and death benefits are level, and there are **no cash surrender values**"; and
- (c) after the juvenile period, "gross premiums are level for the remainder of the premium paying period, and death benefits are
  level for the remainder of the life of the policy."

Drafting note, and it matters: the jumping-juvenile policy "has traditionally been valued in two segments. This exemption will
allow that practice to continue without requiring the calculation of reserves on a unitary basis. **However, within each segment,
both basic and deficiency reserves shall comply with the segmented reserve requirements.**" ¶27 and ¶28 remove the *unitary* leg
only — the segmented calculation still runs.

### 12. Secondary guarantees on universal life (¶¶29–32)

**Heading as printed:** "Calculation of Minimum Valuation Standard for Flexible Premium and Fixed Premium Universal Life
Insurance Policies That Contain Provisions Resulting in the Ability of a Policyowner to Keep a Policy in Force Over a Secondary
Guarantee Period" (PDF p. 1218).

**What counts as a policy with a secondary guarantee (¶29.a).** Three cases:

1. "A policy with a guarantee that the policy will remain in force at the original schedule of benefits, **subject only to the
   payment of specified premiums**";
2. prior to 1/1/2004, "a policy in which the **minimum premium at any duration is less than the corresponding one year valuation
   premium**, calculated using the maximum valuation interest rate and the 1980 CSO valuation tables with or without ten-year
   select mortality factors" — from 1/1/2004 the one-year valuation premium uses ultimate 2001 CSO rates; or
3. any combination of the two.

Note the second limb: a policy can be swept in **without any explicit guarantee clause**, purely because its guaranteed cost
structure lets a minimum premium below the one-year valuation premium keep it alive.

**Secondary guarantee period, and multiple guarantees (¶29.b).** The period is "the period for which the policy is guaranteed to
remain in force subject only to a secondary guarantee." Where a policy contains more than one:

> "the minimum reserve shall be the **greatest of the respective minimum reserves at that valuation date of each unexpired
> secondary guarantee, ignoring all other secondary guarantees**." (p. 1219)

So each unexpired guarantee is valued **stand-alone**, and the maximum is taken — not a combined valuation. Unilateral post-issue
changes are deemed made at issue and force recalculation of ¶30/¶31 reserves from issue.

**The three premium definitions (¶29.c–f).**

- **Specified premiums** = "the premiums specified in the policy, the payment of which guarantees that the policy will remain in
  force at the original schedule of benefits, but which otherwise would be insufficient to keep the policy in force in the
  absence of the guarantee if **maximum mortality and expense charges and minimum interest credits were made and any applicable
  surrender charges were assessed**."
- **Minimum premium** for any policy year = "the premium that, when paid into a policy with a **zero account value at the
  beginning of the policy year, produces a zero account value at the end of the policy year**", using "the policy cost factors
  (including mortality charges, loads and expense charges) and the interest crediting rate, which are all **guaranteed at
  issue**."
- **One-year valuation premium** = "the net one-year premium based upon the original schedule of benefits for a given policy
  year. The one-year valuation premiums for **all policy years are calculated at issue**. The select mortality factors defined in
  paragraphs 17.b., 17.c. and 17.d. **may not be used** to calculate the one-year valuation premiums." It "should reflect the
  frequency of fund processing, as well as the distribution of deaths assumption employed in the calculation of the monthly
  mortality charges to the fund."

**Basic reserves for the secondary guarantees (¶30), verbatim (p. 1219):**

> "Basic reserves for the secondary guarantees shall be the **segmented reserves for the secondary guarantee period**. In
> calculating the segments and the segmented reserves, the gross premiums shall be set equal to the **specified premiums, if
> any, or otherwise to the minimum premiums**, that keep the policy in force and the segments will be determined according to
> the contract segmentation method as defined in paragraph 5."

That is the whole ULSG construction: substitute specified (or failing that, minimum) premiums for gross premiums, run the ¶5
segmentation on them, and take segmented reserves over the secondary guarantee period. **There is no unitary leg here** — ¶30
says "segmented reserves", not "the greater of segmented and unitary".

**Deficiency reserves for the secondary guarantees (¶31).** Calculated "for the secondary guarantee period **in the same manner
as described in paragraph 22**", with gross premiums again set to specified premiums or otherwise minimum premiums.

**Minimum reserves during the secondary guarantee period (¶32).** The greater of:

- (a) "The basic reserves for the secondary guarantee **plus** the deficiency reserve, if any, for the secondary guarantees"; or
- (b) "The minimum reserves required by **other appendices governing universal life plans**."

Limb (b) is an unnamed cross-reference — the appendix does not say which appendix item it means. Do not resolve it to A-585 on
this text alone.

**3. Corrections and refinements the library should take.**

- **Deficiency reserve definition.** `us/regulatory/technical-notes.md` says "A deficiency arises where the valuation net
  premium exceeds the actual gross premium collected", and
  `us/regulatory/statutory-accounting-and-capital.md` says "**Deficiency reserves** — valuation net premiums in excess of gross".
  A-830 keys the test to the **guaranteed gross premium** (¶7: "guaranteed and determined at issue"), not to premium collected,
  and defines the reserve not as the premium shortfall but as **quantity A less the basic reserve**, A being a full
  recalculation of the basic reserve with guaranteed gross premiums substituted for net premiums duration by duration wherever
  the gross is the smaller (¶17). The trigger test at ¶22.b further specifies that the comparison net premium is computed on the
  **minimum valuation standards of mortality and interest**, which need not be the standards used for the basic reserve.
- **1980 CSO is a pre-2004 statement.** `products/term_life/product-spec.md` states flatly "the valuation basis is 1980 CSO
  with optional select factors", and `products/term_life/technical-notes.md` says "deficiency reserves with X-factor select
  mortality on 1980 CSO". A-830 as printed makes that the **pre-1/1/2004** branch and prints the 2001 CSO Mortality Table as the
  minimum standard for basic reserves (¶16), deficiency reserves (¶17), tabular cost of insurance (¶23) and all four exemption
  calculations (¶¶25–28) from 1/1/2004. Both statements should be date-qualified.
- **CRVM for XXX-era business is the A-830 method, not the SVL §5.A construction.** `us/regulatory/technical-notes.md` presents
  CRVM solely as the SVL §5.A single-uniform-percentage modified-net-premium formula. ¶2 of A-830 says its own basic-reserve
  method **constitutes CRVM** for policies it reaches, and that method is `max(segmented, unitary)` with a **segment-scoped**
  expense allowance in the segmented leg (¶11.a.iv restricts both the benefit numerator and the annuity denominator to the first
  segment, where ¶14.a.ii runs them to mandatory expiration). A term or ULSG block valued with the §5.A routine alone is not
  computing A-830 CRVM. The "Formulaic reserves" section should carry the segmented/unitary construction alongside the §5.A one.
- **"Model 830 Section 7" does not resolve against this print.** `products/guaranteed_ul/product-spec.md` ("Model 830 Section
  7 sets minimum reserves for UL with provisions keeping policies in force") and
  `products/universal_life/product-spec.md` ("Model 830 Section 7 and AG 38 govern reserves") both cite a Section 7. The
  appendix has no sections; the material is at **¶¶29–32**, under the heading quoted above. This says nothing about whether the
  separately published Model #830 has a Section 7 — it may — but a citation aimed at the manual must use paragraph numbers.
  Recommended form: `[REG-R154 ¶¶29–32]`.
- **A-830 does not apply to variable life or VUL at all.** ¶3.a.iii and ¶3.a.iv exclude both outright.
  `products/variable_ul/technical-notes.md` currently lists A-830 among items "not read" as if it were potentially relevant;
  the correct statement is that A-830 is **out of scope for VUL by its own terms**, so the open item for VUL is A-270 and A-820,
  not A-830. The drafting note at ¶29 reinforces this for VUL with secondary guarantees meeting ¶3.a.ii.
- **The ULSG safe harbour is a concrete, citable threshold the library does not yet carry.** ¶3.a.ii takes a UL policy out of
  A-830 entirely on three simultaneous conditions — secondary guarantee period ≤ 5 years, specified premium ≥ the net level
  reserve premium for that period, and initial surrender charge ≥ 100% of the first-year annualized specified premium. This
  belongs in `products/guaranteed_ul/` as a scope test.
- **The ULSG construction is now stateable.** `products/guaranteed_ul/product-spec.md` describes the AG 38 interpolation but
  says nothing concrete about the underlying A-830 mechanics. They are: segmented reserves over the secondary guarantee period
  with gross premiums replaced by specified (else minimum) premiums and segments from ¶5 (¶30); deficiency reserves on the same
  substitution via ¶22 (¶31); floor = max(basic + deficiency for the guarantee, the minimum reserve under other UL appendices)
  (¶32); and where several guarantees coexist, **the greatest of the stand-alone reserves for each unexpired guarantee, each
  computed ignoring the others** (¶29.b). Note ¶30 has **no unitary leg**.
- **No effective date is available from this source.** The library has been careful not to infer dates. A-830 supplies **no**
  calendar effective date for itself. Any statement about when XXX first bit must come from elsewhere and must not be attributed
  to [REG-R154]. What [REG-R154] does supply, verbatim, is the 1/1/2004 2001-CSO cutover.
- **YRT reserve credit ceiling.** ¶25.f caps the ceding company's reinsurance reserve credit at the reserve actually held by the
  assuming company whenever the assuming company elects the ¶25 exemption. `us/regulatory/statutory-accounting-and-capital.md`
  describes the ceded deduction as computed on the same mortality, interest and method as the direct reserve, and
  `us/regulatory/technical-notes.md` records YRT credit as "the one-year term mean reserve on the amount ceded on the *original
  policy's* mortality and interest basis" [REG-R92]. ¶25.f is an additional, independent ceiling that applies only in the
  elected-exemption case; it should be recorded next to the SSAP No. 61 rule rather than merged into it.

## Gaps and caveats

  NAIC after the effective date of the 2001 CSO table for this purpose" (¶16). The appendix does not name a successor table.
- **No annuity content**, no reinsurance content beyond ¶25, no mention of A-791, and no reporting or disclosure requirements
  beyond the ¶17.c.vi.(a) actuarial opinion package.
- **No prescribed X values.** X is defined only by the ¶17.c conditions.

  subscripted variables. In the text layer the subscripts drop to the baseline ("GP x+k+t", "q x+k+t-1"), the `R(t)` fraction is

  letter-spacing ("o p t i o n ,  b u t  R"). The formulas as reproduced above were reconstructed from that layout; the

- **Word-internal spaces throughout.** The text layer inserts spurious spaces mid-word at line-wrap points: "vari able",

- **"Attachment 1" versus "Attachment".** ¶¶16.b, 17.b and 17.c all refer to "the select mortality factors in **Attachment 1** of

  referent is unambiguous.
- **The Attachment header conflates two uses.** It says the tables are "the bases to which the respective percentage of
  paragraphs 16.b., 17.b. and 17.c. are applied." In fact ¶¶16.b and 17.b apply the tabulated factors themselves, while ¶17.c

- **The `q` numerator is not defined.** ¶5 defines `q(x+k+t-1)` explicitly but leaves `q(x+k+t)` to inference.
- **Table structure.** Duration columns run 1–19 with a single terminal `20+`; issue-age rows are banded `0-15` and `85+` at the
  ends. Two blank spacer lines appear at the foot of the first page of each two-page table; they carry no data. All six tables
  parsed to exactly 71 rows × 20 values with no ragged rows, which is a strong indication the numeric extraction is clean, but
  the values have **not** been checked against an independent copy of the tables.
- **One value worth a second look before use.** In the Male, Aggregate table the issue-age 61 row shows `75` at duration 10
  where surrounding rows in that corner of the table carry `75` blocks too, so it is consistent; but the general pattern of

  "corrected" by an implementer who assumes it is an artefact. It is as printed.
- **Scope observation, not a derivation.** ¶3 reaches all life insurance policies subject to the six exceptions, but ¶3.b routes
