# Pension Annuity (immediate lifetime annuity, individual) — research notes (UK)

Research notes for the UK individual pension annuity (immediate lifetime annuity purchased
with defined-contribution pension savings). These notes are the citation ground truth for the
UK pension-annuity product documents: source ids S1..S11 and R1..R14 below are frozen — never
renumber.

Access date for all citations: 2026-08-03.

Citation discipline: every extracted fact is tagged `[S#]` or `[R#]` pointing at a document
that was actually fetched and read. `[unverified]` marks statements from general knowledge or
from secondary summaries of documents that could not be retrieved. Where a fetch failed the

## Extracted specifications

### 1. Product structure and legal form
- Single-premium immediate annuity: the insurer pays a guaranteed regular income for the
  annuitant's life in return for a one-off payment of pension funds [S1][S2 §1.1][S5 §3][S6][S9].
- Legal wrappers observed:
  - L&G: one product; funds arrive either via the Open Market Option (originating scheme's
    rules continue to govern tax-free cash) or via an Immediate Vesting Personal Pension
    through the Legal and General Retirement Pension Scheme (L&G pays tax-free cash, usually
    within 3–5 working days of set-up) [S2 §1.5–1.6].
  - Just: policy schedule designates the policy as a Lifetime Annuity, a Scheme Pension, or an
    Immediate Vesting Pension written under the Just Retirement Pension Scheme (scheme rules
    override policy conditions for the IVP form) [S5 §1.2].
  - Canada Life: Lifetime Annuity vs Scheme Pension (plus Enhanced and Beneficiary's/
    Dependant's versions); "full funds" transfer (Canada Life pays tax-free cash) vs Open
    Market Option (ceding scheme pays it); drawdown-sourced transfers carry no tax-free cash
    [S6].
  - Aviva: "Pension Annuity (Open Market Option)" vs "Pension Annuity (Transfer)", the latter
    written under the Aviva Personal Pension Scheme whose rules override the plan document
    [S9 §§1–2].
- Statutory definition: a lifetime annuity must be payable by an insurance company that the
  member had an opportunity to select and be payable until death (or the later of death and
  the end of a term certain); annuities purchased from 6 April 2015 may decrease and are not
  limited to a 10-year term certain [R7]. The relaxation came from the Taxation of Pensions
  Act 2014 Sch 1 Part 2 [R6]; 30-year guarantee periods in current products [S1][S4][S6][S9]
  rely on it.
- Classification: long-term insurance business Class I (life and annuity) under the RAO 2001
  Sch 1 Part II [R8]; Just's conditions state the contract is pension business under s.58
  Finance Act 2012 and long-term business under RAO Sch 1 Part II [S5 §14.11].
- Non-participating: Canada Life states the policy does not share in profits [S7 §7.9]; the
  L&G/Just/Aviva contracts likewise contain no participation mechanics [S2][S5][S9]. Annuities
  in payment are non-profit guaranteed business [unverified as a market-wide statement, but
  true of all four sampled contracts].

### 2. Eligibility and minimum purchase
- Minimum purchase price: £10,000 after tax-free cash (and after any adviser charge) — L&G
  [S1 p4], Canada Life [S6], Aviva [S9]. Just instead requires at least £2,000 to remain to buy
  the annuity after any taxable lump sum [S4]; no £10k floor is stated in the Just KFD [S4].
- Minimum age 55 in most cases (normal minimum pension age), L&G [S1 p5]; Just states 55
  rising to 57 from 6 April 2028, or 50 where allowed by law [S4]; Canada Life 55+, or any age
  for a beneficiary of a deceased member's savings [S6].
- Maximum age: Aviva 90 at purchase [S9]. No maximum stated by L&G/Just/Canada Life in the
  retrieved documents; L&G constrains only the guarantee period (age ≤ 100 at its end) [S1 p10].
- Residence/scheme: Just requires the main home in the UK (excluding Channel Islands/IoM) and
  funds from a UK-registered pension scheme [S4]; Canada Life requires UK-registered scheme
  savings and UK residence [S6]; all four pay only into UK bank accounts as standard
  [S2 §1.3][S5 §11.2][S9] (Aviva can pay overseas at a charge, currently £2.74 per payment
  [S9]).
- Dependant/second-life minimum age: Just — named dependant at least 40 [S4]; Canada Life —
  second annuitant at least 35 (50 for enhanced annuities) [S6].

### 3. Premium, tax-free cash and lump sums
- One single premium; no money can be added after the start date (a further purchase is a new
  policy) [S2 §1.1][S6][S7 §7.3].
- Pension commencement lump sum (PCLS): normally up to 25% of the pot before purchase, capped
  by the Lump Sum Allowance of £268,275 (standard, protections can raise it) [S1 p6][S4][S6];
  PCLS must be taken before the annuity starts and cannot be taken afterwards [S1 p6][S6].
- Just additionally offers a taxable lump sum at outset (taxed as earned income, triggers the
  money-purchase annual allowance), with at least £2,000 retained for the annuity [S4][S5 §10].
- Adviser charges may be deducted from the premium and shown on the quote; commission, where
  paid, is priced into rates [S1 p12][S4][S6][S9]. L&G cannot pay an adviser charge where the
  pot includes a Guaranteed Annuity Rate or GMP [S1 p12].

### 4. Income options (escalation)
- Level (fixed) income for life — all four insurers [S1 p8][S4][S6][S9].
- Fixed-percentage escalation, compounded annually:
  - L&G: any rate up to and including 10% [S1 p8][S2 §3.2].
  - Canada Life: 0.1% to 10% [S6].
  - Just: fixed percentage (rate per policy schedule; no cap stated in KFD/terms) [S4][S5 §7.1.1].
  - Aviva: fixed rate (level not stated in retrieved edition) [S9].
- RPI-linked escalation:
  - L&G: RPI with a floor at zero and "catch-up" — if RPI falls the income is frozen and only
    increases again once RPI exceeds its previous peak; RPI measured over the 12 months ending
    six months before the anniversary, applied on the anniversary [S2 defs "Retail Prices
    Index"].
  - Just: two RPI variants — pure RPI (income can decrease) and RPI zero-floor; RPI measured to
    the month three months before the escalation date [S4][S5 §7.1.2–7.1.3].
  - Canada Life: RPI (income can go down), measured over the 12 months ending three months
    before the change date [S6][S7 defs].
  - Aviva: RPI using the index published the month before the increase versus one year
    earlier; negative RPI → income held level with catch-up above the prior index peak [S9].
- Capped RPI (LPI):
  - L&G: RPI capped 3% and RPI capped 5% ("Limited Price Indexation", using the September RPI
    figure) [S2 §3.2, defs "LPI"].
  - Just: LPI max 5% and LPI max 2.5%, using RPI over 1 October–30 September of the prior
    calendar year, collar 0% [S5 §7.1.4–7.1.5].
  - Canada Life: LPI 5 and LPI 2.5, using RPI for the year ending September [S6][S7 defs].
  - Aviva: "RPI up to a fixed amount" using the government-declared RPI for 1 October–30
    September of the prior year, floor 0 [S9 §4.3].
- Timing of increases: L&G — first increase on the first anniversary (GMP element with post-88
  RPI-capped-3% indexation increases from the payment period after 5 April) [S2 §3.3]; Just —
  escalation date is 1 April (advance + increasing GMP), 1 May (arrears + increasing GMP), else
  the first day of the anniversary month of the first instalment [S5 §7.2]; Canada Life — First
  Change Date per schedule with a defined proportioning rule if before the first anniversary
  [S7 §3.3].
- If an escalation option is chosen, starting income is lower than the level-income equivalent
  [S1 p8][S4][S6].
- Statutory context: only pre-6-April-2015 lifetime annuities were barred from decreasing;
  Just's pure-RPI (decreasing) option is consistent with the post-2015 rules [R7][R6].

### 5. Payment frequency and timing
- Frequencies: monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, yearly — all four [S1 p8][S2 §2.2][S4][S5
  §5.1][S6][S9].
- Timing: in advance (first payment on/just after the start date) or in arrears (first payment
  at the end of the first payment period). Income paid in arrears is higher than in advance
  [S1 p8][S2 §2.3][S6][S9].
- Just pays on the first day of the calendar month, with stub "proportional payments" covering
  the gap between the commencement date and the first scheduled instalment (both advance and
  arrears cases) [S5 §5.2–5.3].
- Arrears policies can be "with proportion": a final pro-rata payment from the last instalment
  to the date of death is paid to the estate; "without proportion" pays nothing for that stub
  (choosing "with proportion" lowers the starting income) [S1 p8][S2 §4][S5 §5.2.4][S7 §3.2].
  Just always pays the death stub on GMP and COSR elements even if "without proportion"
  [S5 §5.2.4(b)].
- Payments due on weekends/bank holidays are made at least one working day early (L&G)
  [S2 §2.4]. Aviva allows any monthly payment day up to the 28th [S9].
- All payments in GBP to a UK bank account; proof that the payee is alive may be required
  [S2 §§1.3, 10.1][S5 §11][S7 §3.6][S9].

### 6. Death benefits — dependant's/second life income
- Structure: optional joint-life benefit paying a chosen percentage of the annuitant's income
  to a survivor for life. All four insurers allow up to 100% [S1 p9][S2 §5.1][S4][S6][S7
  §3.5.2]; percentages are free-form (any % up to 100) at L&G [S1 p9] and Just ("all or part")
  [S4]; Canada Life quotes 50% as a typical example [S6]. Market-standard quoted levels of
  50%/67%/100% are [unverified] as a general statement.
- Basis choices:
  - L&G: "named spouse" (must still be spouse/CP at death), "any spouse" (whoever is spouse/CP
    at death — priced lower income than named), or "named financial dependant" (must prove
    financial dependence at death). For "any spouse", a spouse different from the one at
    purchase qualifies only if married at least the last six months of the annuitant's life
    and not 10+ years younger [S1 p9][S2 §§5.2–5.7].
  - Canada Life: named individual (entitlement survives divorce/remarriage and cannot be
    transferred to anyone else) or "spouse or civil partner at date of death"; an actuarial
    reduction applies if the survivor is more than 10 years younger than the annuitant
    [S7 §3.5.1, §3.5.5]. Scheme Pension non-spouse second annuitants must evidence financial
    dependence at set-up and at death [S6].
  - Aviva: schedule specifies either absolute entitlement of the named dependant, or
    entitlement conditional on being married/civil partner/a Finance Act 2004 Sch 28 para 15
    financial dependant at the annuitant's death [S9 §4.6].
  - Just: named dependant (aged 40+); may need to prove financial dependence (Scheme Pension
    form) [S4][S5 §6.1].
- The dependant's income is a percentage of the annuitant's income and escalates on the same
  basis [S2 §§5.12–5.13][S5 §6.2.1][S7 §3.5.2]. L&G pays the percentage of the higher of the
  income at death and the income at the end of the guarantee period [S2 §5.12].
- Overlap: where combined with a guarantee period, the dependant's income starts either at the
  annuitant's death ("with overlap" — both streams run during the guarantee; lower starting
  income) or at the end of the guarantee period ("without overlap") [S1 p9][S2 §§5.9–5.11]
  [S4][S5 §6.2.2][S6].
- Underwriting: the dependant's/second life's health is underwritten too and can attract an
  enhancement [S6][S9].

### 7. Death benefits — guarantee period (guaranteed minimum payment period)
- Income continues for a minimum term from the start date even if the annuitant dies within
  it. Ranges: L&G 1–30 years, and the annuitant cannot be over 100 at the period end [S1 p10]
  [S2 §§6.5–6.6]; Just 1–30 years [S4]; Canada Life from one month to 30 years [S6]; Aviva
  one-year guarantee as standard, extendable to 30 years, but capped at 10 years where the
  annuity originates from a defined-benefit scheme [S9].
- Remaining payments go to nominated beneficiaries or the estate; the insurer retains ultimate
  discretion (L&G, Just, Canada Life) [S2 §6.3][S5 §4.3.3][S7 §4.1]; Aviva pays the estate/
  dependant [S9].
- Lump-sum commutation of the remaining guarantee on death: Just offers a "lump sum basis"
  guarantee equal to the balance of instalments [S5 §4.3.2]; Aviva offers a lump sum (death
  before 75) equal to outstanding instalments discounted at a set rate, currently 0.75%
  compounded [S9 §4.5]; L&G and Canada Life pay only continuing instalments [S2 §6][S7 §4.2].
- Canada Life continues guaranteed payments "at the same level as if the Annuitant was still
  alive" (escalation continues) [S7 §4.2].

### 8. Death benefits — value protection (capital protection)
- Lump sum on death = protected percentage of the purchase price minus gross income already
  paid: L&G (protect any % up to 100%) [S1 p11][S2 §7]; Just (VP% × fund value after PCLS and
  any taxable lump sum, minus instalments including any final proportionate payment)
  [S5 §8.3]; Canada Life (Purchase Money × VP% minus annuitant instalments, guarantee payments
  due (excluding future RPI/LPI increases) and second-annuitant instalments) [S7 §4.3]; Aviva
  (up to 100% of price paid) [S9].
- Payment trigger with a joint-life policy: on the annuitant's death or on the death of the
  last survivor, chosen at outset [S1 p11][S2 §7.3][S4][S5 §8.4][S7 §4.3][S9]. Paying on first
  death costs more (lower income) [S1 p11].
- Constraint: where VP is paid on the annuitant's death and a dependant's income is also
  chosen, VP% + dependant's income % cannot exceed 100% (does not apply to last-survivor VP)
  [S2 §7.3][S4].
- Mutual exclusivity with the guarantee period: L&G — a policy can have either a guarantee
  period or value protection, not both [S2 §§6.7, 7.6]; Just — same restriction [S4]; Aviva —
  extending value protection beyond the standard 90 days cancels the standard one-year
  guarantee [S9]. Canada Life's provisions net guarantee payments off the VP lump sum,
  implying both can coexist on one policy [S7 §4.3].
- Aviva peculiarities: value protection applies automatically for the first 90 days (lump sum
  to the estate if death within 90 days = price paid minus payments made); optional
  continuation beyond 90 days for life or a fixed term [S9].
- VP is not available on GMP or Section 9(2B) rights (L&G) [S2 §7.7]; Just may limit VP for
  the most severe medical conditions [S4].
- If no death benefit is selected, payments simply stop on death [S1 p6][S4][S6][S9].
  Beneficiary's annuities (Canada Life) carry no death benefit options at all [S6].

### 9. Enhanced / impaired-life underwriting
- All four insurers offer enhanced terms for health and lifestyle factors: L&G (smoking, high
  blood pressure, serious conditions; rate also depends on age, postcode, market conditions)
  [S1 p5]; Just (product is priced on "personal, health and lifestyle information" as standard)
  [S4]; Canada Life (cancer, heart conditions, stroke, diabetes; smoker status, weight, blood
  pressure, cholesterol; questionnaire, no medical examination; second annuitant can qualify
  independently; no enhancement for conditions arising after purchase) [S6]; Aviva (cancer,
  heart attack, stroke, diabetes and other serious conditions; health questionnaire and
  possible GP report, no examination) [S9].
- Mis-statement remedies: L&G may reduce the income (not below its standard rate) and reclaim
  overpayments if medical/lifestyle information is not confirmed [S1 p4]; Aviva reduces to
  standard rates [S9]; Just may reduce income, recover overpayments, or cancel if it would
  never have issued [S4][S5 §13]; Canada Life may re-rate from the first payment date and
  recover overpayments with interest at Bank of England base rate +1%, and reserves the right
  to re-rate where aggregate enhanced-annuity premiums for the life exceed £1,000,000
  [S7 §3.4, §6].
- FCA conduct overlay: firms giving a guaranteed quote must test enhanced-annuity eligibility
  and compare against a market-leading (whole-of-market, including enhanced) quote [R5].

### 10. Surrender, alteration, assignment
- No surrender value; the annuity cannot be cashed in, sold or transferred: L&G ("no cash-in
  value... can't sell it, transfer it or take lump sums") [S1 p4][S2 §12]; Just ("This Policy
  has no surrender value", cl. 14.7) [S5]; Canada Life (cannot be cancelled or exchanged for a
  lump sum except as provided/permitted by legislation) [S7 §7.5]; Aviva (cannot be commuted
  or cashed in, in part or full) [S9 §3.9].
- Exceptions: pension sharing on divorce/dissolution (Welfare Reform and Pensions Act 1999)
  and Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 orders (L&G) [S2 §12.1]; pension sharing orders and insolvent
  scheme wind-up (Aviva) [S9 §3.9]; Occupational Pension Schemes (Discharge of Liability)
  Regulations 1997 reg 3 assignments (Just) [S5 §14.4]; court orders on divorce, at the
  policyholder's cost (Canada Life) [S7 §3.5.6].
- Options cannot be changed after the 30-day cancellation period [S1 p4][S4][S6][S9].
- Matching-adjustment linkage: the absence of policyholder options (or a surrender value not
  exceeding covering assets) is precisely MA eligibility condition 2.2(4) [R1] — UK annuity
  books are structured to satisfy it.
- No paid-up or alteration terms exist (nothing to alter; contract is fully paid) [S2][S5][S7]
  [S9].

### 11. Charges
- No explicit ongoing charges: set-up and administration costs are priced into the annuity
  rate at all four insurers [S1 p6][S4][S6][S9].
- Incidental charges: Aviva — £40 per plan rewrite where client information was wrong; £2.74
  per overseas payment (both "currently") [S9]. Adviser charges/commission are disclosed on
  the quote [S1 p12][S4][S6][S9].

### 12. Contracted-out benefits (GMP / Section 9(2B) / COSR / Protected Rights)
- Where the purchase money includes contracted-out rights, statutory minima attach:
  - GMP (contracting out pre-6 April 1997): L&G [S2 defs]; Just (accrued 1978–1997; paid
    monthly; adjustments where the start date precedes/follows State Pension Date; GMP State
    Pension Age remains 65 (men)/60 (women)) [S5 defs, §4.2]; Aviva and Canada Life similar
    definitions [S9 defs][S7 defs].
  - Survivor minima: 50% of GMP to a widow; 50% of post-88 GMP to a widower/same-sex spouse/
    civil partner — Canada Life [S7 §3.5.4], Aviva [S9], Just (pre-88 GMP to widow, post-88 GMP
    to legal spouse/registered civil partner, COSR 50-style treatment per schedule) [S5
    §§6.3–6.5]. Aviva: 50% of post-5-April-1997 "final salary" contracted-out pension to the
    widow(er)/civil partner where married at contract date [S9].
  - Escalation minima: post-88 GMP increases at RPI capped 3% applied from April/May dates
    [S2 §3.3][S5 §7.2]; scheme maximum-benefit rules cap other increases at the greater of 3%
    and RPI with carry-forward of withheld increases [S2 §9][S9 §4.4].
  - GMP/S9(2B) benefits can only go to a spouse/civil partner (not an unmarried financial
    dependant), and value protection cannot apply to them [S2 §§5.6, 7.7].
- Section 9(2B) rights: benefits from contracting out 6 April 1997–5 April 2016 [S2 defs];
  COSR is Just's label for post-97 contracted-out salary-related benefits [S5 defs].

### 13. Taxation (2026/27 rules as stated by insurers)
- Annuity income is taxed as earned income under PAYE [S1 p5][S2 §8][S4][S6][S9]; Canada Life
  applies an emergency code until HMRC issues one [S6].
- Death benefits:
  - Death before 75: dependant's income normally income-tax-free (except scheme pension);
    lump sums (guarantee balance, value protection) tax-free within the Lump Sum and Death
    Benefit Allowance, standard £1,073,100 [S1 p11][S2 §8][S4][S6].
  - Death at/after 75: income and lump sums taxed at the recipient's marginal rate
    [S1 p11][S4][S6][S9].
- Lump Sum Allowance £268,275 (standard) caps tax-free cash across all pensions [S1 p6][S4]
  [S6].
- Announced change: from 6 April 2027 most pension death benefits (remaining guarantee-period
  income, VP lump sums) are intended to fall within the estate for inheritance tax; per Just's
  summary this is not expected to apply where a dependant's income was chosen [S1 p11][S4].
- Statutory base: Finance Act 2004 (Sch 28 definitions; unauthorised payments Part 4)
  [R7][S5 §13.3][S9 §3.10]; pension freedoms tax framework from the Taxation of Pensions Act
  2014 [R6].

### 14. Cancellation
- 30-day cooling-off from confirmation that the annuity has started (and cancellable any time
  before it starts) — all four [S1 p7][S2 §13][S4][S6][S9].
- On cancellation: income received must be repaid; the insurer tries to return funds to the
  ceding scheme, which may refuse, in which case the client must find another provider; paid
  tax-free cash cannot be returned and the remainder must be used to buy another retirement
  income product within six months or tax charges arise [S1 p7][S2 §13][S6][S9]. HMRC has
  confirmed a paid PCLS cannot be reversed [S4]. Canada Life refunds adviser charges in full
  on cancellation [S6]; Aviva does not [S9].

### 15. Prudential and reserving context (for the projection model)
- Matching adjustment: annuity liabilities are the canonical MA portfolio. Eligibility
  conditions (PRA Rulebook, Matching Adjustment Part 2.2): no future premiums; underwriting
  risk limited to longevity, expense, revision, mortality, recovery-time risk; ≤5% BEL increase
  under the prescribed mortality stress (worse of +15% level or +0.15pp additive); no
  policyholder options beyond a bounded surrender option; ring-fenced assets; prudent person
  principle [R1]. MA permission required before use [R1].
- Solvency UK MA reforms (2023–24): highly-predictable-cash-flow assets admitted subject to a
  10% cap on their share of aggregate MA benefit; expanded liability eligibility; SIG cap
  removed; notched fundamental spread; annual attestation; MALIR reporting; proportionate
  breach regime [R2]. Final rules in PS10/24, effective 30 June 2024 with first attestation by
  31 December 2024 [R3 — unverified detail, PS not retrievable].
- Risk margin: reformed by SI 2023/1346 amending the onshored Delegated Regulation and the
  Solvency 2 Regulations 2015 [R9]; the reduced cost-of-capital/tapering parameters commonly
  cited (4%, λ 0.9 for life) are [unverified] here.
- Conduct: COBS 19.9 annuity comparison information governs the point of sale (market-leading
  quote, enhanced-annuity questions, prescribed comparator template) [R5]. Pension Wise
  signposting and shopping-around statements appear in every KFD [S1][S4][S6][S9].
- FSCS: 100% of a valid claim, no upper limit, for long-term insurance [S1 p15][S4][S6][S9].
- Compensation/authorisation: all four insurers are authorised by the PRA and regulated by the
  FCA and PRA [S1][S4][S6][S9].

### 16. Mortality and demographic assumptions (for the projection model)
- Base tables: CMI SAPS series (S1 2008, S2 2014, S3 2018, S4 February 2024) graduated on UK
  self-administered pension scheme pensioner experience [R10]; S4 uses 2014–2019 data,
  effective date 1 January 2017; full rate spreadsheets restricted to CMI Authorised Users
  [R11]. WP181 proposed amounts-weighted tables incorporating IMD [R11 — noted as consultation
  material, detail unverified].
- Improvements: CMI Mortality Projections Model, current version CMI_2024 (WP201), calibrated
  to England & Wales population data to 31 December 2024, APCOI structure, converging to a
  user-selected long-term rate; software restricted to Authorised Users [R12].
- Public fallback: ONS national life tables (period qx and life expectancy by age/sex, UK,
  three-year averages, updated annually) are freely available [R13].
- Insurer pricing additionally uses postcode and health/lifestyle rating [S1 p5][S9].
- Actuarial standards: technical actuarial work on UK annuity reserving/pricing falls under
  FRC TAS 100 and TAS 200 v2.0 (effective 1 January 2025) [R14].

Representative design for a reference implementation: the L&G Pension Annuity [S1][S2] is the
cleanest representative — single premium; monthly/quarterly/half-yearly/yearly payments in
advance or arrears (with optional proportionate final payment); escalation nil / fixed ≤10% /
RPI (floored, with catch-up) / LPI 5%; optional dependant's income (x% ≤ 100, with/without
overlap); guarantee period 1–30 years XOR value protection (VP% + dependant% ≤ 100 on the
first-death basis); no surrender value. Just/Canada Life/Aviva differ mainly in parameter
bounds and edge mechanics rather than structure, so the same cash-flow engine covers all four
with configuration. Enhanced underwriting is a rating (mortality multiplier / rated age)
overlay, not a structural difference [S1][S4][S6][S9].

Institutional and product variations (context, not modelled here):
- Bulk purchase annuities (buy-ins/buy-outs) transfer DB scheme liabilities to insurers; major
  writers include L&G, Aviva, Just, Pension Insurance Corporation and Rothesay [unverified —
  no BPA document retrieved]. The individual-annuity mechanics above (GMP tranches, LPI
  escalation, scheme maxima) mirror the benefit structures found in BPA business [S2 §9]
  [S5 §§4–7 provide the scheme-pension variant mechanics].
- Fixed-term annuities (income for a set term plus a guaranteed maturity amount) are a distinct
  product not covered by the retrieved documents [unverified].
- Beneficiary's/dependant's annuities (bought with inherited pension funds) are documented in
  the Canada Life materials: any age, no further death benefits [S6][S7].
- Annuity rates are set by insurers with reference to long-dated gilt/credit yields (the MA
  regime ties annuity pricing to spread assets [R1][R2]); day-to-day rate setting is not
  publicly documented [unverified].

## Gaps and caveats

1. **Aviva vintage.** Aviva's current KFD/terms (S10, S11) sit behind a host that returns
   HTTP 403 to automated fetches; the extraction uses the 10/2019 KFD + 02/2020 terms via a
   third-party mirror [S9]. Structural features are stable but the tax content there reflects
   the pre-2024 lifetime-allowance regime, and current-edition details (e.g. whether the
   90-day standard VP persists) are unconfirmed.
2. **PRA documents behind bot-blocking.** PS10/24 (R3) and SS7/18 (R4) could not be fetched
   (403); their contents are described only from secondary summaries and are tagged
   [unverified]. CP19/23 [R2] and the PRA Rulebook MA Part [R1] were fully retrieved and carry
   the verified MA detail.
3. **Risk margin parameters.** SI 2023/1346 was confirmed to exist and to amend the risk-margin
   provisions [R9], but the fetched contents page did not expose the cost-of-capital rate or
   tapering parameters; those remain [unverified].
4. **CMI data access.** Actual S3/S4 mortality rates and the CMI_2024 model are restricted to
   CMI Authorised Users [R11][R12]; only table names, data periods and methodology summaries
   are publicly confirmable. A reference implementation must either license CMI tables or
   substitute public ONS life tables [R13].
5. **No public rate cards.** None of the insurers publishes annuity rates or pricing bases;
