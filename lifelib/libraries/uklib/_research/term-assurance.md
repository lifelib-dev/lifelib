## Extracted specifications

### 1. Product architecture and cover shapes

- UK term assurance is written as long-term insurance business, RAO Class I "Life and
  annuity" — contracts of insurance on human life [R6].
- Market taxonomy (FCA): level term assurance (constant sum assured), decreasing term
  assurance (sum assured reduces, commonly tracking a repayment mortgage), increasing term
  assurance (inflation-linked), renewable term assurance (extension without new underwriting
  — offered in the market but not present in any of the three insurers' current retail
  products examined here), and family income benefit — "an ongoing monthly income … can be
  considered as a decreasing term assurance" [R8].
- All three insurers structure the retail product as one policy with a choice of payout basis:
  - Aviva Life Insurance+: level cover (lump sum), decreasing cover (lump sum decreasing
    monthly by a fixed interest rate), or family income cover (equal monthly instalments from
    claim acceptance to the policy end date) [S6][S7].
  - Royal London Personal Menu Plan: cover payable as level lump sum, increasing lump sum,
    decreasing lump sum, level monthly income, or increasing monthly income (income paid in
    arrears) [S8].
  - L&G sells the shapes as distinct products: Life Insurance (level), Decreasing Life
    Insurance, Increasing Life Insurance, and Family and Personal Income Plan (FPIP — monthly
    benefit) [S2].
- Terminal illness cover (accelerated payment of the death benefit) is embedded in all three
  insurers' term products [S1][S6][S8]; FCA describes it as a common feature paying the full
  death benefit on life expectancy < 12 months [R8].
- Policy ends on payment of the (single) main benefit; joint policies pay once [S1][S6][S8].

### 2. Eligibility, issue ages, terms, expiry ages

Legal & General [S2 limits tables; S3 age table; S4; S5]:
- Minimum age for buying any plan: 18. Policy cannot expire before the 29th birthday
  (minimum age at end of plan 29) [S2][S3].
- Life Insurance (level): max entry age 77; term 1–50 years (1-year minimum has no terminal
  illness cover); must end by 90th birthday [S2][S3].
- Decreasing Life Insurance: max entry age 74; term 5–50 years; end by 90th birthday [S2][S3][S5].
- Increasing Life Insurance: max entry age 77; term 2–50 years (no TIC at 2-yr min); end by
  90th birthday [S2][S3].
- Family and Personal Income Plan: max entry age 64; term 5–40 years; must end by 70th
  birthday (75th with CI; FPIP+CI max term 50 years) [S2][S4].
- UK residence: applicant must be UK resident, living in the UK at least 183 days in the last
  tax year [S1].

Aviva Life Insurance+ [S6][S7]:
- Minimum age 18 (policyholder and life covered) [S6][S7].
- Policy term 1–50 years, in whole years or to a specific age [S7].
- Life-only: max age at start 77; max age at policy end 90; min term 1 year [S7].
- Life + CI: max entry 64; max end age 75 (guaranteed premiums) or 90 (reviewable premiums,
  min term 6 years) [S7].
- Increasing cover option: max entry 77, max end 90, min term 5 years [S7].
- Waiver of premium and total permanent disability: max entry 64, benefit ceases at 70 [S7]
  (WOP claims payable to age 71 per conditions [S6] — see §11).
- Residence: applicant and life covered must be in, and consider their main home to be in,
  the UK, Channel Islands, Isle of Man or Gibraltar with a legal right to live there [S6].

Royal London Personal Menu Plan life cover [S9]:
- Entry ages 18–88 (attained); maximum expiry age 89; term 1–72 years [S9].
- Plan bases: single life, dual life (each life has own cover), joint life first event, joint
  life second event (joint second event and decreasing cover are lump-sum only) [S9].

### 3. Sum assured / benefit amounts and minimum premiums

- L&G maximum cover: £10m level or decreasing life cover, subject to underwriting; £4m
  increasing life; FPIP maximum monthly benefit £10,000/month (£4,000 with CI) [S2][S4].
  Minimum amount of cover: "set by minimum premium" [S2]; "The minimum premium will depend on
  the terms of the individual policy. There is no maximum premium." [S2]. Consumer DLI page:
  "Cover from only £5 a month", "Fixed premiums from £5 a month" [S5].
- Aviva: cover amount per policy schedule; no monetary min/max published in the retrieved
  conditions or summary; changes must leave the premium at or above "the minimum premium
  limit that applies at the time" (limit value not published) [S6].
- Royal London: "unlimited cover, or up to a maximum of £5 million when an increasing cover
  option" is selected [S9]; automatic indexation increases stop if total cover across all
  plans with Royal London would exceed £3,000,000 [S8].
- Market benchmark: ABI average term assurance claim value in 2023 was £54,600 [R8].
- Typical UK minimum premium is around £5/month [S5 for L&G; other insurers publish a
  "minimum premium limit" without a public value [S6]; the often-quoted market range of
  £5–£10/month is otherwise [unverified]].

### 4. Premium structure and guarantees

- Premium frequency: monthly or annual [S1][S6]; Aviva requires direct debit from a UK/CI/IoM/
  Gibraltar bank account in sterling [S6].
- Guaranteed premiums are the norm for life-only cover:
  - L&G: LI/DLI/ILI all guaranteed (G); with critical illness G or reviewable (R); CI-only R
    [S2]. "Premiums are guaranteed to stay the same throughout the length of the plan" unless
    the plan is altered or is an increasing policy; the old conversion option is no longer
    offered [S2].
  - Aviva: "For life cover only, your premiums will be guaranteed"; with CI, guaranteed or
    reviewable [S6][S7].
  - Royal London: life cover premiums guaranteed [S9]; reviewable exists on the menu plan for
    CI-type covers [S8].
- Reviewable premium mechanics (relevant to CI riders, and a modelling template):
  - Aviva: review every 5 years; assumptions cover medical advances/trends, industry
    developments and claims experience, legislation/taxation/regulation, and current/future
    claim cost; personal circumstances not re-assessed; no limit on the change; changes below
    2% or 50p are not implemented; policyholder may instead keep the premium and reduce cover
    [S6].
  - Royal London: premiums fixed for the first 5 years then reviewed 5-yearly; factors include
    expected claims, "the amount of money we'll pay to reinsurance companies with whom we
    share the costs of claims", lapses ("the number of plan owners who give up their plans
    early"), expenses, inflation, investment returns, taxes, and required reserves [S8].
- Premium rating factors disclosed to consumers: age, occupation, health, lifestyle, smoking
  habits, type and amount of cover [S7]; FCA: premiums reflect age, health, risk profile;
  reinsurers "play a key role in shaping these adjustments" [R8].
- Per-mille rate structure is not published by any of the three insurers (retail pricing is
  quote-engine based); characterisation of UK protection pricing as per-mille-rate driven is
  [unverified] at document level.

### 5. Death benefit and exclusions

- Benefit: lump sum (or monthly income for FIB shapes) on death of the life insured during
  the policy term [S1][S6][S8]. Payment in GBP to a UK bank account (L&G) [S1].
- Suicide/self-inflicted-injury exclusion — the only standard exclusion, applying in year one:
  - L&G: policy cancelled if within the **first year** the life insured dies as a result of
    suicide, intentional and serious self-injury, or an event where in L&G's reasonable
    opinion the life insured took their own life [S1].
  - Aviva: no payment if the life covered dies because of suicide or intentional self-inflicted
    injury **within 12 months of the policy start date** [S6][S7].
  - Royal London: death claims excluded if the result of intentional self-inflicted injury
    **within 12 months of the date cover started or restarted**; terminal illness/CI/TPD
    claims resulting from intentional self-inflicted injury are excluded at any time [S8].
- Other refusal grounds are conduct-based, not exclusions: misrepresentation on application,
  lapse before the claim event, failure to cancel a specified policy where the offer required
  it [S1]; untrue or incomplete claim information [S6].
- Underwriting exclusions may be applied case-by-case and shown in the policy schedule
  [S1][S3].
- Annual-premium policies: L&G refunds the premium for the remaining months of the policy
  year when a full-cover claim is paid [S1]. Aviva deducts unpaid premiums (within the grace
  window) from claim payments [S6].

### 6. Terminal illness benefit (standard accelerated benefit)

- Definition is essentially identical across the three insurers (two-limb test):
  a definite diagnosis by the attending/hospital consultant of an illness that (i) has no
  known cure or has progressed to the point where it cannot be cured, and (ii) in the
  consultant's opinion is expected to lead to death within 12 months [S1][S6][S8].
- L&G additionally requires the opinion of its own Medical Officer; the amount paid is the
  cover amount calculated at the date the definition is met (so for decreasing cover the TI
  payment can be less than a later death payment); no TI claim after death [S1].
- Royal London additionally requires the diagnosis to be the first and unequivocal diagnosis,
  by an appropriate medical specialist, confirmed by its chief medical officer [S8].
- Aviva will not pay if the person is "diagnosed with a terminal illness and you're expected
  to live longer than 12 months" [S6].
- L&G: terminal illness cover is not provided on 1-year (and ILI 2-year) minimum-term policies
  ("1 yr (no TIC)") [S2]; adviser page states TIC is automatic for terms of 2+ years [S4].

### 7. Decreasing cover (mortgage protection) mechanics

- Mechanism: the sum assured decreases each month in line with the amortisation schedule of a
  notional capital-and-interest (repayment) loan with a stated annual interest rate.
  - Aviva: the sum "goes down every month, at a rate set when you apply for your policy";
    conditions describe it as decreasing "each month using a fixed interest rate" (rate shown
    in the policy schedule; no default value published) [S6][S7].
  - Royal London (default schedule): pays the amount that would be outstanding on a capital-
    and-interest loan equal to the initial cover amount, same term, **yearly interest rate
    equal to 6%**, equal monthly repayments; cover therefore decreases monthly; warning that
    cover may not repay the mortgage if the actual loan rate exceeds 6% [S8].
  - Royal London (chosen-rate variant): where the "mortgage interest rate applies" feature is
    shown, the schedule uses the yearly interest rate shown in the cover summary; the adviser
    page gives the selectable range as "a set interest rate (between 0% and 15%)" [S8][S9].
  - Royal London "mortgage repayment guarantee": if the cover was taken out in connection with
    a repayment mortgage with matching term and amount, and changes were mirrored, Royal
    London pays the **actual outstanding balance** at claim (less arrears of capital and
    interest) rather than the notional schedule [S8]. This is a distinctive design.
  - L&G: an interest rate is applied to the policy to estimate the repayment-mortgage balance;
    the rate is shown in the Policy Schedule; warning if the mortgage rate is higher [S1][S3].
    Adviser page (verbatim): "Your client can choose whether they want their cover to decrease
    by 5%, 7%, 8%, or 10%" [S4] — i.e. the client selects the schedule rate from 5/7/8/10%.
- Premiums stay level (guaranteed) while the cover decreases [S1][S8].
- Modelling note: the monthly benefit amount B(t) for decreasing cover is the outstanding
  balance of an annuity-certain amortisation at the policy's schedule rate — parameters:
  initial sum assured, term, annual rate (L&G 5/7/8/10% menu [S4]; RL default 6% or chosen
  0–15% [S8][S9]; Aviva fixed rate per schedule [S6]).

### 8. Family income benefit mechanics

- Aviva family income cover: on an accepted claim, equal monthly instalments are paid from
  claim acceptance until the policy end date; the cover amount is expressed as a monthly
  figure; instalments are level unless increasing cover was chosen (3% or 5% fixed), in which
  case instalments continue to increase during payment and premiums do not increase for the
  indexation [S6][S7].
- Aviva commutation: the claimant can at any time during payment convert remaining instalments
  to a lump sum; the benefit is recalculated to be less than the sum of remaining instalments,
  with the reduction set "fairly and reasonably so it reflects how much it costs us to pay out
  in advance" [S6].
- Royal London income shapes: level or increasing monthly income paid in arrears until the
  cover end date; commuted value = monthly payment × months remaining, "reduced fairly and
  reasonably to reflect the fact that you'll be getting all the regular payments early" [S8].
- L&G FPIP: monthly benefit product; claim payment is a monthly benefit which "can be commuted
  as a lump sum at claim stage"; maximum monthly benefit £10,000 (life; £4,000 with CI);
  guaranteed premiums; joint life first event; entry 18–64; term 5–40 years; end by age 70
  [S2].
- FCA framing: FIB "pays out an ongoing monthly income … the benefit decreasing over time as
  the maximum possible payments reduce" [R8] — i.e. the present value of remaining instalments
  runs off like a decreasing term benefit.

### 9. Indexation / increasing cover options

- L&G Increasing Life Insurance (RPI-linked, annual option) [S1][S2]:
  - Cover increases with the 12-month change in RPI at each policy anniversary; if RPI change
    < 1%, no increase; increase capped at 10% p.a. [S1] (product profile states the same and
    "If changes to RPI are 1% or less … stay the same" [S2]).
  - Premium increases by RPI × **1.5**, capped at 15% p.a. [S1][S2].
  - Policyholder may decline; declining three years in a row removes the option for the rest
    of the term; joint policies need both holders to decline [S1].
  - Notice at least 3 months before the anniversary [S1].
- Aviva increasing cover [S6][S7]:
  - Level cover: choice of (a) RPI-linked (RPI measured over the 12 months ending 12 weeks
    before the start of the anniversary month; cover increase capped 10%; premium increase
    capped 15%; RPI ≤ 0% → no change), (b) fixed 3%, or (c) fixed 5% each anniversary.
  - Premium increase = cover increase % × **1.5** × current premium (so fixed 3%/5% produce
    premium increases of 4.5%/7.5%) [S6][S7].
  - Family income cover: fixed 3% or 5% only; premiums do **not** increase; increases continue
    during benefit payment [S6][S7].
  - Skip allowed; three consecutive skips remove the option permanently; ≥ 8 weeks' notice [S6].
- Royal London increasing cover [S8][S9]:
  - Fixed rate (adviser page: 2–5%) or RPI-linked (definition: RPI change over the 12 months
    ending three months before the anniversary, subject to a **minimum of 2% and maximum of
    10%**) [S8][S9].
  - Premium increases by the applied rate × **1.2** [S8] (lower loading than L&G/Aviva's 1.5).
  - Cancelling two consecutive increases ends the option [S8].
  - Increases stop if total cover with Royal London would exceed £3,000,000 [S8].

### 10. Guaranteed insurability / life-event options

- L&G "Increasing your cover" (in T&Cs; available if policy started before the life insured's
  55th birthday; shown on schedule) [S1]:
  - Events: marriage/civil partnership; divorce/dissolution; birth of a child; legal adoption;
    salary increase on promotion or new job; mortgage increase from house move or major home
    improvements. Exercise within 6 months of the event.
  - Limit per and across exercises: increase ≤ lower of 100% of original cover or **£200,000**
    (and ≤ salary-increase % × original cover, or ≤ mortgage increase, for those events).
  - Implemented as an additional policy ending at the 65th birthday or one year after the
    original expiry (whichever first); priced on then-current rates and terms.
  - Not available after age 55 (eldest life), during a WOP claim, or after a condition meeting
    the TI definition [S1]. FPIP variant: increase capped at the lower of 100% of original,
    **£1,400 per month**, rent increase, or % earnings increase [S2].
- Aviva "Life change benefit" (additional benefit, included automatically if accepted on
  standard terms, eldest life < 55 at start, policyholder = life covered) [S6]:
  - Events: marriage/civil partnership; divorce/dissolution; separation; becoming a parent;
    mortgage increase (move/purchase/home improvements); salary increase ≥ 20% from change of
    employer or promotion; rent increases (imposed by landlord, new property, or moving from
    renting to a mortgage).
  - Usable from 6 months after start; new policy within 180 days of the event; repeatable
    until the eldest life turns 55.
  - Limits — level/decreasing: total new cover across exercises ≤ lower of **£200,000** or the
    original cover amount; mortgage-driven increases capped at the mortgage increase; new
    policy must end before the eldest original life turns 70. Family income: total ≤ lower of
    original cover or the equivalent of **£8,000 a year**; rent-driven: monetary rent increase
    × remaining months, same caps [S6].
  - Implemented as a new policy on then-current rates/conditions; increasing cover, fracture
    cover, global treatment and the life change benefit itself cannot be added to the new
    policy; WOP only with a deferred period no shorter than the original [S6].
- Royal London "Cover Increase Options" (included if standard terms) [S8]:
  - Events: marriage/civil partnership or divorce/dissolution; having/adopting a child;
    mortgage increase; first mortgage; rent increase (move or landlord-imposed); salary
    increase. Exercise within 6 months; all lives covered must be under 55.
  - Limits — lump-sum cover: lowest of half the original cover or **£200,000** (rent-driven:
    also monthly rent increase × remaining months); income cover: lowest of half the original
    cover or **£10,000 a year** (rent-driven: monthly rent increase × 12). Limits apply across
    all covers/plans on the same life combined.
  - New cover priced at current rates for current age on the original underwriting terms; must
    run at least the then-minimum term and no longer than the original cover's remaining term.
  - Not available if not on standard terms, during (potential) claims, on plans themselves
    taken out via the option, or if not resident in UK/Jersey/Guernsey/IoM [S8].

### 11. Waiver of premium

- L&G (optional benefit, extra cost, chosen at outset; must be selected before the 64th
  birthday and accepted without extra premium/exclusion) [S1][S2]:
  - Premiums waived after **26 consecutive weeks** of incapacity, until the end of incapacity,
    payment of the cover, or policy expiry [S1].
  - Incapacity definition: totally incapable of carrying out **own occupation** due to illness
    or injury occurring after the policy start date (and not doing any other paid work); if
    not in paid employment, unable to do **3 or more of 6 Specified Work Tasks** (walking 200 m,
    climbing 12 stairs, lifting 2 kg for 60 s, bending/kneeling, getting in and out of a car,
    writing/typing) [S1].
  - Claim notification within 16 weeks of incapacity starting (else deemed to start 16 weeks
    before notification) [S1][S2].
  - Territorial condition: covered while residing/travelling in UK or EU; travel elsewhere
    limited to 3 consecutive months in any 12 [S1].
- Aviva waiver of premium (optional; underwritten; available until the (eldest) life covered
  turns 71) [S6]:
  - Definition: unable to perform the material and substantial duties of **own occupation**;
    if the life covered stopped work more than 12 months before the illness/injury, an
    **activities of daily work** test applies — unable to perform at least **2 of 6 work
    tasks** (same six tasks as L&G) [S6].
  - A **deferred period** applies (shown in the policy schedule; premiums must be paid until
    it ends); premiums then paid by Aviva until the earliest of: policy end date, recovery,
    starting any work, acceptance of a main-benefit claim, or the life covered turning 71 [S6].
  - Not claimable if living outside a listed country group (UK, EU, CI, IoM, Gibraltar,
    Andorra, Australia, Canada, Faroes, Liechtenstein, Monaco, NZ, Norway, San Marino,
    Switzerland, USA, Vatican) for more than 13 consecutive weeks in any 12 months [S6].
- Royal London Personal Menu Plan life cover: **no waiver-of-premium benefit exists in the
  plan details** (zero occurrences in the 84-page document) [S8] — a notable design variation
  (income protection is sold as a separate menu cover instead).

### 12. Joint life provisions

- Standard basis: joint life **first event** — policy pays once on the first death/TI of
  either life, then ends [S1][S2][S6].
- Royal London additionally offers **dual life** (independent covers on each life) and
  **joint life second event** (lump sum only) on the menu plan [S9].
- Separation options (divorce/dissolution/mortgage changes):
  - L&G "Joint life policy separation": within 6 months of the event; original policy
    cancelled and replaced by two single-life policies with the same cover, capped at the
    lower of £1,000,000 or the original amount; new policies end at the 70th birthday or one
    year after original expiry (whichever first); short health questionnaire, full application
    if any "yes" answers [S1].
  - Aviva "Separation benefit": joint policies accepted on standard terms, eldest < 55;
    split into single policies without further health questions; within 180 days; once only;
    not available if the policy is in trust [S6].
- Replacement cover after a first-event claim:
  - L&G: within 6 months of a valid life/TI claim the other life can take a new single policy
    with the same cover (decreasing: the remaining amount at claim), ending at the 70th
    birthday or one year after original expiry, on then-current rates/terms [S1].
- Payout destination on death (no trust): single own-life policy → estate; joint policy →
  surviving policyholder (simultaneous death → estate of the youngest); life-of-another →
  the policyholder; trust → trustees [S7]. Trusts are the promoted IHT route [S7].

### 13. Pre-policy and ancillary free covers

- L&G Free Life Cover (mortgage version): free cover between exchange of contracts and
  completion (Scotland: missives to entry), up to 90 days; pays the lowest of the cover
  applied for, the mortgage amount, or **£300,000**; requires standard-terms acceptance;
  person covered under 55 [S2].
- L&G Accidental Death Benefit: free cover while the application is underwritten; pays the
  cover applied for up to **£300,000** if accidental bodily injury is the sole cause of death
  within 90 days of the accident; lasts up to 90 days or until decision; first life only on
  joint applications; exclusion list includes suicide/self-injury, dangerous sports, non-fare
  flight, crime, war/riot, alcohol/drugs, pre-application accidents [S2].
- L&G Immediate Cover: cover assumed before underwriting completes for urgent cases, up to 60
  days, maximum £3,000,000 life cover (£1.5m with CI); not automatic [S2].
- Aviva House Purchase Cover (additional benefit, level/decreasing only): free death-only
  cover from exchange (Scotland: missives) until the earliest of 90 days, completion, or the
  policy start date; pays the lowest of **£500,000**, the purchase price, or the cover amount;
  suicide excluded [S6].

### 14. Alterations, grace period, cancellation, surrender

- Alterations: change amount of cover, change term, change premium frequency, remove a life,
  remove options [S1][S6]; Aviva: reductions/term-increase/frequency/option-removal amend the
  existing policy, cover increases are written as a new policy; term increases may be
  re-underwritten; changed premium must meet the minimum premium limit [S6]; Royal London
  allows menu-plan changes with analogous rules (splitting covers subject to then-minimum
  terms) [S8].
- Grace/lapse: premiums unpaid 60 days after due date → policy cancelled, cover ends, no
  refund (L&G) [S1]; Aviva: 60 days from each due date to pay; claims in the window have
  unpaid premiums deducted; cancellation after 60 days [S6].
- Cooling-off: 30 days with full premium refund (both) [S1][S6]; after that, cancellation
  gives no refund (L&G: proportionate refund of the unexpired part of an annual premium)
  [S1][S6].
- Surrender value: none. Aviva: "This isn't the kind of policy that you can 'cash in' — so
  you don't get any money if you cancel it" [S6]. FCA: pure protection products "have no
  savings or investment element"; if premiums stop, the policy lapses and no benefit is
  payable regardless of how long it was held [R8]. (L&G/RL documents likewise contain no
  surrender benefit.) No paid-up value exists for any of the three products [S1][S6][S8].
- Misstatement remedies (L&G): deliberate/reckless misrepresentation → cancel and refuse
  claims, premiums may not be refunded; careless → policy amended to the terms that would
  have applied; if higher premiums would have applied, cover is reduced by the formula
  New cover = premium actually charged × original cover ÷ higher premium [S1]. Aviva:
  wrong date of birth → benefit recalculated on the correct DOB; if the correct DOB was
  outside limits → policy cancelled [S6].

### 15. Regulatory, tax and market context

- Prudential regime: the PRA concluded the Solvency II Review with PS15/24 (15 Nov 2024);
  Solvency II assimilated law was replaced by PRA rules "in full from the end of 2024"; the
  reformed UK regime will eventually be known as "Solvency UK" [R5].
- Technical provisions for a BEL projection of term assurance: probability-weighted average
  of future cash-flows discounted on the relevant risk-free term structure, gross of
  reinsurance with separate recoverables [R1]; cash-flow list per rule 13.1 — benefits,
  expenses, premiums, intermediary payments, tax charged to settle obligations [R2];
  contract boundary — for guaranteed-premium term assurance the insurer has no unilateral
  repricing right, so the boundary is the full policy term; reviewable-premium business must
  be tested under rules 3.3/3.7 (premiums "fully reflect risks" only if benefits+expenses can
  never exceed premiums) [R3].
- Conduct regime: distribution of pure protection is governed by ICOBS (Insurance: Conduct of
  Business sourcebook), PROD 4 product governance, and the Consumer Duty; the customer's best
  interests rule is ICOBS 2.5.-1R [R9]. (Direct fetch of the ICOBS handbook text failed —
  see R7 note.)
- Classification: long-term insurance Class I (life and annuity) under RAO 2001 Sch. 1
  Part II [R6].
- Tax: L&G references the qualifying-policy conditions in para 19(3) of Schedule 15 to the
  Income and Corporation Taxes Act 1988 (options must be compatible with qualifying-policy
  status) [S1]. Aviva/RL summaries note death benefits paid to the estate may be subject to
  inheritance tax unless left to a spouse/civil partner or written in trust [S7]. Benefits
  are paid free of income/capital gains tax in normal circumstances [unverified — commonly
  stated but not confirmed from a fetched document in this session].
- FSCS: 100% of a valid claim, no upper limit, for insurance protection (L&G wording:
  "The FSCS will pay 100% of the value of the claim.") [S1].
- Market structure (2023, ABI data via FCA): 436,000 new term assurance policies and
  1,065,000 new accelerated CI policies issued; 3.119m term assurance and 7.164m accelerated
  CI policies in force; term assurance was the most purchased pure protection product; top 5
  insurers ≈ 80% of new business premiums (mortgage-related term assurance: 79–87% over
  2021–24; total mortgage-related term new premiums £176m–£204m/yr) [R8].
- Reinsurance: UK protection insurers cede mortality/morbidity risk heavily; reinsurers
  "have a degree of influence over manufacturing and distribution", provide data and
  expertise for pricing, and premiums are set to reflect the agreed risk shares [R8]. The
  frequently-cited figure that UK protection risk is 70–90%+ reinsured is [unverified]
  (the fetched FCA papers describe the mechanism and significance but do not give a
  quota-share percentage).
- Distribution economics: ~96% of intermediary commission is paid upfront; clawback periods
  of 2–4 years; insurers run Distribution Quality Management systems tracking lapses [R9].

### 16. Actuarial basis references (mortality, improvements, persistency)

- Industry experience tables (CMI):
  - Current: "16" Series term assurance tables, graduated on 2015–2018 experience of
    non-rated term assurance policies, covering mortality (including terminal illness) and
    accelerated critical illness; CMI WP150 (proposals) / WP154 (final, tables unchanged)
    [R10]. Public confirmation of exact names: **TMNL16 / TFNL16** (male/female non-smoker
    "lives" term assurance tables, 5-year select period) adopted in the IFoA Formulae and
    Tables 2025 edition [R12].
  - Access: CMI tables issued after 1 March 2013 (so the "16" and "08" Series) are available
    only to CMI-subscriber organisations; older series are public [R11].
  - Public older series (downloadable): "00" Series assured-lives tables on 1999–2002
    experience — permanent assurances AMC00/AMS00/AMN00, AFC00/AFS00/AFN00; temporary
    assurances TMC00/TMS00/TMN00, TFC00/TFS00/TFN00 [R13][R11]. (For an open reference
    implementation these, or the AM92/AF92-era tables, are the practical choices; AM92 was
    the assured-lives table in the previous IFoA Formulae and Tables edition [R12].)
  - Series history for context: "08" Series (2007–2010), "00" Series (1999–2002), "92" Series
    (1991–1994), "80" Series (1979–1982) [R11].
- Mortality improvements: CMI Mortality Projections Model, latest fetched-confirmed version
  CMI_2024 (published 30 June 2025 with WP201; calibrated to England & Wales population data;
  users choose the long-term rate; new "fitted overlay" for pandemic-period data) [R14].
  CMI_2025/WP211 (March 2026) exists per search results but was not fetched [unverified].
- Persistency/lapse: FCA interim report data (2024 policies in force, pure protection):
  average lapse rate 5% p.a.; highest observed 23% in policy year 1 for non-advised
  intermediated sales with 4-year clawback; modest lapse spikes just after 2-year and 4-year
  commission clawback periods end [R9]. Insurers use lapse experience in pricing and
  reviewable-premium reviews [R8][S8]. Historical FSA/ABI persistency reports: the FCA
  Handbook still contains SUP 16.8 "Persistency reports from insurers" (persistency rate
  formula CF×100/CC) per search results, but the handbook page itself was not fetched
  [unverified beyond the search snippet].
- Professional standards: technical actuarial work on UK term assurance (pricing, reserving,
  TPs) falls under FRC TAS 100 v2.0 (effective 1 July 2023, all technical actuarial work,
  mandatory for IFoA members) [R15] and TAS 200: Insurance v2.0 (effective 1 January 2025)
  [R16]. IFoA Actuarial Profession Standards (APS) also apply [unverified — not fetched].
- Gender-neutral pricing: UK retail insurance pricing must be gender-neutral following the
  EU Gender Directive/Test-Achats line as retained in UK law [unverified — widely known but
  not confirmed from a fetched document in this session; none of the three insurers' documents
  list sex as a rating factor, and Aviva/L&G list age, smoker status, health, lifestyle,
  occupation [S6][S7]].

Most representative design for a reference implementation: a guaranteed-premium, single or
joint-life-first-death term assurance with (i) level, (ii) decreasing (amortisation-schedule
sum assured at a fixed annual interest rate in the 5–8% region — L&G's 5/7/8/10% menu and
RL's 6% default bracket this), and (iii) family income (level monthly instalments payable from
death to expiry) payout shapes; terminal illness acceleration at no extra cost; 12-month
suicide exclusion only; optional RPI-linked indexation with a 10% benefit cap and a premium
loading multiplier (1.5 is the mode across L&G/Aviva); optional guaranteed insurability capped
at ~£200,000; optional waiver of premium with a 26-week deferred period; monthly premiums with
a ~£5/month minimum, 60-day grace, no surrender or paid-up values.

## Gaps and caveats

- **Premium rates**: no insurer publishes rate tables; retail premiums are quote-engine
  outputs. Only the £5/month minimum premium (L&G) is public [S5]. Any per-mille rate
  structure for the reference model must be constructed from CMI tables + loadings and marked
  [std]/[unverified].
- **Aviva decreasing interest rate value**: the conditions and summary confirm a fixed rate
  set at application and shown in the schedule [S6][S7], but the selectable value/range is not
  published in the fetched documents.
- **Aviva minimum premium**: referenced as "the minimum premium limit that applies at the
  time" without a value [S6].
- **CMI "16" Series table values**: post-2013 CMI tables (including the full 16-Series
  term-assurance set and its smoker/duration variants) are subscriber-only [R11]; only the
  names/basis are publicly confirmable ([R10][R12]). Older public tables ("00" Series
  TMC00/TMN00/TMS00 etc. [R13], and the AM92 generation [R12]) are the practical open-data
  substitutes.
- **ICOBS text**: the FCA Handbook could not be machine-fetched (JS-rendered); ICOBS's role
  and the ICOBS 2.5.-1R best-interests rule are sourced indirectly via the FCA market study
  [R9]; direct handbook citations remain unverified (R7 fetched_ok=false).
- **Renewable/convertible term**: FCA confirms renewable term assurance is "offered" in the
  UK market [R8], but none of the three insurers' fetched retail documents contain a renewal
  or conversion option (L&G's conversion option is explicitly "no longer offered" [S2]).
  UK retail term assurance is effectively non-renewable, non-convertible; treat any
  renewal/conversion feature as an optional extension, not core.
- **Reinsurance share of UK protection risk**: mechanism and significance verified [R8];
  quantitative cession rates not published in fetched documents — [unverified].
- **Persistency data granularity**: FCA MS24/1.4 gives 2024 point-in-time lapse rates and
  clawback-related spikes [R9]; a full lapse curve by duration for term assurance is not
  public in the fetched sources; historical FSA persistency reports were not fetched.
- **CMI_2025**: existence (March 2026, WP211) known only from search results — [unverified].
- **Vitality, Zurich, Scottish Widows**: not fetched this session; the three-insurer set
  (L&G, Aviva, Royal London) covers all three payout shapes with policy-conditions + summary
  pairs, which was judged sufficient. Adding a fourth insurer would mainly refine the
  variations table.
- **Tax treatment detail** (qualifying policy rules, IHT/trust mechanics beyond the insurer
  summaries): only the fragments cited in §15 are verified; the rest is [unverified].
